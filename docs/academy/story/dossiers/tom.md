## 1. Who they really are

Tom is the quiet engine that keeps a loud group from falling apart. In the real class he is the one who books the restaurant, sorts the tickets, and — because he owns a car and drives in from out of town — offers to pick everyone up so they can drink. He is generous in the specific, load-bearing way that doesn't announce itself: he compiled a Minna-no-Nihongo vocab CSV and shared it with the whole class ("You're an actual STAR," someone said), and he stayed loyal to the slow class and the beloved sensei rather than jumping to the faster on-campus level. The evidence describes him as *the quiet, systematic realist of the trio — the one who builds the plan that actually holds the group together.* That is the spine of the character.

His warmth runs through hobbies that are all a little wholesome and a little methodical. He's an RHS (Royal Horticultural Society) member who organises garden day-trips to Wisley and can smuggle a guest in free; he bakes (matcha brownies, salmon miso soup from Japan Centre recipes); he has a beloved dog, **Chestnut** (チェスナットさん), who is canonically "the best dog" and gets worked into every conversation via photos and stickers. He is a relentless researcher and deal-hunter who tests every new AI study tool and reports back with screenshots and verdicts, and he's genuinely good at explaining things — the class keeps telling him to start a YouTube channel ("and probably get sued by Google 😂"). His humour is dry, occasionally dark, and cross-lingual: one-word replies ("Oui", "はい🫡", "Señor 😳", a "Dankeschön"), and once he made an AI generate a song calling everyone in a group chat a bitch ("Duo was onto me").

The canon voice anchor — *playful challenge language, fast escalation, the "final boss" showman who matures into designing generous rematches* — maps almost exactly onto the real Tom's methodical-competitor streak: the jconj verb-conjugation streak of 74 ("determined to get to 100"), the day he announced *"I just finished memorising all the N4 level kanji 🥳,"* the Pokémon-superfan habit of turning anything countable into a game and teaching new words by finding the creature whose name contains them. The real detail *deepens* the anchor rather than contradicting it: his "final boss" bravado is a performance layered over a genuinely diligent, generous person, and the four-season job is to let the showmanship soften into mentorship. One reconciliation note for authors: the anchor's "games / dramatic battle framing" is fed by a franchise the source wall forbids naming (see §6). In-Academy, Tom's game love is rendered as **generic creature-collecting / photo-vocabulary games and counting-as-play**, never borrowed IP.

Benign real texture available to authors: birthday **18 December**; a Pikachu tattoo he's proud of (*"とても痛かった、でも大好きです"*) — usable only as *"a small tattoo he loves,"* not as franchise IP; works in trade/policy (checks free-trade agreements and government policy, uses AI tools at work) and at Lidl; watches *The Apothecary Diaries* weekly and *Hot Spot* on Netflix; a city-pop and jpop-goldies listener; rock-climbs at EustonWall after class; knows some Italian and German phrases; regularly "somehow in a different city that weekend" (up north, Swansea, Bristol-area). Represent every bit of this warmly.

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — most important)

**Learning speciality:** Tom is the class's **methodical kana-and-kanji grinder and tool-tester.** His pedagogical signature is *verification*: memorable input plus a check step, reading-gated word-marking, and turning play (counting, game names, challenges) into drill. Every scene he leads or supports should make the learner *read or repair by shape/reading rather than translate first* — his single most transferable habit.

**Beat 5 · Chapter 5 — LEAD. Reservoir `l1-l07`; transfer: "read/select an item without translation-first prompting."**
- **Language functions the scene makes the learner NEED:** decode **katakana** at speed; use **counters** to talk about the game's pieces (〜まい for cards, 〜つ/〜こ for tokens, 〜り/〜にん for players); issue and follow light **commands/imperatives** to run play (〜てください、ひいてください、みてください); and *select the right card by reading it*, not by an English gloss.
- **Activity types:** reading (katakana recognition), vocabulary (counters), listening + speaking (game commands as a live loop).
- **Why the real texture makes the Japanese necessary:** Tom really does own a Japanese photo-vocab game and really does count the whole table for fun. A game you can't win unless you *read the katakana on the smallest card* is the most honest possible reason to stop translating and start reading — and the paper-cat clue rewards attention over spectacle, exactly the skill katakana demands.

**Beat 15 · Chapter 15 — SUPPORT. Reservoir `l1-l22`–`l1-l26`; transfer: "complete the katakana sequence and repair a sound/shape error in context."**
- **Language functions:** complete a **katakana sequence**; **repair a sound/shape error** (シ vs ツ, ン vs ソ, ー long-vowel placement, small ャュョ); offer **casual reactions** (あ、それだ / おしい！/ いいね) that correct without mocking.
- **Activity types:** writing (kana shape production), listening (hearing the mis-sounded mora), light speaking.
- **Why the real texture works:** Tom's neat-hiragana-from-a-YouTube-video discipline and shared kana resources make him the person who fixes the chorus by *shape and sound*, generously — modelling that repair is a gift, not a verdict.

**Beat 18 · Chapter 18 — SUPPORT. Reservoir `l2-l09`–`l2-l11`; transfer: "describe a wanted program, button condition, and changed plan."**
- **Language functions:** state what you **want** (〜がほしい／〜たい — "the version I want is…"); express a **condition** (〜と / 〜たら — "if you press this, then…"); state a **changed plan** (〜ことにする／やっぱり…). Layered onto the chapter's real job: **kanji as evidence** — read the character to confirm the word before marking it known.
- **Activity types:** kanji (reading-to-verify), grammar (want / conditional / plan-change), transfer (revise a false mnemonic).
- **Why the real texture works:** Tom's actual method — *"memorable images work, add a verification step,"* the カエル→換える card, marking words known only when he can read the kanji — is literally a *button-condition + verification* routine. The Japanese for wanting-a-version, setting-a-condition, and changing-a-plan is the exact language his real study workflow already runs on.

**Beat 32 · Chapter 32 — SUPPORT/Return. Reservoir: N2 quotation/evidentiality/hedging (contract rows 31–33); transfer: contained procedural repair without exposing withdrawn material.**
- **Language functions:** **immediate repair and apology** (失礼しました／すぐ直します); **procedural Japanese** for "this is the wrong draft, use that one" (こちらではなく、そちらを…); **evidential hedging** to flag an unverified source (〜みたい／〜らしい／〜そう) and **quotation** (〜と書いてあった／〜と言っていた) to attribute a claim to a document rather than a person.
- **Activity types:** speaking (spontaneous repair), listening (catch the wrong-version cue), transfer (procedure under pressure).
- **Why the real texture works:** Tom checks free-trade agreements and government policy for a living and tests tools by verdict — he is the class's natural *which-document-is-authoritative* voice. Version discipline in Japanese is his day-job instinct made pedagogical.

**Beat 44 · Chapter 44 — Future. Reservoir: N1 editing/adaptation rationale (contract rows 43–45); transfer: make the public version vivid without restoring withdrawn detail.**
- **Language functions:** **editing/adaptation rationale** (この方が伝わる／あえて省く — "we leave this out on purpose"); **concession + keigo** to justify a constraint gracefully; argue that **constraint creates the final form** without flattening anyone's voice.
- **Activity types:** writing/editing, speaking (extended justification), transfer (hand a method to someone else in Japanese).
- **Why the real texture works:** the baker-gardener who knows a bed and a bake are shaped by what you *withhold*, and the generous rematch-designer who hands over the marker, is the right voice to teach that omission is authored meaning — and to do it as mentorship, in Japanese.
