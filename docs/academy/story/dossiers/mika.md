## 1. Who he really is

Mika is the class's **quiet polyglot** — a person who has studied many foreign languages and hears Japanese the way a tuner hears a string: as pitch, mora, and the length of a pause. He is *a little shy*, and the shyness has a very specific shape. He is **shy about speaking first, and unstoppable once he starts**. His silence at the top of a turn is not slowness and not fear of the material; it is a habit of *listening all the way to the end before he adds anything*. The canon voice anchor names this exactly: he "does not fill silence quickly," he "attends to timing and silence" where Xingyu attends to rhythm and memorability, and he "leaves space." The real detail deepens the anchor rather than replacing it — the space he leaves is a polyglot's professional patience, not a deficit.

His signature real line is **「ええと…もう一回、いいですか」** ("um... one more time, is that okay?") — the moment of being terrified to speak first, immediately followed by the fluency that arrives once he has committed. And his running quirk is that he will **drop an unexpected language fact and then retreat** — a small, precise observation ("in Korean that vowel is longer," "that pitch drop is like French") offered sideways, then a step back into quiet before anyone can put him on the spot.

Crucially, Mika **openly names his own difficulty**: *he is not confident reading kanji.* This is the most useful thing about him as a learning character. His ear is his strength and his eye for kanji is his stated weak spot — and instead of hiding that, he says it out loud and asks for the one thing he needs. That confession is not a flaw to be cured; it is his method. He models the exact N4 repair strategy the game teaches (`ext-mika-repair-strategy`, N4): **"Ask for repetition, then identify one word you want explained."** His whole character is the emotional proof that naming the unclear part is competence, not weakness.

His warmth shows in **kind, careful communication** and in **language-exchange / penpal** energy (HelloTalk-style): he is the person who makes it safe to be a beginner out loud, who wants to be *brave enough to type first and to ask "once more" without feeling silly*. His growth, per the pressure row, is to **stop yielding the floor when his timing observation actually matters** — to claim the quiet role as expertise. He takes gentle study advice from **Sophie** (precision/confidence) and **Rie** (the room), and his ear naturally pairs and contrasts with **Xingyu** (sound-as-memory) and, at his limit beat, **Francis** (what did we actually hear?).

**Voice fingerprint (keep true):** short turns; a beat of silence before he answers; one concrete observation about *sound or interval*, never a personality verdict; the sideways language fact; the retreat. Canon diagnostic line to hold as his register: **音ももう一度聞こう。間が少し長い。** ("Let's listen to the sound once more too. The pause is a little long.")

## 2. Four-season arc across his five beats

His deeper arc (from the bible + matrix): **treats patience as expertise, and later protects quiet event roles.** He starts as the shy one who yields the floor and ends as the person who *guards* quiet participation as a full, chosen role for everyone. He owns `callback:quiet-count` end to end, so his five beats are also the four stages of that callback plus its contribution echo.

### Beat 1 — Ch 4 · `s1e04-welcome-frequency` (LEAD) · *seed of quiet-count*
The atlas work stalls on a listening path: a route point that only opens if you catch a soft audio cue at the right moment, and the class keeps missing it by being **loud instead of on time.** Mika — the last person anyone expects to lead — finds that **rhythm, not volume, opens the listening path.** He uses a **pause and a count** to locate the entrance ("wait... 1, 2 — *there*"), and asks for the phrase 「もう一回、いいですか」 not once but as a *technique*: repeat, count the interval, enter on the beat. His shyness is on full display (he almost doesn't say it; he drops the timing fact and starts to retreat) — and the scene's job is to make the class realise his quiet attention is an **ensemble skill**, not a personality footnote. **Seed:** the pause-and-count as a way to locate a listening entrance. **End state contribution to the season:** quiet attention is now a named, shared tool.

### Beat 2 — Ch 15 · `s1e15-chorus-with-a-hole` (contribution) · *echo of quiet-count*
Xingyu shares a **memorable but flawed study chorus** for repair. Mika's count from Ch 4 is what makes the chorus *locatable and singable* — the group can find the hole because they know where the beat is — **but the count cannot prove the words.** This is the honest limit of his gift: timing tells you *where* the error is, not *what* it should be. Mika contributes the rhythmic scaffold; the class still needs Xingyu's ear-memory and someone's kanji/reading to fill the gap (a quiet nod to his own kanji-reading weak spot — he can place the missing syllable but wants the reading confirmed). Warmth over ridicule: the flaw is offered, located, and repaired without anyone being embarrassed.

### Beat 3 — Ch 25 · `s3e01-after-the-applause` (LIMIT) · *with Francis*
Exhibition feedback arrives as **one polite phrase that could mean praise, doubt, or refusal.** Mika and **Francis compare what they each heard.** This is his *limit* beat: the ear that is unbeatable on timing and pitch discovers that **tone and implication are not the same as sound** — you can hear a phrase perfectly and still not know what it meant. His pressure pattern bites here: he starts to **yield the floor** ("maybe I misheard...") even though his timing read of the *pause before* the phrase is real evidence. Growth is to hold his observation as expertise: the length of the hesitation before someone says 「けっこうです」 is data. He and Francis don't resolve it into one truth; they name three readings. This is where the fact-drop-then-retreat gag first turns from cute to costly — retreating loses information the class needs.

### Beat 4 — Ch 34 · `s3e10-empty-microphone` (RETURN) · *transform of quiet-count*
A host **drops out** and the group has to **redistribute public speaking without coercion.** Here Mika's arc lands its turn: the quiet-count **transforms into a backstage cue for a host who does not want the microphone.** Instead of forcing a shy person forward (or being forced forward himself), Mika builds a *structure that lets a quiet role be a full role* — he cues entrances from offstage with the same pause-and-count, so someone can contribute publicly without being exposed. This is him **protecting quiet event roles**, the exact "later" half of his arc. He no longer just leaves space; he *engineers* it for others. Role negotiation and spontaneous repair; quiet participation counts as full participation.

### Beat 5 — Ch 46 · `s4e10-public-evening` (FUTURE) · *payoff of quiet-count*
At the public Japanese evening, **one live misunderstanding forces a transparent repair** in front of an audience. The **same quiet cue** Mika seeded in Ch 4 now **creates the space for that repair** — a shared, understood pause that lets the group stop, name the confusion, ask 「もう一回、いいですか」 on a real stage, and fix it *visibly and kindly* rather than papering over it. Integrated N1: live listening, response, source explanation, audience negotiation. His future is not "cured of shyness" — it is a room that has learned to use his pause on purpose. The terrified-to-speak-first boy is now the reason a whole audience can breathe through a mistake.

## 3. The Japanese his story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION *(the point)*

**Mika's learning speciality:** *the listening-and-repair loop* — mora/pitch timing, clarification requests, and the low-pressure confidence to name exactly what you didn't catch. He is the game's home for **「もう一回、いいですか」 → identify one unclear word → ask for it → re-enter.** He is also the diegetic reason **pronunciation and multilingual comparison** exist as a study lens (compare a Japanese sound to a sound you already own), and — via his own confession — the model for **naming a weakness (kanji reading) as a strategy, not a shame.**

Reservoir note: his lead chapter draws the possession/request reservoir `l1-l05`–`l1-l06` (transfer: *identify whose item it is and request the right one*). Mika's scene **layers listening + clarification on top of that possessive/request grammar** — you can't request the *right* item until you've correctly *heard whose it is and which one*, which is precisely his mora-timing wheelhouse. That is the elegant fit: the l05/06 content supplies the words; Mika's scene supplies the *reason you must hear them exactly.*
