# Chapter 1 - 「はじめまして」 One Notice, Three Routes

**Core question:** A card says "come to the desk." Who is at the desk, and what is the desk for?
**Story change:** The first Blue Door card gains a usable first sentence.
**Language family:** `は/です`, `これ/それ/あれ`, `の`, `何ですか`, `だれですか`; self-introductions and simple notice reading.
**Runtime status:** planned N5 sequence. All `planned:` links are specified in [LESSON-CONTENT-ch1-3.md](./LESSON-CONTENT-ch1-3.md) and are not yet publishable runtime activities.

## S1.1 - A Notice Needs a Noun

**Location:** Card Rack, L03.
**Narrative job:** Make naming a practical act rather than an icebreaker ritual.
**Authentic language need:** Identify the subject of a welcome instruction.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-notice-subject` - `select-one`, identify the thing a card refers to.
**Intent ID:** `notice.name-referent`

_The Card Rack holds three handwritten cards. One says `ここに 来て ください` - Please come here. Suzu has drawn a small pencil line under `ここ`._

**SUZU:** `「ここ」だけでは、足りません。ここは、どこですか。`
_“Here” alone is not enough. Where is here?_

**RIE:** `いい質問です。まず、カードが 指しているものに 名前を つけましょう。`
_Good question. First, let us name what the card is pointing to._

**Teaching card - practical frame:**

| What the reader needs | N5 form                 | Meaning                 |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| name the place        | `ここは デスクです。`   | This is the Desk.       |
| name the person       | `りえさんは 先生です。` | Rie is the facilitator. |
| ask what it is        | `これは 何ですか。`     | What is this?           |
| ask who it is         | `あの人は だれですか。` | Who is that person?     |

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                    | Bridge                                 | N4                                                             |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ここは デスクです。` | `ここは、オープン・ドア・デスクです。` | `ここが、初めて来た人に案内をするオープン・ドア・デスクです。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _Suzu found the first problem: “here” is not useful until a reader knows what “here” names._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-notice-subject`.

## S1.2 - The Shape That Holds a Name

**Location:** Open Door Desk classroom.
**Narrative job:** Give the group a short, dignified way to introduce itself and a place.
**Authentic language need:** Make the Desk card say who can help.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-introduce-self` - ordering and short-text response using `は/です`.
**Intent ID:** `introduce.name-role`

_Rie writes a single frame on the board. The green marker works long enough to underline `は`, then retires without warning._

**RIE:** `名前と 役目が 分かると、聞く相手が 分かります。`
_When the name and role are clear, the reader knows whom to ask._

**RIE:** `「A は B です」。短いですが、入口になります。`
_“A wa B desu.” It is short, but it can be an entrance._

**Explanation - use, then rule:**

> `わたしは りえです。先生です。`
> _I am Rie. I am the facilitator._
>
> `すずさんは カードの 人です。`
> _Suzu is the person for the cards._
>
> `は` sets the topic; `です` gives the reader a settled, polite identification. The useful outcome is not "know the particle." It is "tell a new arrival who can help."

**Choice - `practice-order`:**

- `自分の 名前から` - build a self-introduction first.
- `デスクの 名前から` - build the notice sentence first.

Both paths use the same four practice items and join at S1.3.

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                    | Bridge                                   | N4                                                        |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| `わたしは すずです。` | `わたしは すずです。カードを 直します。` | `わたしは、案内カードを分かりやすく直している すずです。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _`は/です` lets a card name the person who can help. Start with the shortest useful version._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-introduce-self`.

## S1.3 - This, That, and the Actual Desk

**Location:** Noticeboard and desk.
**Narrative job:** Turn demonstratives into a reader-care problem.
**Authentic language need:** Distinguish the card, the desk, and a visible help object.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-point-and-name` - `select-one` and matching response for `これ/それ/あれ` and `の`.
**Intent ID:** `notice.point-with-context`

_Rie sets out a blue folder by the Desk, rolls a pencil toward Suzu, and points to a coat hook across the room. Leo, who has arrived early to look at the route map, immediately labels the coat hook "not relevant."_

**LEO:** `あれは、カードでは ありません。`
_That over there is not a card._

**SUZU:** `でも、カードに「あれ」と 書いたら、読んだ人は 困ります。`
_But if the card says “that,” the reader will be stuck._

**RIE:** `そうですね。話している二人には「あれ」で足ります。カードには、名前が要ります。`
_Exactly. “That” may be enough for two people talking. A card needs a name._

**Teaching card:**

| Context                                    | Japanese                             | Why it works                                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rie holds the folder.                      | `これは 青い フォリオです。`         | `これ` is near the speaker.                             |
| Suzu has the pencil.                       | `それは すずさんの えんぴつですか。` | `それ` is near the listener; `の` shows possession.     |
| The folder is on the desk across the room. | `あれは デスクの フォリオです。`     | `あれ` points far away; the noun keeps it useful later. |

**Practice beat:** Rewrite `あれを 見て ください` for a card. Best answer: `青い フォリオを 見て ください。`

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                                | Bridge                                      | N4                                             |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `これは、デスクの フォリオです。` | `これは、デスクに ある 青い フォリオです。` | `これは、デスクに置いてある青いフォリオです。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _Pointing words help in conversation; a notice also needs the noun that lets a new reader act._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-point-and-name`.

## S1.4 - The First Repair

**Location:** Card Rack.
**Narrative job:** Give Suzu a first, visible success without making her perfectionism a joke.
**Authentic language need:** Replace a vague instruction with a name, role, and action.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-card-name-repair` - short-text response and self-check.
**Intent ID:** `notice.rewrite-help-instruction`

_The original card says `ここで 聞いて ください`. Suzu places a blank strip below it. She pauses before writing, as if every possible version is waiting at once._

**SUZU:** `「ここ」は、直せます。「何を聞くか」も 書きたいです。`
_I can repair “here.” I would like to write what people can ask as well._

**RIE:** `一度に全部は書かなくていいですよ。次の行動を一つ、先に。`
_You do not need to write everything at once. Start with one next action._

**Learner task:** Draft one of these layers, then use the model after trying:

1. `ここは オープン・ドア・デスクです。`
2. `りえさんは ここに います。`
3. **Read-only survival phrase:** `わからない ときは、りえさんに 聞いて ください。`

**Model answer - later support, not first view:**

> `ここは オープン・ドア・デスクです。りえさんは ここに います。わからない ときは、りえさんに 聞いて ください。`
>
> _This is the Open Door Desk. Rie is here. If you are unsure, please ask Rie._

**Self-review:** Can a reader identify the place, the person, and one action?
**Recap if skipped:** _The first card now names the Desk, Rie, and a useful next action._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-card-name-repair`.

## S1.5 - Optional Study Connection: The Missing Noun

**Location:** Quiet Table, L05.
**Narrative job:** Let Suzu practise stopping before she over-solves a card.
**Authentic language need:** Ask one clarification question and select a concrete noun.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-card-name-repair`; no additional mastery event.
**Intent ID:** `suzu.choose-one-useful-detail`

_Suzu has three versions of a card that all say almost the same thing. Leo studies them with the solemnity of a route inspector._

**LEO:** `このカードは、四つの 入口が あります。`
_This card has four entrances._

**SUZU:** `それは、よくない ですか。`
_Is that not good?_

**LEO:** `読む人が 一人なら、一つで いいと 思います。`
_If there is one reader, I think one is enough._

**Learner choice - cosmetic:** Lead the card with `デスク` or `りえさん`. The other line follows; the content re-joins.
**Connection recap:** _Suzu kept one detail first and left the rest for a later card. That is not less clear; it is easier to use._
**Optional payoff:** Bag note, **First Useful Noun**.

## S1.6 - The Blue Door Gets Its First Line

**Location:** Open Door Desk, chapter close.
**Narrative job:** Pay off the chapter's learning with a small, public artefact.
**Authentic language need:** Read the repaired card and recognise the language just used.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-selfintro-note` - short self-introduction or notice sentence; self-review.
**Intent ID:** `folio.record-first-repair`

_Suzu clips the finished card to the folio. The blue half-door is still only half a door, but it is beside a sentence that can now do its job._

> `ここは オープン・ドア・デスクです。りえさんは ここに います。わからない ときは、りえさんに 聞いて ください。`

**RIE:** `短いですね。でも、来た人は 次に何をするか 分かります。`
_It is short. But a person arriving knows what to do next._

**SUZU:** `次のカードは、場所ですね。`
_The next card is about place, then._

**RIE:** `そうです。カードが「ここ」と言うなら、今度は本当に連れていきましょう。`
_Exactly. If a card says “here,” next time we should actually get someone there._

**Chapter recap:**

- `は/です` names a person, role, or place.
- `これ/それ/あれ` works when speaker and listener share a view; a notice needs the actual noun too.
- `の` helps a reader connect an object to its owner or place.
- A useful card names the place, the helper, and one action.

**Handoff:** Campus; Chapter 2 route task; **Continue learning** opens the next unfinished core activity.

## Chapter 1 author notes

- No character is required to share a job, hobby, history, or personal reason for studying. The self-introduction task offers fictional/neutral examples and accepts an unnamed learner response.
- `わからない ときは、りえさんに 聞いて ください` is a recognition-only survival phrase in this chapter. The Ch. 1 activity assesses naming the place/helper, not production or analysis of `ときは/てください`; a later request unit owns that grammar.
- Suzu's flaw is scope control, not "being too clever." The scene lets her learn to prioritise without making her the joke.
- The chapter's visual reward is the repaired card and an optional recap note, never an academic unlock.
- Caption, transcript, furigana, and gloss are authored from the same line source. Audio and motion add atmosphere only.
