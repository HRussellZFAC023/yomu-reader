## 1. Who they really are

Felix is the person in the room who is genuinely, visibly delighted — and whose delight happens to be *aimed*. Where another student's excitement sprays across the whole table, Felix's lands on one small thing and holds there: the card that doesn't sit flush with its neighbours, the marker that has been nudged a centimetre, the animal in the corner of the frame everyone else read past. His recorded trait is "observant delight focused on cats," and the load-bearing word is not *cats* — it is *focused*. Cats are simply the most honest example of how his attention works: he notices the thing that moved, the thing that is out of place, the thing that is quietly alive at the edge of a scene, and he lights up about it out loud.

That makes him the class's least anxious noticer. He is not correcting anyone (that's Sophie), not decomposing form (that's Shin), not framing a challenge (that's Tom). He points, he grins, he counts — *"二匹いる"* — and because the pointing came wrapped in delight rather than a gotcha, the room follows his finger without getting defensive. His social tactic is **pass, don't hold**: he hands the interesting thing to the next person as a question rather than keeping it as his own trophy. His recorded habit set — "animal counters, observation, and question-passing" — is one continuous gesture: *see it → count it → ask someone else about it.*

His running joke is the **paper-cat marker**: a tiny folded paper cat that starts life (in Tom's oversized Chapter 5 game) as a throwaway mascot marking the least dramatic clue on the board. Felix is the one who takes that joke seriously — not solemnly, but *attentively*. His whole arc is the recorded line **"follows a playful marker into real evidence and learns that joy can be method."** He begins believing joy and rigour are opposites (you either have fun or you do the careful work); he ends knowing that his delight *is* an instrument — that the thing he noticed because it was cute turns out to be the thing that was actually true.

**Reconciling with the voice anchor.** The anchor gives four fields to protect: attention lands on the out-of-place living detail; sentence movement runs *observation → count → handed-off question*; social tactic is playful redirection of attention (he moves the room's eyes without moving himself to the centre); contradiction is that his lightness is doing serious work he doesn't yet claim credit for. Nothing in this dossier adds biography on top of that — it only gives the four fields concrete scenes to live in.

**Production note (meta, not diegetic).** Felix's approved-quality sprites are the style anchor the rest of the cast's art is measured against. That is a pipeline fact about asset quality, not a story fact — it never surfaces in dialogue, and it grants no likeness clearance (his runtime portrait stays blocked; presentation is `name-only`).

## 2. Four-season arc across the five beats

His deeper arc — *joy can be method* — is a slow promotion of his own delight from "the fun part" to "the useful part," and it rides the four stages of the `paper-cat` callback. Beat by beat:

**Beat 1 — Arrival / lead · Ch 17 `s1e17-catwalk-clue`.** The paper cat that Tom left as a mascot (Ch 5 seed) has been *moved*, and the index cards around it are mislabeled. Everyone else is arguing about the big, dramatic parts of the board; Felix is the only one still looking at the small marker, because it's the cat and the cat is where his eyes go. He tracks it — *"さっき、ここにいた猫、今そこにある"* — and by counting the cards (*何枚*) and naming which card is *the one someone re-labelled* with a relative clause, he turns a cute distraction into the scene's actual lead. This is the callback **echo**: the marker moves from mascot to trail. His delight *is* the method here, but he doesn't know that yet — he thinks he just got lucky noticing the cat.

**Beat 2 — Contribution · Ch 21 `s1e21-questions-in-the-dark` (Peter's lead).** Peter rebuilds a district by getting one strong question out of each learner in the dark. Felix's bounded competence — **question-passing** — is exactly what this scene needs. He doesn't answer; he hands his observation to the next person *as* a question so their answer can carry the map forward. Where in Ch 17 the pass was reflexive and playful, here it's deliberate: he learns his instinct to give the interesting thing away is a leadership move, not just a habit. His delight starts to look like technique.

**Beat 3 — Limit · Ch 29 `s3e05-chair-not-reserved` (Jenny's lead).** Jenny declines a visible event role, and the group's first instinct is to treat the empty chair as a problem to *solve* — to notice the gap and fill it, brightly, helpfully. This is Felix's limit chapter because "notice the out-of-place thing and cheerfully point at it" is precisely the wrong tool here: a person who has stepped back is not a mislabeled card. His gift — aimed delight, pointing at what's different — can become intrusive. He has to learn that some gaps are *authored*, not errors, and that the kind thing is to leave the marker where she put it. Joy learns restraint; his method meets the one place it must not operate.

**Beat 4 — Return · Ch 35 `s3e11-names-in-the-margin`.** The old page's provenance is under investigation, and it turns out the paper, the ink, and the *placement* of markers across successive classes are the evidence. Felix returns because he is the class's living memory of *where the marker has been* — he watched it move in Ch 17, so he can testify to placement over time. This is the callback **transform**: paper, ink and position make the paper cat *version evidence rather than a mascot*. His delight is now fully method — the thing he loved because it was charming is the thing that dates the page. He describes physical evidence in careful N2 (nominalized, source-qualified) without over-claiming what it proves.

**Beat 5 — Future · Ch 48 `s4e12-next-page`.** At graduation the learner leaves one bounded contribution and a blank page under the class's terms. Felix's payoff is quiet: he doesn't need to be at the centre. His whole arc argued that noticing-and-passing is a complete way to contribute, so his future beat is him doing exactly that one more time — spotting the small living detail in the graduation scene, counting it, and handing it on as a question rather than a conclusion. (The `paper-cat` callback's formal payoff is Stasi's at Ch 44 — she uses a *new neutral marker* to hold a withheld passage's place without exposing it. Felix seeds and carries that idea; Stasi lands it. That hand-off is itself in character: Felix passes the interesting thing to the next person.)

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

**Felix's learning speciality:** *identifying the specific thing among similar things, and counting it.* Concretely — **relative (noun-modifying) clauses**, **counters** (animal 匹, flat/paper 枚, and general 個/つ), and **observation-to-question transfer** (turning "I notice X" into "which one is the X that…?" for someone else to answer). His scenes make the learner *need* the grammar of specification: when several cards look alike and one has been tampered with, you cannot proceed with vocabulary alone — you must build a modifier clause to say *which* one.

Because his attention naturally lands on the out-of-place living detail, every Felix scene supplies an authentic reason to distinguish and enumerate rather than to merely name. That is the pedagogical engine.

### Beat-by-beat learning table

| Beat / Ch | Reservoir | Language function the scene makes the learner NEED | Activity type | Why the real texture makes the Japanese necessary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ch 17 (lead)** — `s1e17-catwalk-clue` | `l2-l05`–`l2-l08` (invite · report what was said · seek agreement · **identify a person/thing by a relative clause**) | **Noun-modifying clauses** (「ラベルを付け替えたカード」= the card whose label was changed / 「さっき動いた猫」= the cat that moved just now); **animal + flat counters** (猫が二匹／カードが何枚); **reporting + seeking agreement** (「〜と言っていたでしょう?」) to confirm which card was re-labelled | **Grammar** (relative clauses) + **vocabulary** (counters) + **reading** (mislabeled cards) + light **listening** (who said the label was right) | The cards are visually near-identical and one was tampered with. To make placement count as evidence he must nominalize actions into things you can compare (the folding, the fading, the position) and hedge his conclusion — exactly the N2 move from "I saw" to "this suggests, to this confidence level." **Tie-back to the reservoir.** His lead sits squarely on `l2-l05`–`l2-l08`, whose transfer function is *"invite, report what was said, seek agreement, and identify a person by a relative clause."* The catwalk-clue scene exercises all four in one motion: he *reports* what the card claimed, *seeks agreement* that it was re-labelled, and *identifies* the tampered card by relative clause — with counters doing the enumeration the reservoir's neighbours (Ch 12's counting, Ch 16's compare-then/now) set up.

## 5. Comedy & callbacks

**Running joke:** the **paper-cat marker**. It maps to the plot-level `callback:paper-cat` family (owners: Tom, Felix, Stasi; budget 5), and Felix owns the middle two moments of its lifecycle.

| Lifecycle stage | Chapter | Owner | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Seed** | Ch 5 (Tom) | Tom | A tiny paper cat marks the *least* dramatic clue in Tom's oversized game — pure mascot, comic anticlimax. |
| **Echo** | **Ch 17 (Felix, lead)** | **Felix** | Felix follows the *moved* marker through mislabeled cards. The joke gains a job: the mascot is now a trail. |
| **Transform** | **Ch 35 (Felix returns)** | **Felix** | Paper, ink, and placement make the marker *version evidence rather than a mascot* — the cute thing dates the page. |
| **Payoff** | Ch 44 (Stasi) | Stasi | Stasi uses a *new neutral* marker to hold a withheld passage's place without exposing its content — the idea Felix's restraint (Ch 29) and repair seed made possible. |

**How the comedy works and stays kind.** The humour is *timing and callback*, never humiliation: the gag is that the silliest object on the board keeps turning out to matter, and that the class keeps under-rating it exactly as they under-rate Felix's "just noticing." Every laugh is at the *situation* (a paper cat outlasting the dramatic clues), never at a person. Felix's delight is the through-line the audience roots for; the payoff lands because the mascot everyone dismissed became the one honest witness.

**Secondary comic beat (behavioural, not a prop):** Felix reflexively counts things out loud with the right counter and then hands the count over as a question — *"三匹…いや、四匹? 誰か数えて"* — a small recurring rhythm that doubles as counter drill. Keep it a light tic, not a catchphrase; the voice contract says catchphrases never substitute for the four load-bearing voice fields.

## 6. Do-not

- **Do not** invent biography: no job, age, nationality, home, family, or a real cat of his own. Canon: *"may use only recorded traits, not invented biography."* Only observation, cats-as-one-lens, question-passing, and the paper-cat marker are authorized.
- **Do not** let cats become his whole personality. Registry consent note: *"cats are one attention home, never his whole personality."* Foreground **observation and delight-as-method**; cats are the vehicle, not the identity. He should carry scenes that have no cat in them (Ch 21, 29, 35).
- **Do not** make his delight cloying or make him the comic-relief simpleton. His arc is that joy is *rigorous* — undercutting that turns "joy can be method" into "joy is a distraction," the opposite of canon.
- **Do not** have him solve the Ch 29 boundary by cheerfully filling Jenny's gap. That beat exists to *limit* his instinct; the lesson is restraint. Reversing it breaks both his arc and the `open-chair` callback.
- **Do not** claim the paper-cat's final payoff for Felix. Ch 44 is **Stasi's**; Felix seeds and transforms, then passes it — consistent with his hand-off character.
- **Do not** over-claim evidence in Ch 35. He describes placement and hedges (*〜かもしれない*); he does not "solve" provenance. The mystery resolves to *stewardship*, not a single author (STORY-BIBLE causal spine).
- **Do not** ship a runtime portrait or imply approved likeness. Presentation is `name-only`; his sprites' quality is a pipeline benchmark, not a diegetic or clearance fact.
