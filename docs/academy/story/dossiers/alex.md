## 1. Who they really are

Alex is the quiet senpai of the class — the one with, by some distance, the most cinematic real life, delivered in the flattest possible voice. He is a full salaryman: he works for Nippon Steel (the owner's own canonical example), gets Japanese-language work email about pig-iron imports, and once went **live on Channel News Asia in Singapore** to talk Asian–US steel markets. His entire account of that is: **"I survived."** It was, he'll admit deadpan, "always my dream to be on Singaporean TV." He travels constantly — US, Canada (family there), Texas, and enough time zones that his running self-diagnosis is that he has "forgotten all my Japanese" — and yet he shows up. He climbed Mount Fuji and mentions it the way other people mention picking up dry cleaning. He has cousins in Japan, used to live on the very street where the Dalston Japanese market is held, and in the class photo he is the one standing at the back.

The unifying trait — and the thing that makes him a gift to a *learning* VN — is **register as personality**. Alex undersells the biggest news in the room. He announces a mountain, an apartment flood he has to rush home for, a live TV appearance, or an entire emigration in the same even, itemised tone. The class's comic education is learning to *listen for what he does not stress* — because the load-bearing clause is always the one delivered most calmly. This is the exact seam the canon voice anchor already names ("large news delivered like a timetable"), so nothing here contradicts the four-season arc; the real texture just gives the timetable its cargo. His warmth shows up sideways: he'd far rather help a newer learner than be helped, he pocket-dials the group chat and apologises for it ("Sorry I pocket dialled haha"), and he leans lightly into a self-aware cowboy-salaryman bit ("私はテキサスにいきました、そして今はカウボーイです"). With Peter he shares a steel-industry kinship and the group's **サラリーマン** identity gag ("わたしは… We are サラリーマン"); with Sophie there's a standing dynamic where she translates his Japanese work emails; Henry name-checks him as the one already living the dream. He is experienced and unflappable, says little, and means all of it.

**Reconciliation with the anchor:** every real detail deepens the established "understated, refuses departure-as-courage" reading rather than bending it. Steel/CNA/Texas/Fuji all become *why* his understatement is load-bearing — a man who has genuinely done large things and therefore doesn't need to perform them. The one adjustment the real texture makes to how writers stage him: under pressure his voice **narrows further** (more timetable, fewer words), it does not open into eloquent vulnerability. That is his contradiction and it must survive Season 4.

## 2. Four-season arc (beat by beat)

Alex's deep arc: a man to whom "leaving for Japan" could be handed as the obvious heroic climax — and the story refuses that framing. His future beat resolves as *leaving, staying, or undecided, none ranked* (matrix consent note). Across five beats he teaches the class to hear enormous news calmly and to let a departure be ordinary rather than brave.

- **Arrival — Ch 11 · `s1e11-storm-route-variant` (LEAD).** A fictional storm strips the signs off Alex's exhibition route. He does not panic; he re-routes from memory, narrating the recovery like a train timetable: "I checked the map, then walked. Long, but quiet." The scene establishes his attention pattern (sequence over drama) and seeds his relationship callback: *the biggest fact arrives underscored by the calmest clause*. Real texture: the seasoned traveller and Fuji-climber for whom a lost route is a solvable inconvenience, not a crisis. Reason-giving without shame — "I changed course" is not an apology.
- **Contribution — Ch 20 · `s1e20-map-from-memory` (Rose leads; Alex supports).** Rose gathers overlapping accounts of the old route instead of restoring one perfect copy. Alex's bounded competence alters the shared work: he supplies the *prepared, pre-sequenced* version — he had already checked and set things up in advance — and, crucially, he says **enough without narrating everything**. His understatement becomes a method, not just a tic: he gives the class the load-bearing steps and withholds the padding. This echoes his arrival register into a useful action (relationship-callback lifecycle: contribution echoes the arrival seed).
- **Limit — Ch 30 · `s3e06-two-schedules` (shared LEAD with Angel).** Alex and Angel hold **conflicting but defensible** versions of the event plan. This is his Limit beat: a disagreement, not a disclosure. His timetable-calm is now under pressure — and the danger the scene dramatises is that his understatement can read as certainty. Neither document is treated as a moral verdict; the class must separate sequence, hearsay, and evidence. Alex models naming his own uncertainty ("I thought it was Thursday, but I heard it changed") rather than defending his version as truth. He touches — without owning — the plot-level `overbuilt-schedule` callback family at its **transform** point (owners Sam/Angel/Robert): two defensible schedules force the class to state assumptions and decision ownership.
- **Return — Ch 39 · `s4e03-polite-no` (Robert & Sam lead; Alex returns).** The venue asks for a cleaner, more inspirational plan than the evidence supports; the group declines with one clear alternative and room to answer — the **payoff** of `overbuilt-schedule`. Alex's Return proves the ensemble *heard* his Limit: the class now handles a competing-plans situation the way the two-schedules beat taught them — hold the boundary, keep the relationship, concede in form while refusing in substance. Alex, the salaryman fluent in formal decline, is the natural model here; his contribution is showing that a keigo "no" can be warm.
- **Future — Ch 43 · `s4e07-journey-not-everyone-takes` (LEAD).** Japan-related opportunities split the ensemble into people leaving, staying, and undecided. Alex's real-life pivot — a job offer in Japan (Nippon Steel, the owner's canonical example; the offer is the turn, "C5.7") — is the gravitational centre, and the chapter's whole job is to **let it be ordinary**. He announces it flattest of all: **「あのさ…来月、日本で働くことになったんだ。」** The class refuses to let him wave off the send-off — *the room's reaction IS the scene* — and the "for-work-someday" reason-token that has floated through the term lands on him here as payoff. He gives Sam-style warmth outward ("Sorry to see you leave, Sam-san" energy, now turned on himself). The resolution holds ambiguity: departure is not crowned as the brave choice; staying and undecided are equally whole.

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — most important)

**Learning speciality:** *understatement as a grammatical skill* — Alex is the class's teacher of **register, sequencing, and plans-under-uncertainty**. His scenes make the learner practise the difference between saying a lot and saying *enough*, and between a fact, a plan, and a rumour. His travel/steel/mountain life is the authentic reason the target language is needed: a real globe-trotter genuinely must sequence a re-route, report a changed schedule, decline formally at work, and announce a move — in Japanese, calmly.

Beat-by-beat, tied to the CONTENT-LINKAGE reservoir:

- **Ch 11 (reservoir `l1-l16`; transfer: *locate and reroute under changed conditions*).**
  - **Language functions the scene makes the learner NEED:** te-form sequencing of actions (〜て、〜て / 〜てから), giving and following directions with place particles (〜を / 〜で / 〜に), and light past-experience/travel narration.
  - **Activity types:** grammar (te-form chaining) + reading/listening (route directions) + speaking transfer (re-narrate the recovered route). Writing micro-task: a two-line "what I did, in order" note.
  - **Authentic reason:** a real heavy traveller and Fuji-summiter who has lost a trailhead sign before. "I checked the map, then walked. Long, but quiet." — the learner needs *order* words because the storm made order the whole problem. Changing course "without shame" is the pragmatic frame: reason-giving, not apology.

- **Ch 20 (reservoir `l2-l16`–`l2-l18`; transfer: *prepare in advance, sequence action, and say enough without narrating everything*).**
  - **Language functions:** preparation aspect (〜ておく), sequencing across a longer stretch (〜たあとで / 〜てから / 〜とおりに), and **selective narration register** — choosing which steps to state and which to leave implicit.
  - **Activity types:** grammar + speaking + a discrimination/writing task (given a padded account, cut it to the load-bearing steps). This is the ext-alex-route-memory (N4, sequencing + past experience) hook.
  - **Authentic reason:** Alex is the classmate who *already set it up* and reports only what matters. His understatement becomes a teachable editing skill — the learner practises saying enough, mirroring how a busy salaryman actually briefs a group.

- **Ch 30 (reservoir: required N3 family for Ch 28–30 — *conditions, refusal, role negotiation, conflicting plans*).**
  - **Language functions:** reported speech and **hearsay/evidentiality** (〜と言っていた / 〜そうだ / 〜らしい), softened conditions (〜たら / 〜なら), and oral renegotiation.
  - **Activity types:** listening (hear a softened condition) → reading (compare two schedules) → speaking (renegotiate or decline orally) → writing (a clarified plan). Four-skill proof exactly as the linkage table requires.
  - **Authentic reason:** the man whose actual job is coordinating shipments and calendars across time zones is exactly who would hold a "defensible but wrong" version. The learner needs *hearsay markers* to say "I heard it changed" instead of asserting a false certainty — the pedagogical heart of the scene.

- **Ch 39 (reservoir: N2/N1 family for Ch 37–39 — *concession, formal refusal*).**
  - **Language functions:** keigo, concession-then-refusal frames (せっかく〜ですが / 〜のは難しいと思います), and offering one clear alternative (〜のかわりに / 〜ではどうでしょうか).
  - **Activity types:** reading/listening (interpret the venue's polite ask) + speaking transfer (produce a warm, bounded "no") + writing (a short audience-aware reply).
  - **Authentic reason:** a salaryman declines things politely for a living. Alex's return is the credible model that a formal "no" preserves the relationship — the exact language the class needs to keep the venue and the boundary.

- **Ch 43 (reservoir `l4` band — N1 family for Ch 43–45 — *plans under uncertainty and non-comparative futures*).**
  - **Language functions:** decided-plan and volitional forms (〜ことになった / 〜つもり / 〜予定 / 〜(よ)うと思う), uncertainty hedges (〜かもしれない / 〜かどうか / まだわからない), and **non-comparative future talk** (stating one's path without ranking it above another's).
  - **Activity types:** listening/reading (parse the flat-toned announcement and chat updates) + structured writing production (author separate decided, possible, and staying/restarting updates without ranking them). The current runtime records writing, not speaking; the comprehension challenge remains register-based because the learner must catch the load-bearing clause under the calm before writing it.
  - **Authentic reason:** the real move to Japan for work. 「来月、日本で働くことになったんだ。」 The learner needs 〜ことになった precisely because Alex refuses to dramatise it, and needs non-comparative framing so that "I'm leaving," "I'm staying," and "I don't know yet" can all be said without one becoming the brave answer.

**Cross-beat learning through-line:** direction/sequence (N5) → preparation + selective sequencing (N4) → hearsay + renegotiation (N3) → formal concession/refusal (N2) → decided-plans-under-uncertainty (N1). Alex is a clean difficulty ramp whose *register* difficulty rises even faster than his grammar difficulty — the learner is always training the ear as much as the grammar.
