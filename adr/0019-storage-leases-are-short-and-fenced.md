# Storage leases are short where the work is storage, and fenced

GM storage has no compare-and-set, so Yomu serializes each read-modify-write of shared storage with a Storage Lease (`withGmStorageLease`): a bakery lock in which every contending tab owns one claim key, and a claim counts until its `leaseUntil`. Every lease used to last one minute and nothing told a dead holder from a slow one, so a tab that died mid-save held other tabs' saves up for about a minute behind a disabled button.

Decisions:

1. A lease whose critical section is only storage work lasts `STORAGE_WORK_LEASE_MS` (five seconds) and names the keys it guards: the local deck (`local-yomu-srs-deck`), Settings persistence (`reader-settings-persistence`) and Live Review Claims (`newtab-live-review`). A tab that dies mid-save holds the others up for five seconds at most while storage answers promptly (decision 7).
2. A live holder keeps its lease. It renews on entry and whenever a sixth of its claim has passed, checked on every timer tick (a third of the lease), so while storage is prompt every tick renews, with half a tick of slack for the time the last renewal took to land, and a tick may run about 2.3 s late. Every managed write of a guarded key also renews the lease when due, so a holder that keeps saving keeps its lease however the browser throttles its timers.
3. A holder whose lease lapsed cannot write. Other tabs treat a claim as gone at its `leaseUntil`; the holder stops writing guarded keys a landing margin (one second, or two storage round trips when storage is slower) before that. A lapsed claim is never written back to life: a waiter queues again, and a holder's guarded writes, rollback deletes included, are refused with `StorageLeaseLapsedError`, which managed storage passes through unwrapped. A tab that resumes with a stale snapshot therefore cannot overwrite what another tab committed meanwhile. It may leave an unreferenced new card record, never a torn deck index or settings pair. Writes that landed before the lapse stay, though: a grade overwrites its existing card record in place before the index write. A holder whose guarded writes all passed the fence finished under a live lease, so a renewal that fails after its last write (a tab frozen with one in flight) does not fail the operation.
4. The cost is fail-safe. A tab suspended for more than about four seconds mid-save (an iOS app switch, a frozen background tab) has the rest of that save refused. A refused deck save first checks, under a fresh lease, whether every record it changed is already stored as it meant: if so it happened, so the index is committed and the save reported done, because a grade reported failed would be graded again by the learner or a queued retry. Otherwise nothing it wrote is referenced and saving again is safe: the learner is told "Your Academy deck was not saved because saving was interrupted. Try again.", not that storage may be full, and Study does not queue the grade. A background tab whose timers are throttled to once a minute may re-queue on each poll and time out. A Live Review Claim refused this way asks once more instead of letting the grade through, because the claims it read may be stale; if what it read already refused the grade, that refusal stands.
5. A lease held across network requests keeps the one-minute default: the review queue (`newtab-grade-queue`, ADR-0018 decision 6) and Academy account sync. Their holders can be alive yet write nothing while they wait for a provider, and a short lease would let a second tab send the same review. The epoch-control lease keeps 30 seconds. The Japanese-site-language preference lease keeps a minute, because its section waits for the Settings lease inside it.
6. A learner's save says "Waiting for another よむ tab to finish saving…" only once another tab has held it up for about 1.5 seconds: a same-origin web lock this tab did not request, or another tab's claim ahead in the queue. Slow storage, such as Safari waking an extension's background page, and this tab's own earlier save do not count. The status clears when the save proceeds or fails.
7. Slow storage only delays a save. Each claim write times its two storage calls (the write and the read confirming it) and takes the faster as the round trip, so one call a frozen tab held up does not count. The next claim lasts its lease plus twenty of those round trips, up to the one-minute default: a waiting tab polls in about seven (the three-read mutation fence, the claims, its claim write) and renews once half its claim is spent. A renewal that finds storage slower renews again at once, and a claim counts as soon as its write lands. Claim writes are not wrapped in the managed-state mutation fence: the claimant checks it on every poll and around its section, and managed writes of guarded keys check it themselves. With a fixed five-second claim fenced on every write, a deck save lapsed itself at 250 ms a call and timed out at 400 ms, where 2.0.4's one-minute lease saved at 600 ms. Storage that answers in a millisecond or two stretches a claim by a few dozen milliseconds. A tab whose storage jumps from prompt to more than about a second a call in the middle of a save can still have that save refused, as in decision 4.
