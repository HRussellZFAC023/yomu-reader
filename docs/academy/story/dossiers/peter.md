## 1. Who they really are

Peter is the polite, buttoned-up professional gentleman of the class — the one adult in the room who arrived from a real office day and still has the register of the meeting he just left. He works an office job in or near the **steel / commodities world** (he genuinely needed to follow the Asian–US steel markets for work, and once relied on CNA's steel coverage to do his job), clocks off around 5, and sometimes works from home. He's been to **Denmark**. He's the kind of learner who flags the **JLPT December application window** to the group before anyone else has thought about signing up.

His comedy is dry and understated. He says "**Splendid**." He makes wry, deadpan asides about work and about LinkedIn — *"the stuff LinkedIn profile-pic dreams are made of"* — and he is the reliable instigator of the **pre-class ramen run** (*六時にいっぷどに合いますか？* — "shall we meet at Ippudo at six?"), the fixed social ritual he runs with Aakash and Henry. He has a gentle pedant's delight in language: he shared the Guardian piece on Japanese name order, and he notices literal kanji meanings out loud (*大雨* — "literally just 'big rain', nice").

In the class he began **peripheral** — paired with Shaun in the first-term scrapbook, present in the class photo (standing in front of the second Tom), the dependable presence who turns up in the exact seat nobody thought to check. That last detail *is* his canon voice anchor: the unoccupied-looking seat. His deeper arc, straight from the bible, is that this quiet observer **grows into the person who asks whether ownership is the wrong question** — the one whose few words alter the whole task. The Academy keeps his real texture and reads it as *understatement, not absence*: his silence is a professional's habit of waiting until he has the one question worth the room's time. When he does speak, it reframes.

His Japanese in-fiction is the natural home for his real voice: careful, slightly formal, keigo-flavoured. He reaches for `〜なければなりません` and set phrases; when he can't make a session he writes a properly polite apology rather than a shrug. This maps cleanly onto the canon specialty — **review, questions, observation** — without inventing anything: a man who reviews a market before he trades an opinion, and asks the one clarifying question that saves everyone an hour.

**Reconciliation with the anchor:** every real detail here *deepens* "sparse questions that reframe" rather than fighting it. The salaryman-formal register explains the keigo; the "waits, then asks the framing question" growth (VOICE line: *"Peter waits so long that others decide around him; growth is asking the framing question in time"*) is his four-season spine. Nothing here implies a hidden biography — his job, JLPT interest, and ramen habit are surface, benign texture the owner explicitly authorized.

## 2. Four-season arc across his five continuity beats

His beats follow the matrix schedule **Arrival(21) → Contribution(25) → Limit(34) → Return(42) → Future(48)**, and his owned callback rides on top of them.

**Ch 21 — Arrival & LEAD · `s1e21-questions-in-the-dark`.**
The atlas district goes dark and cannot be restored from any single person's memory. Peter's method — *one strong question per learner* — rebuilds it from partial knowledge: he doesn't supply answers, he supplies the question that makes each classmate's fragment usable, then has them teach it back. This is the **echo** of `callback:one-strong-question` (seeded Ch 7, where a question opened a discussion without needing shared taste). The peripheral observer becomes, for one chapter, the quiet leader — observation *becomes* leadership. Real texture: the steel-desk professional who unlocks a stuck meeting by asking the reframing question, not by knowing the number himself.

**Ch 25 — Contribution · `s3e01-after-the-applause` (support).**
Exhibition feedback arrives as a single polite phrase that could mean praise, doubt, or refusal. Mika and Francis compare what they heard; Peter's contribution is the buttoned-up professional's ear for register — he's spent years decoding whether a client email means yes, no, or "not like this." His careful keigo instinct lets him name the tonal ambiguity instead of flattening it. (Adjacent, non-beat texture worth authoring: **Ch 27 `s3e03-helpful-rewrite`** — a polished edit makes *Peter's* contribution "sound like nobody." His sparse, un-showy voice is exactly the kind that a well-meaning rewrite erases; Sophie's lesson there — repair the error, don't replace the voice — lands hardest because the voice being overwritten is his.)

**Ch 34 — Limit · `s3e10-empty-microphone` (support).**
A public-speaking host drops out and the class must redistribute the microphone *without coercion*. Peter's **limit** is that he will not seize the mic — and the class must learn that his quiet role (asking the question, reviewing, taking the notes nobody else kept) is **full participation**, not a gap to fill. His real constraints — off at 5, sometimes remote, can't always be front-of-house — give the scene an honest shape: the reliable man who keeps the machine running is not obliged to become the performer. The **Return** proof is that the ensemble redesigns the evening around his real role rather than pressuring him into a louder one.

**Ch 42 — Return & LEAD · `s4e06-open-question`.**
The season-4 payoff of his whole arc and the **transform** of `callback:one-strong-question`: the class is deadlocked over naming a single owner for the finished atlas, and Peter asks whether *finishing it requires an owner at all* — whether the demand for one name is itself the wrong frame. The class rejects the false premise. The peripheral observer of Season 1 is now the person whose one question dissolves the argument. Real texture: his professional habit of asking "do we actually need this?" plus his naming-convention pedantry (who *owns* a name, and in what order?) make him the natural challenger of an ownership premise.

**Ch 48 — Future · `s4e12-next-page`.**
Graduation. The **payoff** of his callback is that *the learner may leave an open question as a valid final contribution* — Peter's entire method, handed to the player as a legitimate way to end. His **future** beat: he flagged the JLPT December window in Season 1; his open horizon is sitting the exam — deliberately left as an unclosed question, not a promise, honoring the matrix rule that a future leaves one possibility open without predicting a real person's life.

## 3. The Japanese his story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — most important)

**Learning speciality:** *question formation, review, teach-back, and register-reading.* Peter is the class's engine for turning a stuck task into an answerable one by asking the right question, and for reviewing/parsing formal register. His scenes make the learner NEED interrogatives, embedded questions (`〜か`, `〜かどうか`), teach-back, reported speech, and eventually counterfactual argument.

**Beat-by-beat learning (each tied to the CONTENT-LINKAGE reservoir):**

**Ch 21 · reservoir `l2-l19`–`l2-l22`** — transfer function: *build, carry, revise, and forecast an intention through questions and teach-back.*
- **Language functions the scene makes NEEDED:** interrogative + embedded question formation (`何を`, `どこで`, `〜か`, `〜かどうか`); intention/forecast (`〜つもり`, `〜ようと思います`, volitional); `〜とき` for the moment of asking; polite request for confirmation.
- **Activity types:** *speaking* + *grammar* (question formation) + *transfer* (teach-back). The learner must FORM a precise question, ask it of a "classmate," then TEACH the answer back — the changed-context transfer is rebuilding the dark district with only questions as tools.
- **Why the real texture makes it authentic:** Peter-the-professional unlocks meetings with one clarifying question rather than an answer; the scene makes *asking well* the competence, so the learner practices question formation as a genuine problem-solving tool, not a drill.

**Ch 25 · reservoir `l2-l23`–`l2-l25` shading into 25–27's N3 family (reported speech, implication, register-preserving paraphrase)** — transfer function: infer intent from a polite phrase; report what was said.
- **Language functions NEEDED:** reported speech (`〜と言っていました`, `〜そうです`); implication/hedged reading of one polite phrase; register awareness (keigo vs plain, honne vs tatemae).
- **Activity types:** *listening* + *grammar* (reported speech) + *transfer* (compare two heard readings). Learner hears one ambiguous line and must report it three ways (praise / doubt / refusal) with accurate reported-speech framing.
- **Why the real texture:** the salaryman who reads client email for its real meaning is the perfect diegetic reason to teach that a polite Japanese phrase carries several readings — the LinkedIn-decoder's ear becomes the learner's skill.

**Ch 34 · reservoir 34–36 N2 (formal event language, role negotiation, permission scope)** — transfer function: negotiate a public role without coercion; repair spontaneously.
- **Language functions NEEDED:** offering/requesting/declining a role (`〜ていただけますか`, `〜てもらえますか`, soft refusal `ちょっと…`); formal event register; spontaneous repair phrases.
- **Activity types:** *speaking* + *listening* + *transfer* (redistribute roles live). Learner must offer a role, hear a "no," and redesign — practicing that a declined role is answered with a new plan, not pressure.
- **Why the real texture:** Peter's real availability limits (off at 5, sometimes remote) supply an honest reason someone declines the mic; the learner practices consent-respecting role language on a case where "no" is legitimate and not a failure to fix.

**Ch 42 · reservoir 40–42 N1 (perspective, compatible accounts, counterfactuals, argument framing)** — transfer function: challenge a false premise without flattening the speaker.
- **Language functions NEEDED:** counterfactual conditionals (`〜としたら`, `〜ていなかったら`, `〜なら`); premise-framing (`そもそも`, `〜という前提`); argument structure that questions rather than contradicts.
- **Activity types:** *reading* + *speaking* + *transfer* (dismantle a stuck argument with one question). Learner must build the reframing question in N1 register — attacking the frame, not the person.
- **Why the real texture:** his "do we actually need one owner?" question and his name-order pedantry make ownership-questioning his natural voice; the learner practices high-level argument as the quiet challenger, not the loud winner.

**Ch 48 · graduation composition (`s4e12-next-page`)** — transfer function: produce one bounded final contribution and choose its tone.
- **Language functions NEEDED:** composition/summary register; leaving an *open question* as a valid finished contribution; future-plan language held at `〜つもり` / open-ended rather than committed.
- **Activity types:** *writing* + *transfer* (author the final bounded page) + bond/continuity payoff. The learner may legitimately end by leaving a question — Peter's whole method licensed as an outcome.
- **Why the real texture:** his JLPT-December horizon models a future stated as an open question, teaching the learner that "I don't know yet, but here is the question I'll carry" is a complete, adult way to close.

**learningFunctions (consolidated):** question-formation, embedded-questions (`〜か`/`〜かどうか`), teach-back, intention/forecast (`〜つもり`/volitional), reported-speech, register/keigo-reading, role-negotiation-and-refusal, counterfactual-argument, graduation-composition.

## 4. Elective bond route (PROPOSED — requires a `bond-authored` manifest entry)

> **Canon status:** Peter is `continuity-only`. This is a proposal for a future reviewed manifest entry (exactly six ordered `appointmentPackageIds`, platonic, task-named, deferrable). It is **not** shipped canon and must not be treated as one. Every appointment states its activity and an easy way to decline; one clear "no" ends persuasion.

Route theme: *the quiet professional who teaches you to ask before you answer.*

1. **Recognition (ordinary task)** — *Ippudo at six.* Coordinate the pre-class ramen meet: *六時にいっぷどに合いますか？* Confirm time, place, and who's coming.
   - **Language function:** time + invitation + confirmation (`〜に`, `〜ませんか`, `大丈夫です`).
2. **Different route** — *Reading the market.* Peter asks you to help him skim a Japanese steel-market headline (Nikkei/CNA-style) he needs for work; the "study" is real-world reading, not a textbook page.
   - **Language function:** headline reading — topic marker `〜は`, noun compounds, asking a word's meaning (`〜ってどういう意味ですか`).
3. **Reciprocity** — *He quizzes you.* Peter runs a short JLPT-style question drill on you, using his sparse-reframing style: he asks the one question that shows what you don't yet know, then has you teach the point back.
   - **Language function:** embedded questions (`〜かどうか`) + teach-back + self-review.
4. **Friction** — *Name order.* He shares the Guardian article on Japanese name order and gently, pedantically corrects an assumption you stated; a small, register-polite disagreement about surname-first vs given-first.
   - **Language function:** stating an opinion + polite disagreement (`〜と思いますが…`, `〜という`).
5. **Repair** — *Locked out.* Peter locks himself out of the office (or a work call runs past 5) and misses a session; he sends a careful keigo apology. You accept it and reframe the missed meet as no big deal.
   - **Language function:** apology + keigo (`申し訳ありません`, `〜てしまいました`) + accepting graciously.
6. **Future** — *The December window.* Peter asks whether you'll sit the JLPT too — and leaves it genuinely open, no pressure, an invitation you can decline.
   - **Language function:** future plans (`〜つもりです`) + open, low-pressure invitation.

Fallback (if unplayed): a neutral shared-task memory ("studied for the exam together once"), never exposing any withdrawal reason.

## 6. Do-not

- **Do not give him a hidden biography.** Canon is explicit: his sparse questions must change the *frame*, not imply concealed history. No secret backstory, no dramatic past. The silence is method, not mystery.
- **Do not make his quietness a deficit to be cured.** Ch 34's whole point is that his quiet role is full participation. Never write the class "fixing" his reticence by pushing him to the mic; the growth is *him* asking the framing question in time, on his terms.
- **Do not let a "helpful rewrite" stand.** Ch 27 exists to protect his voice; never author a scene where his contribution is smoothed into anonymity *without* the correction landing.
- **Do not weaponize real texture.** His steel/commodities job, the office lock-out, the Denmark trip, the JLPT interest, and the LinkedIn/naming pedantry are benign, warm colour. Never spin them into invented workplace drama, financial stress, medical content, or relationship gossip. Represent the professional gentleman with dignity.
- **Do not turn JLPT/Japan into a performance prize.** His December-exam horizon stays an *open* question (matrix Future rule) — never a ranked achievement or a "brave choice" others are measured against.
- **Do not overwrite Shaun.** Their Season-1 pairing is real, but Shaun is story-only with no specialty; do not let Peter's scenes retroactively author a Shaun biography.
- **Do not exceed the callback budget** (`callback:one-strong-question` = 5 uses) or create a second seed. Package manifests may add small local callbacks but cannot re-seed the plot-level one.
- **Surnames never.** First name only, per the standing safety frame; all characterization sits behind Rie's AI-fiction disclaimer.
