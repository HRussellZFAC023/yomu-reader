## 1. Who they really are

Aakash is the group's **warm centre of gravity** — the one who books the table, confirms the time twice, welcomes the newcomer, and softens a plan until nobody's left out. He created the class chat (originally named around ともだち) and appointed himself, unasked, its reminder service. If the canon anchor is "energetic invitations and concrete city detail," the real person is exactly that: the friend who answers "where shall we meet?" before anyone else has finished reading the question, and who already knows which exit of the station has the good ramen.

He is a **self-styled サラリーマン** — a corporate office worker whose desk sits near the university, so class is a short, over-optimistic walk from a 1:1 that always seems to overrun. Work is forever scheduling something (a late one-to-one, a "sorting something with the team" evening) precisely when Japanese class resumes; he treats this as a personal running gag rather than a grievance. He commutes into London by train, which is where a lot of his study actually happens — Nihongoal on the platform, a Terrace House episode he only half-follows ("they talk a lot about their feelings, so I'm quite lost").

He is, by his own cheerful admission, an **old soul** — got tired of clubbing at eighteen, prefers a quiet couple of pints with a mate who lives thirty seconds away over "going out out." Family-oriented (spent his birthday on low-key drinks and meals with family; an auntie visits and that weekend is sacred). He suffers badly in heat — bought a portable AC unit and will tell you about it. Stylish in a way that reads as taste rather than effort: **anime streetwear, city-pop on rotation, classic cars in the camera roll, and a Hello Kitty charm clipped to an otherwise cool outfit.** He rates the world aesthetically — a good evening, to Aakash, is one that would make a decent city-pop album cover.

His real reason to be here is **immersion and travel**: the dream is a **solo camera road-trip around Japan that deliberately minimises Tokyo** — Osaka, Minō Falls, a 70s/80s music bar he'd recommend to anyone, a car show, the World Expo, new experiences tested entirely in his own Japanese. That dream is the engine under all his study, and it's the honest answer to why an inconsistent student keeps showing up.

**Reconciliation with the anchor.** The real detail *deepens* the locked voice rather than fighting it. "Learns to pause before adding momentum" and "becomes reliable during disagreement" map cleanly onto the real old-soul/homebody/diplomat: the same warmth that makes him accelerate a plan is the warmth that, grown, learns to hold still and let someone else's condition land. His stylist's eye ("would this be a good album cover?") is the concrete-city-detail attention the anchor names. Nothing here contradicts the four-season arc; it furnishes it.

**How he differs from his contrast partner (Robert).** Both push a plan forward, but Aakash's momentum is *route and vibe* ("get off at TCR, it's two minutes, there's a spot") in fast short additions; Robert's is *welcome and logistics*, opened broadly then itemised. Keep them adjacent and never let their turns blur: Aakash names the street; Robert names the table.

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

**Learning speciality:** Aakash is the **directions-and-plans specialist with a stylist's descriptive ear.** His scenes make the learner *need* the language of getting somewhere, coordinating people, restating what someone else wants, and talking about likes and futures — always with concrete city detail as the reason. Pedagogically he also models the *authentic inconsistent learner*: enthusiastic, immersion-first, honest about skipped homework, which licenses the game to teach self-repair and "partial is enough" without shame.

Every beat below follows the linkage pattern: **Need → Input → Lesson → Repair → Transfer → Consequence → Return.** The chapter is a transfer scene, never a lesson wrapper.

### Ch 3 — reservoir `l1-l03`–`l1-l04` (Minna L3–L4)
- **Language functions the scene makes the learner NEED:** place demonstratives and location questions (ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ, 〜はどこですか), the place/direction particles (に・へ・で), **distinguishing identity from location**, and **asking permission / asking for help** (手伝ってください, 〜てもいいですか, お願いします).
- **Activity types hosted:** speaking (live route-giving), listening (following a spoken route), grammar (place particles), and a **transfer** where the label surface disappears and the learner must *ask* for the missing place-name rather than read it.
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** Aakash is confidently walking everyone through a rainy route — the labels vanish, so directions become a *live spoken* problem, not a worksheet. His having-to-ask-for-help is the exact moment "asking becomes competence," which is *the* function of the chapter. His album-cover eye motivates describing the *place*, not just naming it.

### Ch 11 — reservoir `l1-l16` (Minna L16)
- **Functions needed:** te-form sequencing and 〜てから ("go here, then turn, then it's on the left"), locating and **rerouting under changed conditions**.
- **Activity types:** listening + speaking transfer (rebuild a broken route aloud), grammar (te-form chaining).
- **Why the texture forces it:** the storm removed the signs; Aakash's commuter back-way knowledge only helps if he can *sequence* it in Japanese for someone else under pressure. Support role → the learner practises reception and confirmation, not just production.

### Ch 30 — required family: **N3 conditions, refusal, role negotiation, conflicting plans**
- **Functions needed:** **reported speech / restating a condition accurately** (〜と言っていました, 〜って), conditionals (〜たら・〜ば・〜なら), hearsay vs. evidence (〜そうです), and hedged, non-overriding refusal (ちょっと…, 〜んですが).
- **Activity types:** listening (hear two softened conditions), reading (two schedules), **speaking transfer** (restate the *other person's* condition before responding), writing (a clarified plan).
- **Why the texture forces it:** Aakash's whole Limit is that he wants to *add a plan*; the pedagogy makes the learner do what Aakash must learn — **repeat another person's condition back correctly before proposing.** The grammar (reported conditionals) is literally the shape of his growth. This is the scene's deepest learning-story fusion.

### Ch 39 — required family: **N2/N1 concession + formal refusal (keigo)**
- **Functions needed:** polite refusal with concession (申し訳ありませんが…, 〜かねます, 〜のは難しいです), offering one clear alternative, and **leaving room for an answer** (〜ていただけますか, 〜はいかがでしょうか).
- **Activity types:** speaking/writing transfer (decline the venue warmly), listening (hear the "cleaner ending" request's real pressure).
- **Why the texture forces it:** the diplomat who softens everything now needs the *formal* register to hold a boundary without breaking the relationship — his warmth channelled into keigo. The learner needs the polite-no that keeps the door open, exactly Aakash's return-beat behaviour.

### Ch 43 — required family: **N1 plans under uncertainty, non-comparative futures**
- **Functions needed:** intention and uncertainty (〜つもり, 〜たいと思っています, 〜かもしれない, volitional 〜(よ)う), talking about a solo trip and an open plan without ranking it against others'.
- **Activity types:** speaking (describe an uncertain future), reading/writing (a plan that isn't a promise).
- **Why the texture forces it:** his real road-trip dream is *genuinely* uncertain (budget-conscious, work, life) — so the language of "maybe, I'm thinking of, I intend to" is honest, not decorative. The learner practises futures that hold ambiguity, mirroring the chapter's refusal to make leaving the brave choice.

### Bonus authored transfer — the "two-reason café pitch" (`ext-aakash-two-reasons`, N4)
His foodie/aesthetic voice hosts a **し reason-listing** transfer: he pitches a place because *the music's good **and** it's near the station* — likes + description + **し … し**, twice. Pure Aakash: taste and route in one grammatical breath. Slot as a class-thread or bond-route surface.

**learningFunctions summary:** directions & place particles · asking permission and help · te-form sequencing & rerouting · reported speech / restating a condition · conditionals & hedged refusal · keigo concession & polite-no · plans under uncertainty (non-comparative futures) · likes + description with し reason-listing.
