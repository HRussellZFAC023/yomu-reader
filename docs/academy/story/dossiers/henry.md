## 1. Who they really are

Henry is the one who built the thing. He is the organizer of the real class and the person who coded the study app the whole game is a portrait of — which is the running self-referential joke of his character: *the study tool he built instead of studying is basically this game.* He is a grammar-obsessed tinkerer who reaches for a mechanism before he reaches for the person in front of him, and who catches the human detail a beat late — usually after the technical claim has already left his mouth, qualified with a soft "…probably" (`たぶん`). That late qualifier is his signature; it is written into his canonical diagnostic line (`表示は直りました。たぶん。`).

The real texture all rhymes with the canon anchor:

- **Tool-builder.** He coded a browser extension to fold the mnemonic site Uchisen into his study app, talks about writing an Anki plugin, grinds jpdb with userscripts, and helped patch the community immersion userscript himself ("I helped create the patch for it actually"). He made a public YouTube video on studying with jpdb. In-canon this becomes his **mnemonic-and-integration study design** without ever naming a real employer.
- **Grammar-first learner.** Cure Dolly for は vs が, Tae Kim, switched from Minna no Nihongo to Genki, hoards resources (paper + digital textbooks he gives his spares away). Reading-strong, and openly admits his handwriting/output has atrophied ("I don't write anything out now") — a genuine learner weakness the story can *use*, not hide.
- **Generous to a fault.** Books the restaurants, buys the group's tickets, offers to treat people (offered to cover a friend's convention ticket as a birthday gift), writes the welcome messages in Japanese, organizes the group gift for sensei. Warm, emoji-heavy, first to say "you got this / proud of you / it'll be fineee." He is the hype-man.
- **Endearingly over-trusting of his own systems.** Leans on AI for logistics and lets it miscalculate a bill split — the anecdote that produced "how am I gonna read Japanese if I can't read English." He experiments constantly with AI tools and enjoys comparing how models behave. In-canon this reads as **over-trusting a mechanism past the human question** — his exact pressure behavior.
- **The Japan undertone.** He has a long-distance relationship (met on HelloTalk) and is "working hard to be with her someday"; solo Japan trips he didn't want to come back from; a December birthday. This is his quiet off-stage motivation for why the language *matters* to him — kept as warmth, never as class romance (all in-class bonds stay platonic; the HelloTalk/romance route is the player's, not Henry's, and not with anyone in this class).

The self-portrait he'd never write for himself: *the one with too many laptops, who wants a shortcut that isn't cheating and to have already built the thing before he needed it.* He proposes the biggest version of every plan and keeps raising the challenge (the summer "speedrun to chapter 41"). His growth across four seasons is the honest one for a builder: stop hiding behind the tool, edit instead of defend, document the limits, and leave the mechanism understandable to someone who isn't you.

## 2. Four-season arc (beat by beat)

Henry's deeper arc — *inventive but distracted; starts with tools and systems and catches the human detail late; turns shortcuts into responsibility; learns to leave a mechanism understandable to others* — plays out across his five continuity beats. He owns two plot callbacks: **`callback:wrong-charger`** (with Angel) and **`callback:useful-number-notebook`** (with Sophie and Rose).

**Beat 1 — Arrival / Ch 2 `s1e02-margin-map` (LEAD).**
Henry's dense, over-engineered kana labeling system produces a route on the atlas that only he can read. It is beautiful, it is clever, it is a private mnemonic scheme (the RTK/Uchisen instinct made physical) — and it is unreadable to the room. The class has to *edit his idea without rejecting him*, and the learner is the one who gently re-labels a point in plain kana so anyone can follow it. This is the **seed of `callback:wrong-charger`**: a tool prepared carefully except for the one connector the room actually needs — the real "wrong charger every week" gag, transposed. His late-qualifier voice is established here: he defends the system, then hears the human note late and says "…yeah, okay, make it readable."

**Beat 2 — Contribution / Ch 16 `s1e16-the-night-the-map-went-dark` (SUPPORT to Angel's lead).**
Angel's literal tool instruction erases the visible route; Henry's technically-correct instruction is part of why the display fails. This is the **echo of `callback:wrong-charger`**: the joke moves from hardware to instruction — the thing was *right* and still went dark, because he explained the mechanism past the human question (over-trusting his own output, exactly like the bill-split). The contribution that changes the scene is **teach-back**: instead of fixing it silently, Henry has to explain the cause so someone else in the class can preserve the knowledge and run it. His competence stops being private here.

**Beat 3 — Limit / Ch 26 `s3e02-caption-without-owner`.**
Rose reveals the old caption; Henry goes looking for a reliable author field and there isn't one. His whole reflex is *find the workaround* — query the metadata, add a field, build the lookup — and the story refuses him the technical shortcut. **Provenance is absence, not a puzzle to solve.** His limit is accepting that some human records don't have a clean answer key and that adding one would be inventing ownership. The builder who wants "a shortcut that isn't cheating" meets a case where the honest move is to leave the gap unfilled. No comedy callback runs during this beat (it is a quiet, uncertain admission).

**Beat 4 — Return / Ch 38 `s4e02-map-of-claims` (with Sophie).**
Henry and Sophie build an evidence map that marks confidence instead of hiding gaps — the **transform of `callback:useful-number-notebook`** (seeded Ch 14, echoed Ch 20): the notebook that once recorded a number *with context* now becomes a map with explicit confidence levels and source kinds. This is Henry's return: the systems instinct redeemed in service of honesty. His real experience distinguishing reliable from unreliable sources (jpdb data over vibes; comparing how different AI models behave) becomes a virtue — he builds the tool, but now it *shows* its uncertainty rather than papering over it. Sophie supplies calibration discipline; Henry supplies the structure.

**Beat 5 — Future / Ch 45 `s4e09-rehearsal-for-leaving`.**
The **payoff of `callback:wrong-charger`**: Henry hands another person a tested, documented setup — connector included — and asks them to run it *without him*. The man who brought the wrong charger every week now delivers a complete, legible, someone-else-can-run-it handoff. It surfaces as a practical act during the leaving-rehearsal, support shown before it is named. The quiet real undertone — building something that survives your absence, working toward a someday-elsewhere — gives the handoff its weight without any biography being stated. His arc closes exactly on the anchor: *leave a mechanism understandable to others.*

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — most important)

**Henry's learning speciality:** *making a private system legible to other people.* His lane is tool-mediated study design, mnemonics, and — crucially — **teach-back and documentation**: the language of explaining a mechanism, giving reasons, and handing over instructions someone else can follow. Grammar-obsessed by nature (は vs が, reason-giving, nominalization), he is the natural quest-giver for **reason/cause, opinion, source description, calibrated certainty, and handover register**. His signature learner weakness — strong reading, atrophied handwriting/output — makes him the character who legitimately *needs* the writing and speaking activities his scenes host.

Every beat follows the Need → Input → Lesson → Repair → Transfer → Consequence → Return pattern. Reservoirs are from `CONTENT-LINKAGE.md`.

### Beat 2 — Ch 2 `s1e02-margin-map` — reservoir `l1-l01`–`l1-l02`
- **Transfer function (canon):** introduce a person and label a useful object in kana.
- **Language functions the scene makes NECESSARY:** hiragana literacy (read/write plain kana, not a private cipher); object labeling `これは〜です`; person/self introduction `わたしは〜です` / `こちらは〜さんです`; **reason-giving with `〜から`** ("this label, because…").
- **Activity types:** **writing** (hiragana — his weak skill, so it bites), **reading** (decode a route without translation-first), **vocabulary** (useful-object labels), light **speaking** (introduce).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** Henry's over-built mnemonic labels are unreadable, so the learner *must* rewrite in legible kana and *must* give a kind reason for the edit — the scene's whole point is that editing an idea is not rejecting its maker. His real Uchisen/RTK mnemonic habit is the authentic engine: a clever private system that only works if it's translated into plain shared kana.

### Beat 3 — Ch 16 `s1e16-the-night-the-map-went-dark` — reservoir `l2-l01` shard + `l2-l02`–`l2-l04`
- **Transfer function (canon):** open the next notebook, compare then/now, and state an opinion; teach-back preserves knowledge.
- **Language functions:** **cause / reason** (`〜て` for sequence-cause, `〜から` / `〜ので`); **then/now comparison** (`前は〜、今は〜`, `〜より`); **stating an opinion** (`〜と思います`); teach-back explanation (say the mechanism so another person can act).
- **Activity types:** **grammar** (te-form cause, comparison), **speaking** (teach-back — explain so someone else runs it), **listening** (follow the cause someone else states).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** the display went dark from a *correct* instruction — Henry's real habit of over-trusting his own output (the bill-split gag, sanitized to comedy with no numbers) is the authentic reason the class needs teach-back Japanese: the fix is worthless unless it's explained in language another person can re-run.

### Beat 4 — Ch 26 `s3e02-caption-without-owner` — N3 family (Ch 25–27 required content: reported speech, nominalization, source description)
- **Transfer function (canon):** nominalization and source description; provenance is absence, not a puzzle shortcut.
- **Language functions:** **nominalization** (`〜こと` / `〜の`, `〜という`); **source / evidential description** (`〜によると`, hearsay `〜そうだ` vs `〜らしい`); **describing absence with an embedded question** (`誰が書いたか分からない`, `〜がない`).
- **Activity types:** **reading** (annotate a source), **writing** (state what the record does and does *not* support), **grammar** (nominalization + embedded questions).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** the tool-builder wants a metadata field to query and there isn't one. The Japanese he needs is the language of *saying, precisely, that the author is unknown* — nominalized, hedged, honest — instead of a language of false certainty. His find-the-workaround reflex is exactly what the grammar has to defeat.

### Beat 5 — Ch 38 `s4e02-map-of-claims` — N2 family (Ch 37–39 required content: synthesis, source quality, calibrated certainty)
- **Transfer function (canon):** synthesis, source quality, and calibrated certainty; the notebook becomes an evidence map.
- **Language functions:** **calibrated certainty / modality** (`〜と思われる`, `〜かもしれない`, `〜に違いない`, `〜はず`, `〜ようだ`, `確かに〜が、`); **synthesis connectives** (`一方で`, `それに対して`); evaluating and labeling source quality.
- **Activity types:** **reading** (multiple sources) + structured **writing** (assemble an evidence map that labels every claim's source, confidence, and hedge) + **transfer/synthesis**. The runtime does not claim speaking evidence for this chapter.
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** Henry genuinely distinguishes reliable data from noise (jpdb over vibes; probing how different AI models behave). Here that instinct meets the Japanese for *marking* confidence — the tool now says "この情報は確かではない" out loud. Real skill, honest grammar.

### Beat 6 — Ch 45 `s4e09-rehearsal-for-leaving` — N1 family (Ch 43–45 required content: plans under uncertainty, editing rationale, mastery-aware production)
- **Transfer function (canon):** mastery-aware production; support shown before it is named; hand over a tested, documented setup.
- **Language functions:** **plans under uncertainty** (`〜ておく`, `〜たら`, `〜場合は`, `〜ように`); **handover / documentation register**; **honorific request to run something** (`〜ていただけますか`); **editing rationale** (`〜のではなく〜`, `〜ように直しました`).
- **Activity types:** **writing** (documentation another person can follow — his atrophied-output skill, now mastered), **speaking** (extended spontaneous handover), **transfer**.
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** the builder of the whole ecosystem, the hoarder of laptops, finally has to write instructions so good that someone can run his setup without him. The Japanese of "I've set this up, please run it, here's what to do if X" is the language his arc has been climbing toward — and the quiet leaving-undertone makes it matter.

**Aggregate learning functions Henry's story teaches:** hiragana literacy & object labeling; reason-giving (`から`); te-form cause & sequence; then/now comparison; opinion (`と思う`); teach-back explanation; nominalization (`こと`/`の`/`という`); source & evidential description (`によると`/`そうだ`/`らしい`); embedded questions (`誰が〜か`); calibrated certainty / modality hedging; synthesis connectives; plans under uncertainty (`ておく`/`たら`/`場合`); documentation & handover register incl. honorific request.

1. **Recognition — "The unreadable elegant thing."** Ordinary task: Henry shows the learner his study setup / a mnemonic scene he's proud of. Function: **describing a system + `〜という`** ("this is a thing where…"). The learner sees the builder's delight before the builder's blind spot.

4. **Friction — "Just let me build it."** Under pressure he over-builds — proposes the biggest version, explains the mechanism past the question. The learner (or the task) refuses the escalation. Function: **softened disagreement / declining a plan** (`それはちょっと…`, `〜なくてもいいと思う`). Karaoke-veto energy, but pointed at his over-engineering, not at him.
5. **Repair — "The honest limit."** Henry documents what his tool *can't* do and says so plainly. Function: **stating limits & uncertainty** (`〜のは難しい`, `〜か分からない`, `確かではない`). He edits instead of defends.
6. **Future — "Run it without me."** He hands over a tested, documented setup and asks the learner to run it. Function: **handover + honorific request under uncertainty** (`〜ておいたので、〜ていただけますか`). Mirrors and reinforces the Ch 45 wrong-charger payoff without resolving it off-screen.
