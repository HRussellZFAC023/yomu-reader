## 1. Who they really are

Onke-san is the person who turns "I don't know" into a document. In the real class she
is the organiser — the one with a colour-coded spreadsheet for everything, including the
trip nobody has agreed to yet. She pinned the meeting times, she ran the logistics for the
surprise send-off party, and she is the reason a running joke ("we should go to Japan
someday") slowly, quietly became a tab with dates in it. She is generous with that
competence: the plan is never a power move, it is a gift she hands the room so nobody else
has to carry the uncertainty.

Her signature real line is the whole character in eight syllables: **「だいじょうぶ、リストにしたよ」**
("It's fine — I've put it on a list"). A spreadsheet appears *before* any decision is made.
The class laughs, and then quietly uses the spreadsheet, because it is always right.

This slots exactly onto the canon voice card — *"converts uncertainty into a plan, table,
or tool"* — and the paired arc, *"learns to expose assumptions and trust an ensemble after
the master plan breaks."* The real texture deepens the anchor without bending it: her plans
are colour-coded (a **shared** aesthetic with Sophie's colour-coded grammar notes, but a
different job — Sophie codes *confidence*, Onke-san codes *state*: done / doing / to-do).

Interests that drive her scenes: **planning, technology, and writing** — spreadsheets,
lists, trip logistics, and the small tools that track them. Her Japanese has a lane nobody
else in the class owns: the **ready-vs-to-do aspect system** — 〜てある (it is *ready*,
someone prepared it and the result stands) versus 〜ておく (I will *do it in advance*,
preparatory). Her real corpus line `ext-angel-ready-list` (N4) is literally two of these
side by side: **「予定表はもう作ってあります」** ("the schedule's already made and stands") /
**「地図は送ってあるけど、時間はまだ。」** ("the map's been sent, but the time — not yet"). That
half-finished second clause is her in one breath: meticulous, honest about the gap, already
tracking it.

**Voice contrast to protect (VOICE-AND-DIALOGUE):** with Henry, *Henry reaches for the
mechanism; Onke-san reaches for the plan around it.* Henry qualifies late, after a technical
claim; she **front-loads structure, then discovers an assumption.** They are the two
systems-minded people in the room, which is why they co-own the projection chain that makes
the atlas *look* responsive in the finale (STORY-BIBLE §The ending, fact 4) — and why the
"one unknown word" study-deck seed is a Henry/Tom/Onke-san idea, three tool-brains agreeing.

**Pressure behaviour (fixed):** under stress she *strengthens the spreadsheet when the
premise is wrong* — she optimises the plan instead of questioning it. **Growth is exposing
the assumption before rebuilding the plan.** Every friction beat she appears in is a
variation on this single failure mode. It is never cured into someone spontaneous; she
stays a planner, she just learns to audit the premise first.

## 2. Four-season arc (beat-by-beat across her 5 continuity beats)

Her deeper arc — *a plan is only as good as its unexamined assumption; and a good plan can
be handed to someone else* — plays out as a slow transfer of ownership: from "my master
plan holds the room together" to "here is a tested, documented setup — you run it without me."

### Beat 1 — Arrival · Ch 16 · `s1e16-the-night-the-map-went-dark` (LEAD)
Her literal, technically-correct tool instruction erases the visible route. The class had
the map on screen; Onke-san gave the exact right command — for the wrong assumption about
what state the display was in — and it went dark. This is the **echo** of
`callback:wrong-charger` (seeded Ch 2 with Henry: a tool prepared carefully except for the
one connector the room needs). The joke lands, but the recovery is the point: the class
**preserves the knowledge through teach-back** rather than waiting for her to fix it alone.
Her attention detail seeds here — the colour-coded ready/to-do list — and the room learns
that her competence can produce the failure *and* the framework to recover from it.

### Beat 2 — Contribution · Ch 18 · `s1e18-the-memory-card-museum` (support to Stasi's lead)
Stasi's funniest mnemonic proves false; the chapter is about kanji evidence and revision.
Onke-san's **contribution** is structural: she is the one who turns the museum of false
memories into a checkable list — a wanted-feature spec, a button condition, a changed plan
(the ch18 reservoir functions). She converts Stasi's vivid wrong memory into something the
class can *test* without flattening the expression. Her running "list-for-everything" gag
is played warmly here: the list is what lets a false memory be revised instead of defended.

### Beat 3 — Limit · Ch 30 · `s3e06-two-schedules` (co-lead with Alex)
The **transform** of `callback:overbuilt-schedule` (seeded Ch 6 with Sam; echoed Ch 13 via
Robert's dinner sequence). Alex and Onke-san hold **conflicting but defensible** versions of
the event plan. Her limit: she wants to win by having the *better spreadsheet*. The chapter
refuses to make either document a moral verdict — the resolution is not "whose plan is
right" but **stating assumptions and naming decision ownership out loud.** This is her arc's
hinge: she can no longer strengthen the table to win; she has to expose the premise. It is
also where "the trip nobody agreed to yet" stops being a joke and becomes a real,
contested, human negotiation.

### Beat 4 — Return · Ch 32 · `s3e08-right-screen-wrong-draft` (LEAD)
The **transform** of `callback:wrong-charger`: the right screen loads the *wrong draft* — an
obsolete internal version containing withdrawn material. It never leaves the room. The joke
has moved from hardware (Ch 2/16: the connector, the command) to **version responsibility**
(the draft, the state). This is Onke-san's return and her mastery moment on her own terms:
she is the one who catches it, because ready-vs-to-do (〜てある/〜ておく) is *her grammar* —
she knows the difference between "this is prepared and stands" and "this was superseded."
Immediate repair, apology, procedural Japanese; urgency does not override consent. She has
finally learned to audit the premise (which version is live?) before acting on the plan.

### Beat 5 — Future · Ch 45 · `s4e09-rehearsal-for-leaving`
The **payoff** of `callback:wrong-charger`: Henry hands another person a tested, documented
setup and asks them to run it **without him** — and the person who makes that handoff
*legible* is Onke-san. Her whole arc was "the plan holds the room together"; her future is
"the plan can be handed on and still work when I'm not there." Relationship callbacks surface
as practical acts, not speeches. She rehearses leaving by making herself unnecessary — the
most generous thing a planner can do. Consent note honoured: *plans expose assumptions and
can be transferred to others.*

## 3. The Japanese her story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — the point)

**Learning speciality: the aspect / state system — READY vs TO-DO vs ACCIDENTALLY-DONE.**
No other cast member owns this. Onke-san is the class's native teacher of *how Japanese marks
the state of a plan*: prepared-and-standing, done-in-advance, spontaneously-changed,
regrettably-completed, and self-vs-other causation. Her real spreadsheet habit is the
authentic reason the learner *needs* these forms — you cannot keep a colour-coded ready/to-do
list in Japanese without them.

For each beat, the exact language function the scene makes the learner NEED, the activity
type, and the real-texture reason it is unavoidable. Reservoir references per
CONTENT-LINKAGE.

### Ch 16 (LEAD) — reservoir: `l2-l01` support shard + `l2-l02`–`l2-l04`
Transfer function (linkage): *open the next notebook, compare then/now, state an opinion; no
completion gate on the support shard.*
- **NEED:** describe *what changed* when the map went dark — the difference between "the map
  disappeared" (自動詞 消えた, spontaneous) and "the instruction erased it" (他動詞 消した,
  caused), plus "I've gone and erased it" (〜てしまいました, accidental completion), and the
  then/now compare (前は見えていた / 今は真っ暗). Then state an opinion about the cause
  (〜と思います).
- **Activity types:** grammar (transitive/intransitive pairs; 〜てしまう) → speaking/listening
  (teach-back: one learner explains the cause, another repeats it accurately) → transfer
  (the source display is gone; the learner must reconstruct the route from spoken memory).
- **Why the real texture forces it:** her literal-instruction failure is *only* a story if the
  learner can articulate cause vs accident. "It broke" is not enough; the class survives by
  saying **precisely** who/what did what. Teach-back is the mechanic and the theme.

### Ch 18 — reservoir: `l2-l09`–`l2-l11`
Transfer function (linkage): *describe a wanted program, button condition, and changed plan.*
- **NEED:** 〜てほしい / 〜たい (the feature the class *wants* from a checklist), the button/
  conditional 〜と ("push this and it clears") — the exact grammar that caused Ch 16's dark
  map, now used deliberately — and 〜ことになりました / 〜ことにしました (the plan *changed*).
- **Activity types:** grammar (conditional 〜と; volitional-desire 〜てほしい) → writing (spec the
  wanted checklist behaviour) → reading (parse a changed-plan notice).
- **Why the real texture forces it:** turning Stasi's false mnemonic into a *checkable* item is
  literally writing a condition ("if this, then that") and a wanted outcome. Her list gag is
  the pedagogy: a list only works if each row is a testable condition, not a vibe.

### Ch 30 (co-lead) — required family: N3 conditions, refusal, role negotiation, conflicting plans
Four-skill proof (linkage): *hear a softened condition; read two schedules; decline or
renegotiate orally; write a clarified plan.*
- **NEED:** sequence and evidence language — 〜てから / 〜前に / 〜後で (order), reported speech
  and hearsay 〜そうです / 〜と言っていました (whose account is this?), softened refusal /
  renegotiation 〜んですが…、ちょっと…、〜ませんか. The learner must state an **assumption**
  aloud, not just a preference.
- **Activity types:** reading (two schedules side by side) → listening (a softened condition)
  → speaking (decline / renegotiate without making the other plan "wrong") → writing (a
  clarified plan that names its assumptions).
- **Why the real texture forces it:** "the trip nobody agreed to yet" finally collides with a
  second defensible plan (Alex's). The learner cannot resolve it by picking the prettier
  spreadsheet; they must *surface the hidden assumption in Japanese* — which is exactly
  Onke-san's growth edge made into a task.

### Ch 32 (LEAD) — required family: N2 quotation, translation choice, evidentiality, hedging
Four-skill proof (linkage): *annotate a source; justify a choice; distinguish observation/
hearsay/inference; write calibrated claims.* Plus procedural repair + apology.
- **NEED:** her home grammar under pressure — 〜てある (this version is *prepared and stands*)
  vs a superseded draft; evidential/hedging 〜はずです / 〜ようです / 〜かもしれません ("this
  *should* be the live one"); procedural keigo repair 申し訳ありません・ただ今差し替えます・
  〜てしまいました.
- **Activity types:** reading/annotation (mark which draft is live and how you know) → writing
  (a calibrated claim: what is confirmed vs assumed) → speaking (immediate apology + repair
  procedure) → transfer (fix state without exposing the withdrawn material — consent over
  urgency).
- **Why the real texture forces it:** only a ready-vs-to-do specialist catches a wrong-draft
  load. The scene *is* 〜てある/〜ておく under stakes: is this file done-and-standing, or was it
  meant to be replaced? Her spreadsheet brain becomes the class's safety check.

### Ch 45 — required family: N1 plans under uncertainty, editing rationale, mastery-aware production
Four-skill proof (linkage): *compare non-equivalent futures; revise for public use; perform an
extended spontaneous response.*
- **NEED:** 〜予定 / 〜つもり / 〜ことになっている (plans held with acknowledged uncertainty),
  nominalisation and conditional 〜ば/〜たら for a documented handoff, and extended spontaneous
  explanation ("run it like this, and if X, then Y").
- **Activity types:** writing (a runbook another person can execute) → speaking (hand it over
  live, spontaneously, answering questions) → transfer (the author steps away; the plan must
  survive without her).
- **Why the real texture forces it:** her generosity was always the plan; her mastery is making
  the plan *transferable*. The learner produces the documented, tested setup Henry hands off —
  the literal payoff of the four-year charger/version callback, in fluent procedural Japanese.

**learningFunctions (return object):** transitive/intransitive verb pairs (自動詞・他動詞);
〜てしまう accidental completion; then/now comparison + 〜と思います opinion; 〜てある resultant
state vs 〜ておく preparatory (ready-vs-to-do); 〜てほしい/〜たい wanted feature; conditional
〜と/〜たら (button condition); 〜ことになる/〜ことにする changed plan; sequence 〜てから/前に/後で;
reported speech & hearsay 〜そうです/〜と言っていました; softened refusal & renegotiation
(〜んですが/ちょっと/〜ませんか); evidential hedging 〜はず/〜よう/〜かもしれません; procedural
apology keigo (申し訳ありません/差し替え); plans-under-uncertainty 〜予定/〜つもり/〜ことになっている.

## 4. Elective bond route (PROPOSED — requires `bond-authored` manifest + consent)

Not yet authorised. Sketched here because her interests make an unusually clean route: every
appointment is a real planning artefact. Follows the required Recognition → Friction → Support
spine, defer-friendly, no affection score, friction/repair shown as **changed work**. Place:
a café corner she has colonised with a laptop (grounded `cafe`).

1. **Recognition (ordinary task).** She asks the learner to help fill a study-plan tab —
   which lessons are *done* vs *to-do*. *Function:* 〜てある/〜ておく sorting; simple
   〜ました/まだ. The learner learns her ready/to-do distinction by using it.
2. **Different route.** The learner proposes doing it *without* the spreadsheet — just talking
   through the week. She's uneasy but tries it. *Function:* time expressions + frequency
   (毎週/〜に〜回), spoken planning instead of written.
3. **Reciprocity.** She builds the learner a personalised colour-coded review list as a gift
   and explains her colour system. *Function:* giving/receiving (〜てあげる/〜てくれる),
   reason-giving (〜から/〜ので).
4. **Friction (transforms a seeded detail).** A plan she made breaks on an assumption she
   never checked — the learner spotted it and she optimised the plan instead of listening.
   Her pressure mode fires. *Function:* softened disagreement + stating an assumption aloud
   (〜と思っていたんですが…、実は…).
5. **Repair.** She reopens the plan, exposes the premise first, and rebuilds it *with* the
   learner's input rather than around it. *Function:* revising conditions (〜たら/〜なら),
   acknowledging (なるほど/確かに), calibrated commitment.
6. **Future.** She hands the learner a finished, documented tracker and asks them to run their
   own study without her — one voluntary next appointment set. *Function:* handoff /
   instructions (〜ておいてください), plans with uncertainty (〜予定/〜つもり).
