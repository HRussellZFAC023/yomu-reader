# Staying with it

Missing two weeks changes one thing: today has cards in it.

One learner in the community lost a 1,480-day streak and celebrated the work it recorded. That is the useful meaning of a streak. It tells you that you returned many times. It is not a debt contract with yesterday.

## Start with ten

When 400 cards are waiting, do ten. Stop if ten is what fits. Come back tomorrow.

Do not reset a deck because the number looks ugly. Do not grade everything Easy to clear the screen. The schedule can recover if the grades stay honest.

Failed words return sooner. Known words move away. Each real answer gives the scheduler information, even when the queue is still large.

## Lower the daily number

A daily target should leave room to read. If reviews take all the time you meant to spend with Japanese, mine fewer words and lower the target.

Use Fail and Pass mode when five grades turn into hesitation. Hide review steps that do not help your current goal. Pick one source instead of mixing every connected queue.

There is no daily cap in the local deck. That means you may do as much as you like. It also means the number is not a command.

## Keep an effort record

Stats can show reviews, accuracy, time and the days you returned. Use it to notice patterns. A week of short sessions may be healthier than one heroic Sunday.

The best recovery plan is deliberately boring:

1. Review a small number.
2. Read or watch something easy.
3. Save almost nothing.
4. Repeat tomorrow.

If the system makes you avoid Japanese, change the system. The method belongs to you.
