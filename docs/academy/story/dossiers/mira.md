## 1. Who they really are

Mira is the friend who arrives through the phone. She is US-based (**Miami**), a Japanese-learning friend Henry knows from an online circle rather than the UCL evening class, and she re-enters the group's life the way distant friends actually do — a message lands in the group chat one evening, warm and unannounced, and suddenly she is *present* again as if no weeks had passed. Her one in-person anchor is a **visit** that lines up with the group's RHS-garden day out, so she is mostly a voice, a typing indicator, and — when she does show up — the person who slots into the plan without any drama about having been away.

She is a **self-directed, modest-habit learner**: roughly **30–60 minutes a day**, meetup-driven, and devoted to **game-based / vocabulary-photography learning** — she photographs the world to feed her own word list. That habit gives her a very specific superpower and a very specific complaint, which are the same thing: she plays a study game all the way to the bottom, hits the point where it stops teaching her new words, and asks — cheerfully, not bitterly — **"okay, so where's the rest of Japanese hiding?"** She is the person who notices the *ceiling* of a tool the moment the vocabulary runs out.

Her voice, reconciled with the canon anchor (`CAST-AND-CONSENT` line 90 and the `VOICE-AND-DIALOGUE` contrast row): **friendly, direct, practical, and playful about study tools.** In the paired contrast with Nanako, *both make entry easy* — but where Nanako tunes pragmatic warmth and closes exchanges cleanly, **Mira tests whether a plan or a tool actually works in ordinary use, and she is comfortable restarting small.** She mixes beginner Japanese with casual English and is unembarrassed about the beginner part. Two real details deepen the anchor rather than fight it:

- **She joins late and contributes the first-event solution.** Latecomer + solver is her signature shape. The group's whole comedic engine is lateness; Mira takes the group trope and inverts it into competence — she is late *and* she is the one who sees the fix everyone technical-brained their way past.
- **She models returning without guilt.** When she comes back after an absence she does not perform an apology. She just picks the thread back up and — crucially — **invites another learner to restart too.** She is living proof that a lapsed streak is not a moral failure.

She is generous in the group's found-family way: she welcomed **Nanako** into the conversation, and the **camera-quest / photo-vocabulary crew (Tom, Aakash, Henry)** welcomed her. Low-drama, teasing, kind. She belongs to Henry's orbit the way Nanako does — real people rendered as warm fiction, never gossip.

**Do NOT contradict the anchor:** she is not a classroom lesson-lead, not a majime-crew regular, not in the Thursday room grinding chapters. Her competence is *lived-in self-study*, not curriculum rank.

## 2. Four-season arc — her five continuity beats

Her beats fire through **Henry's orbit, the group chat, and visits/reunions** — never the classroom curriculum. Her whole arc rhymes: *a distant friend keeps proving that showing up late, or restarting small, still changes the outcome.*

### Beat 1 — **Arrival + Lead, Ch 24** · `s1e24-lanterns-return` — "When the Lanterns Return"
Her introduction is her lead. The Season-2 exhibition finale is stalling: the rebuilt route works technically, but it stays *dead* — everyone keeps reaching for another patch. Mira, joining the group chat **from a distance** (Miami, hours off, half in English), watches the thread and says the thing nobody in the fix-it huddle can see: **the final route doesn't need another technical fix — it needs live narration.** People coordinating out loud, not a better object. She joins late and contributes the first-event solution. This is her half of the ensemble **`callback:first-lantern` transform** (owned by Rie + ensemble): the map becomes responsive *because people coordinate, not because the object is magical.* Learning frame: **integrated N4 production; joining late can still change the outcome.**

### Beat 2 — **Contribution, Ch 28** · `s3e04-terms-of-invitation`
The public Japanese-evening venue asks for a public version *before every contributor has agreed.* Sam leads the consent language; Mira contributes **from the group chat**, in her practical register: she stress-tests the *plan* the way she stress-tests a study app — "does this actually work in ordinary use, or does it just look finished?" She is the one who asks whether the shortcut has a hidden ceiling. Her practicality serves consent here instead of tooling. **Conditions, scope, formal requests, respectful refusal.**

### Beat 3 — **Limit, Ch 32** · `s3e08-right-screen-wrong-draft`
Rehearsal loads an obsolete internal draft containing *withdrawn* material; it must be repaired in the room and never leave it. This is Mira's **corrected assumption** — the inversion of her own lead-chapter lesson. Her instinct is the technical fix (version control, "just swap the file"). The correction: **some things are not a version problem, they are a consent problem, and urgency does not override consent.** The fixer learns her fix-it reflex must yield. (This limit sets up her support appearance the following chapter, **ch 33 `s3e09-what-we-can-say`**, where Jodi, Nanako, and Mira separate memory, permission, and public claim.) **Immediate repair, apology, procedural Japanese; evidential/hedged language.**

### Beat 4 — **Return, Ch 43** (Season 4, N1)
She comes back after another absence — **and there is no guilt.** No apology theatre; she picks the thread up and, in character, **invites someone else who lapsed to restart too.** The ensemble proves it heard her Ch 32 limit through changed behavior: nobody rushes her, and the group treats her restart as normal, not as a debt. Her much-higher language now lets her help the group **compare non-equivalent futures** — whose "someday in Japan," whose plan, held side by side without ranking them. **N1 plans under uncertainty; returning-without-guilt, now fluent.**

### Beat 5 — **Future, Ch 48** (graduation)
Her future leaves **one open possibility** without predicting the real person's life — a next meetup, a maybe-Japan reunion, the modest daily habit simply *continuing*. She is one of the selected spoken callbacks in the controlled graduation montage; the rest is recorded in the journal, not a roll call. This is where **`callback:first-lantern` pays off**: the ordinary mechanism is named, the atlas closes, and a **non-lit blank page remains valid** — the friend who arrived from a distance leaves the door open the same way she entered.
