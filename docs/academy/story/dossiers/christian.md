## 1. Who they really are

Christian is the person who has already **done the thing** before anyone finishes deciding whether to do it. When the class chat lights up with "which room are we in?", Christian is the one who has the number, the Moodle homework-folder link, and — inexplicably — a plan. He joined the class the least glamorous way (a link someone forwarded) and quietly grew into the group's logistics spine: room numbers, official emails pasted whole, the browser-only trick for reading Google Maps in Japanese that he shared like it was nothing. He belongs to the homework-and-Moodle helper cluster with Sophie and Aakash; where Sophie *calibrates* and Aakash *invites*, Christian **executes**.

The engine underneath is real and physical. He likes the gym and will describe his routine hour by hour if you leave the door open even a crack — "I like sport" is less a hobby statement than a worldview. Movement, health, and routines are how he thinks: a problem is a sequence of steps you perform in order, and if the steps are good the problem dissolves. This is his gift and his flaw in one. Give him a too-hot classroom and he will not open a window; he will produce his electric desk fan, and then a *system* — angled airflow, a duty rotation, a diagram — until the fix is three times larger than the heat. His canonical arc is exactly this correction: **learning to guide without turning every shared problem into his personal engineering project.** He solves *for* people; the growth is solving *with* them, and letting a solution be declined or handed off.

He is also, warmly, the delightfully odd one, and the group loves him for it. His phone has a mind of its own — it pocket-dials the group, leaves butt-voicemails ("my phone accidentially redialled in my pouch"), and produces the occasional mystery "????" call that nobody explains and everybody forgives. And once a chapter, without fail, **Christian produces the recorder.** It is never explained. It is not a plot device. He simply has a recorder, and at some point it is in his hand, and the class has collectively agreed never to ask. His steadiness and his hapless-gadget comedy are the same trait viewed from two angles: a man who trusts objects and routines to carry him, and is entirely unbothered when they misfire.

He is a volunteer, a reliable body in the class photo, someone who once missed a session for a family emergency and came back without turning it into a story — he just picked up the next task. That is the whole of him: shows up, acts, keeps the room running, hands you the link before you knew you needed it, and has a fan and a recorder and a gym schedule and absolutely no interest in being asked about any of it.

**Reconciliation with the anchor.** The three source strands agree more than they look. The "steady, slightly formal logistics guy" (chat) and the "energetic, action-first" tester (codex) are one person: formal because he speaks in *procedures*, energetic because he'd rather do than debate. The "delightfully odd" recorder-and-fan comedy (dossiers) is the same reliability turned up one notch past useful. Nothing here contradicts the four-season arc — the over-engineered hot-room solution *is* his canonical callback home, and "guide without taking over" is literally his chapter-10 lesson.

## 2. Four-season arc (his five continuity beats)

His durable relationship callback family is his own: **the elaborate hot-room solution** (Arrival seed) → transformed at his Limit → paid off through changed behavior at his Future. The recorder and the pocket-dialling ride alongside as comedy, suspended whenever a real boundary is in the room (per ledger rule).

### Beat 1 — Arrival · Chapter 10 · `s1e10-instructions-for-a-cloud` (LEAD)
The class is building a **weather-lantern** for the atlas — a lantern whose display is supposed to follow a written sequence of instructions ("do this, then this, then this"). Christian writes the instructions. They are thorough, imperative, and *his*. Then the wind — or the temperamental projection chain — takes the cards and blows them out of order, and the cloud does everything at once. His flawless procedure fails not because a step was wrong but because he owned every step and left no room for the sequence to be repaired by anyone but him.
**Seed planted:** the too-hot-room instinct in miniature — Christian over-specifies, the system out-runs the need. This is the seed of `callback:hot-room-solution` (his relationship callback) and establishes his attention pattern: *he enters a task by writing the steps.*
**Real texture in the scene:** the fan is on the desk; the recorder appears once and is not explained; he already had the room's homework link open in another tab. His energy is warm, not bossy — the failure is affectionate, and he laughs at it.

### Beat 2 — Contribution · Chapter 16 · `s1e16-the-night-the-map-went-dark`
Angel's literal, technically-correct tool instruction blanks the whole route, and the class must **preserve the knowledge through teach-back** rather than one person re-fixing it. This is Christian's contribution beat *precisely because he doesn't rush the fix.* His sequencing instinct — the thing that overshot in Ch10 — is now the right tool: he breaks the recovery into a **te-form ordered procedure the whole room can perform**, and instead of executing it himself he narrates the steps so others do them. His competence (turning a mess into an ordered sequence) changes the shared work; the growth is that the sequence lives in the class's hands, not his. The fan and recorder are present as running comedy; the phone pocket-dials at the worst possible moment and the room laughs.

### Beat 3 — Limit · Chapter 30 · `s3e06-two-schedules` (`callback:overbuilt-schedule` pressure point)
Alex and Angel hold two conflicting-but-defensible versions of the event plan. Christian's instinct screams *build a third, better master-schedule and route around the whole disagreement* — the hot-room reflex at full size. **His limit is that he cannot engineer his way out of a human disagreement.** The transform of his callback happens here: the elaborate over-solution meets a problem that is not a heat problem, and building a bigger apparatus would erase two people's legitimate positions. His consent-note — *practical action can be declined or handed off* — becomes the lesson. He offers his schedule; someone declines it; he has to sit in the ambiguity instead of laminating over it. The comedy is suspended the moment the disagreement turns real.

### Beat 4 — Return · Chapter 34 · `s3e10-empty-microphone`
A host drops out; public speaking roles must be **redistributed without coercion**, and quiet roles must count as full participation. Christian returns changed: the man who used to hand you a finished procedure now **negotiates a role and takes a backstage one on purpose.** He runs the cues, keeps the sequence, and — canonically — he and **Jenny anchor the classroom-incident report (C5.5)** here: the two practical-problem-solvers, his outward action and her private precision, co-authoring the record of what went wrong without blaming anyone. The recorder finally has a *reason* to exist in-frame (someone needs the audio cue captured) and the class notices and says nothing, which is the joke's gentlest payoff. His changed behavior: he guides by holding the sequence for others rather than performing it.

### Beat 5 — Future · Chapter 45 · `s4e09-rehearsal-for-leaving`
Relationship callbacks surface as **practical acts** while the class rehearses both the event and the goodbye. Christian's future is open and quiet: he doesn't announce a plan, he *does* one last generous, well-sequenced thing and lets it be run by someone else. The hot-room callback pays off — faced with a small logistics snag, he offers a tested, documented, hand-off-able procedure and then **steps back and lets another person own it**, the exact inverse of the Ch10 lantern he couldn't let go of. His fan gets left for the next class. The recorder is produced one final time; nobody asks; he smiles. Graduation acknowledges him without freezing him into a finished life — the volunteer who kept the room running, learning at last to keep it running *without his hands on every step.*

## 3. The Japanese his story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — most important)

**Christian's learning speciality:** **procedural, sequenced, low-blame language** — instructions, ordered actions, cause, and *visible states without accusation*. His whole voice is imperative sequences (Jenny's contrast is economical observation), so he is the class's natural home for **te-form as the connective tissue of doing things in order**, and for the grammar that lets you describe what happened to a shared object without blaming a person. His signature real habit — *visible states, no blame, てしまう* — is a pedagogical gift: 「録音機も壊れてしまいました」 folds the recorder joke and the grammar point into a single breath (`ext-christian-incident-desk`, N4). He mostly code-switches to English for pure logistics (pasting the official email, the Moodle link, the room number), which models the authentic learner behavior of *doing real business in Japanese only where the Japanese is ready.*

### Beat-by-beat learning integration

**Chapter 10 — LEAD — reservoir `l1-l15` ("coordinate a small gathering through sequenced action")**
- **Language function the scene makes the learner NEED:** te-form action sequencing (…て、…て…) and the instruction/request form …てください; giving a **reason/cause** for a step (…から / …ので); the "shall we / let's" coordination move (…ましょうか／…ましょう).
- **Activity type:** grammar + speaking + a light **writing** task (write the ordered instruction cards) that becomes a **transfer** activity when the cards blow out of order and must be re-sequenced live.
- **Why the real texture makes the Japanese necessary:** Christian *writes procedures* — it is who he is — so the learner isn't drilling te-form abstractly; they are helping a real classmate fix a lantern by putting his steps back in order. When the cloud does everything at once, the learner physically *needs* 「まず…て、つぎに…てください」 to repair the sequence. The failure of a too-controlled procedure teaches the theme (instructions guide without taking over) and the grammar in the same motion.

**Chapter 16 — Contribution — reservoir `l2-l01` shard + `l2-l02`–`l2-l04`**
- **Language function needed:** **ています** for ongoing/resultant visible states ("the route is deleted / it's showing nothing"), described **without blame**; compare then/now; state an opinion about what to do next; the folding-in of **てしまう** for "it's gone and done" (his signature).
- **Activity type:** listening + speaking **teach-back** — the learner receives Christian's ordered recovery steps by ear and performs them, then reports the resulting state (…ています) back to the room.
- **Why the real texture works:** the whole point of the beat is that no single person re-fixes the blank map; Christian's sequencing lives in everyone's hands. The learner *needs* the "no-blame visible-state" register because Angel's instruction caused the failure and the class refuses to weaponize it — 「消えてしまっています」 describes the state, not the culprit. His habit is the pedagogy.

**Chapter 30 — Limit — reservoir 28–30 (N3 conditions, refusal, role negotiation, conflicting plans)**
- **Language function needed:** conditional planning (…たら／…ば／…なら), **softened refusal**, and renegotiating a plan orally when two versions conflict; the language of *offering* an action and having it **declined** ("if we did X…" / "I could, but…").
- **Activity type:** speaking/listening negotiation transfer — hear two defensible schedules, decline or renegotiate without a winner, write one clarified plan.
- **Why the real texture works:** Christian's reflex is to build a master-schedule and route around the disagreement — his over-engineering instinct. The scene forces the exact Japanese he'd rather skip: not *commanding* a solution but *conditionally offering* one and accepting a "no." His consent-note (practical action can be **declined or handed off**) is the grammar target — the learner practices hedged offers precisely because Christian must learn to make them.

**Chapter 34 — Return — reservoir 34–36 (N2 formal event language, source comparison, permission scope)**
- **Language function needed:** **role negotiation** and spontaneous repair in a semi-formal register (offering/accepting a backstage job, keigo-lite); co-authoring an accurate **incident report** — factual sequencing, no-blame state description at N2 (with Jenny, C5.5).
- **Activity type:** speaking (role negotiation) + **writing** (the incident report) + listening (cue-running). The recorder's captured audio becomes a legitimate **source-comparison** artifact.
- **Why the real texture works:** the man who used to hand you a finished procedure now *takes the quiet role and holds the sequence for others.* Writing the incident report with Jenny is his sequencing skill turned ethical — order the events, describe the states, assign no blame. The recorder finally has a reason to exist, so the running joke becomes a real evidence source: comedy transformed into learning material.

**Chapter 45 — Future — reservoir 43–45 (N1 plans under uncertainty, editing rationale, mastery-aware production)**
- **Language function needed:** talking about **plans under genuine uncertainty** (non-committal futures, …つもり／…ことになる／…かもしれない with hedging), giving the **rationale** for an editing/hand-off choice, and extended spontaneous production that doesn't over-claim.
- **Activity type:** speaking (mastery-aware, spontaneous) + a small **transfer/writing** artifact — the documented, hand-off-able procedure he leaves behind.
- **Why the real texture works:** his payoff is doing one last well-sequenced generous thing and *letting someone else run it.* The N1 target is exactly that restraint in language: describe a plan, hand it off, explain *why* you're not doing it yourself — the inverse of the Ch10 lantern he clutched. The fan he leaves for the next class is the physical version of the sentence.

**Reservoir tie-back:** Ch10 → `l1-l15`; Ch16 → `l2-l01`+`l2-l02–04`; Ch30/34/45 → the required N3/N2/N1 content contracts (28–30, 34–36, 43–45). No listening beat is gated on unheld audio; the recorder's "audio" is diegetic and only becomes a graded source once real audio rights exist.

## 4. Elective bond route (proposal — requires `bond-authored` manifest entry before it can issue invitations)

Platonic, task-named, deferrable. Home base: the **gym / the courtyard bench after a workout**, with the classroom as fallback. His real interests — fitness, routines, practical gadgets — drive every appointment; each carries a language function.

1. **Ordinary task — "The routine, hour by hour."** Christian walks the learner through his gym routine as a *sequence*. Learner describes their own daily routine back. **Language:** daily-schedule + time expressions + te-form sequencing (…てから…). *Reusable state:* the learner's stated routine becomes callback-able.
2. **Different route — "His way vs. yours."** Christian solves a small shared problem (a broken locker, a schedule clash) the Christian way — build a procedure. The learner sees his method differs from theirs. **Language:** instructions/…てください and comparative "you could also…" (…もいいですよ). *New support style:* "Christian's ordered checklist" becomes available.
3. **Reciprocity — "He remembered."** He recalls a bounded thing the learner said earlier (a workout they mentioned, a place they walk) and builds it into a plan. **Language:** reported/remembered speech (…と言っていましたね) + suggestion. *Callback:* one learner detail enters the shared voice set.
4. **Friction — "Just open the window."** The too-hot-room problem, one-on-one: Christian starts engineering the elaborate fix; the learner (or the moment) offers the two-second answer. Neither is framed as the flattering choice — his system is genuinely thorough; the simple fix is genuinely enough. **Language:** softened disagreement + conditional (…たら) + declining an offer kindly. *Occurs only after his Ch30 Limit.*
5. **Repair — "Hand it off."** He acts differently: presents a fix, then *asks the learner to run it* instead of running it himself. **Language:** requesting-that-someone-do (…てもらえますか) + explaining a hand-off rationale. *Trust shown as changed work, no confession speech.*
6. **Future — "Train together, sometime."** He proposes one voluntary next thing beyond class — a standing gym/walk appointment — stated as an easy-to-decline open plan. **Language:** non-committal future + invitation with a clear opt-out (…ませんか、無理なら大丈夫). *Season 4 only; graduation/alumni wording can acknowledge the route.*

Consent: every invitation names the activity and an easy decline; one clear "no" ends it and the plan changes rather than the argument sharpening. The recorder may appear as texture but is never the *reason* for a scene.
