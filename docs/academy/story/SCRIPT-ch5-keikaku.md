# Chapter 5 - 「けいかく」 Open Rooms Rehearsal

**Core question:** Can the Desk make a plan that still works when a guest has different food needs, cannot find the place, or the weather changes?
**Story change:** Mika stops trying to foresee every possibility alone; the group uses a controlled rehearsal scenario to test whether their planned welcome is genuinely usable.
**Language family:** `なら`, `ありませんか`, `ように/ないように`, listening for plan details, adapted dialogue, planning writing, Kanji 7, reflection.
**Runtime status:** **fully mapped to the current content graph** under `unit-level-3-plus-lesson-09`. Activity labels and outcomes below must stay aligned to `src/academy/content.ts`.

## S5.1 - The Rehearsal Brief

**Location:** Open Door Desk, planning table.
**Narrative job:** Establish why the current lesson's shared-weekend-plan scenario is in the story without pretending it is the Open Rooms event itself.
**Authentic language need:** Test whether a small plan carries enough detail to transfer to a bigger welcome plan.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-listen-weekend-plan` - `outcome-listen-for-gist`, `outcome-listen-for-detail`.
**Intent ID:** `plan.test-small-scenario-before-event`

_Mika has put the Blue Door Folio beside a one-page rehearsal brief. The brief is a fictional training scenario: two people arranging a Sunday shared meal near a river, with a station meeting point and a rain alternative. It is deliberately smaller than Open Rooms Afternoon._

**MIKA:** `大きい計画の前に、小さい計画を試したいです。`
_Before the large plan, I want to test a small one._

**SUZU:** `同じ言葉が使えますか。`
_Can we use the same language?_

**RIE:** `使えます。相手、場所、目的、もしもの時。計画の大きさが変わっても、必要なことは似ています。`
_We can. The people, place, purpose, and “what if.” The size of the plan changes, but what it needs is similar._

**Plain-language activity reason:** _Listen once for the overall plan, then again for its time, food, support action, and rain fallback. You are checking whether a reader can act without guessing._

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5 support                        | Bridge                                             | N4 target                                                    |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `日曜日に、いっしょに 食べます。` | `日曜日に、駅で 会ってから、いっしょに 食べます。` | `日曜日に川の近くで昼ごはんを食べるなら、駅で会いませんか。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _The team chose a small weekend-plan rehearsal to test the language Open Rooms will need later._
**Handoff:** `activity-listen-weekend-plan`.

## S5.2 - Hear the Plan Before You See It

**Location:** Planning table, transcript closed.
**Narrative job:** Give Mika evidence that a clear plan can be understood before it is perfected.
**Authentic language need:** Identify the gist, actions, quantities, and contingency in a short conversation.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-listen-weekend-plan` - current transcript asset is optional after first attempt.
**Intent ID:** `plan.listen-for-actionable-detail`

_The group listens to the rehearsal conversation. The Desk’s working rule is on a small card: “first understand the action; then inspect the wording.” No one is asked to play audio, and the full text route is equivalent._

**MIKA:** `最初は、全部 分からなくても いいですか。`
_Is it alright if I do not understand everything at first?_

**RIE:** `はい。まず、何を決めているか。次に、何を持っていくか。最後に、雨ならどうするかです。`
_Yes. First, what they are deciding. Then, what they will bring. Finally, what they will do if it rains._

**Learning evidence visible in the activity:**

- Main idea: a shared Sunday meal near a river.
- Detail: a station meeting point and vegetable option.
- Support: a route photo and small map.
- Contingency: move to a cafe if it rains.

**Choice - `practice-order`:** Listen for the meeting details first / listen for the rain alternative first. Both preserve the same two required responses.
**Recap if skipped:** _The rehearsal plan is understandable when the listener can name its purpose and fallback, not when they catch every word._
**Handoff:** `activity-listen-weekend-plan`.

## S5.3 - A Condition Is a Way to Care

**Location:** Rain Window.
**Narrative job:** Let Mika choose one ordinary fallback instead of multiplying contingencies.
**Authentic language need:** Offer a considerate alternative from a stated condition.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-nara-suggestion` - `outcome-suggest-with-nara`.
**Intent ID:** `plan.propose-from-condition`

_Mika has written three rain plans, two wind plans, and a plan for “weather behaving strangely.” Rie turns the page over and points to the one question that matters first._

**RIE:** `雨なら、どうしますか。まずは、それだけで十分です。`
_If it rains, what will you do? For now, that one question is enough._

**MIKA:** `雨なら、近くのカフェに 変えませんか。`
_If it rains, shall we change to a nearby cafe?_

**RIE:** `はい。相手が言った条件を受けて、次の提案ができています。`
_Yes. You have taken the stated condition and made the next proposal._

**Explanation:** `なら` picks up shared context and makes a useful response. It is not a weather effect; it is a way to say, “given that, here is a considerate next step.”

**N5 support:** `雨です。カフェに 行きましょう。`
**Bridge:** `雨の ときは、カフェに 行きませんか。`
**N4 target:** `雨なら、近くのカフェに 変えませんか。`

**Recap if skipped:** _Mika chose one clear rain alternative with `なら` instead of trying to write every possible future._
**Handoff:** `activity-nara-suggestion`.

## S5.4 - The Question That Makes an Option Real

**Location:** Planning table, menu card.
**Narrative job:** Give Suzu a practical use for a polite negative question.
**Authentic language need:** Check whether a useful option is available before publishing it on a plan.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-polite-negative-question` - `outcome-ask-with-arimasenka`.
**Intent ID:** `plan.ask-availability-politely`

_The rehearsal brief has a meal, but the draft card says only “food.” Suzu does not want to promise an option that does not exist._

**SUZU:** `野菜の料理は ありますか。`
_Are there vegetable dishes?_

**RIE:** `ありますか、でもいいです。今日は、もう少しやわらかく、予定を確かめる形も使いましょう。`
_Arimasu ka is fine too. Today, let us also use the gentler form for checking a plan._

**SUZU:** `野菜の料理は ありませんか。`
_Are there any vegetable dishes?_

**Explanation:** `ありませんか` is a polite availability question. It does not imply that an option must exist; it makes space to check before a group relies on it.

**Practice prompt:** Write the current model answer exactly: `野菜の料理はありませんか。`
**Recap if skipped:** _A published plan checks availability before it reassures a reader._
**Handoff:** `activity-polite-negative-question`.

## S5.5 - Do Something So Another Person Can Act

**Location:** Errand Loop, route-card photo point.
**Narrative job:** Let Leo add support to the plan without treating a map as self-explanatory.
**Authentic language need:** Explain why a photo or map is being sent.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-purpose-youni` - `outcome-express-purpose-youni`.
**Intent ID:** `plan.explain-support-purpose`

_Leo has a route photo, a map, and a pencil that has been asked to do too much. This time he starts with the reader’s result rather than the drawing._

**LEO:** `みんなが 場所を 見つけられるように、写真を 送ります。`
_I will send a photo so everyone can find the place._

**LEO:** `道に 迷わないように、小さい 地図も 作ります。`
_I will make a small map so no one gets lost._

**RIE:** `目的が先に見えると、支えが飾りではなくなります。`
_When the purpose comes first, support stops being decoration._

**Teaching card:**

| Intended result                      | Support action                      |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| `みんなが 場所を 見つけられるように` | `大きい 案内板の 写真を 送ります。` |
| `道に 迷わないように`                | `小さい 地図を 作ります。`          |

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5 support          | Bridge                                   | N4 target                                                    |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `写真を 送ります。` | `場所が 分かるように、写真を 送ります。` | `みんなが場所を見つけられるように、案内板の写真を送ります。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _Leo made every route support answer the question “so that what?”_
**Handoff:** `activity-purpose-youni`.

## S5.6 - One Voice, Same Plan

**Location:** Window Rehearsal, L07.
**Narrative job:** Let Nori turn a two-person plan into a private rehearsal without making performance the evidence of learning.
**Authentic language need:** Adapt an availability question and purpose statement into two or three independent lines.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-solo-dialogue-adaptation` - `outcome-adapt-dialogue-solo`.
**Intent ID:** `plan.adapt-dialogue-for-one-speaker`

_Nori places the two-speaker rehearsal beside a blank card. The task is not to sound fearless. It is to make the plan usable in one person’s voice._

**NORI:** `一人で言うなら、どこから始めますか。`
_If you say it alone, where do you begin?_

**RIE:** `必要な二つを選びましょう。質問一つと、助ける理由一つです。`
_Choose two things that matter: one question and one reason you will help._

**Draft model:**

> `野菜の料理は ありませんか。みんなが 迷わないように、地図を 送ります。雨なら、カフェに 変えませんか。`

Until that mismatch is resolved in the content model, the author panel must not mark this beat publishable as audio-off equivalent.

**Recap if skipped:** _The shared plan can be adapted into one person’s useful message. Text rehearsal is still a complete learning route._

## S5.7 - Write the Plan Someone Else Can Use

**Location:** Quiet Table.
**Narrative job:** Turn the rehearsal into a durable, reader-centred message.
**Authentic language need:** Write a shared-plan message with a suggestion, purpose, availability question, and fallback.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-write-shared-plan` - `outcome-write-shared-plan`; current model/rubric assets apply.
**Intent ID:** `plan.write-actionable-message`

_Suzu makes a four-column checklist: time, place, support, alternative. Mika starts a fifth column, pauses, and writes “only if needed” at the top instead._

**SUZU:** `読んだ人が、次に何をするか分かる文にしましょう。`
_Let us make a sentence where the reader knows what to do next._

**RIE:** `モデルは、書いたあとに見ます。先に、自分の計画を置いてみましょう。`
_Use the model after you write. First, put your own plan down._

**Activity contract:** The current activity asks for a 90-130 character Sunday-lunch plan with `なら`, `ありませんか`, one `ように/ないように` purpose, and a rain fallback. The model and rubric appear only after the first attempt, except the rubric remains available for self-review.

**Story transfer:** The group does not claim this rehearsal message is the Open Rooms guide. It proves what the final guide will need: reader action, support, option, and fallback.

**Recap if skipped:** _The rehearsal became a message someone could use without a second explanation._
**Handoff:** `activity-write-shared-plan`.

## S5.8 - Kanji 7 at the Card Rack

**Location:** Card Rack menu card.
**Narrative job:** Make the current Kanji 7 set legible in a genuine planning context.
**Authentic language need:** Read food, quantity, and size information on a shared-plan card.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-kanji-7` - `outcome-recognise-kanji-7`.
**Intent ID:** `plan.read-kanji-in-context`

_The rehearsal menu has been written by three people. It is efficient, legible, and contains exactly the seven characters that will now be made less alarming._

**RIE:** `漢字は、テストの壁ではありません。今日の計画に、もう出ている言葉です。`
_Kanji are not a test wall. They are words already present in today’s plan._

**Suzu's visual mnemonic card:**

| Kanji       | Useful word         | Visual cue                          |
| ----------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| `肉`        | `肉料理`            | the food choice on the plan         |
| `料` + `理` | `料理`              | ingredients arranged into a dish    |
| `野`        | `野菜`              | food grown in an open field         |
| `半`        | `半分`              | a plan shared in halves             |
| `大` / `小` | `大きい` / `小さい` | large and small route/support cards |

**Learner task:** Match each character to a familiar word, then order `日曜日に / 大きい案内板の写真を / 送ります。`
**Recap if skipped:** _The Kanji 7 set is part of the plan’s food, quantity, and support language, not a separate ornament._
**Handoff:** `activity-kanji-7`.

## S5.9 - The Card Rack Test

**Location:** Open Door Desk, special scene.
**Narrative job:** Pay off Mika's arc with a small, successful decision rather than a grand event.
**Authentic language need:** Test whether the plan covers purpose, option, and fallback; choose a next rehearsal.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-lesson-reflection` - self-assessment only; optional Study Connection `The Dry Chair`.
**Intent ID:** `plan.reflect-and-choose-next-rehearsal`

_The group lays the route photo, the small map, and the meal plan side by side. Suzu looks for contradictions. Leo looks for missing landmarks. Nori looks for the place where a reader can say no. Mika looks at the rain line once, then does not add another._

**MIKA:** `雨なら、カフェです。これで、いいと 思います。`
_If it rains, it is the cafe. I think this is enough._

**RIE:** `いいですね。「全部」ではなく、「次に必要なこと」があります。`
_Good. It does not contain everything. It contains what is needed next._

**Reflection prompt:** _How ready are you to propose a plan, explain its purpose, and handle rain?_ The learner chooses `not-yet`, `almost`, or `ready`; the result changes recommended review/support, never story outcome.

**Optional special scene - The Dry Chair:** Mika revises the rain line from “cafe” to “cafe by the entrance, where the Desk person waits from six until half past six.” It is a recap variation, not a required card.

**Recap if skipped:** _The rehearsal is complete enough to teach from. The next practice choice remains yours._
**Handoff:** `activity-lesson-reflection`; Campus and Review stay direct.

## S5.10 - The Fifth Card Is Not a Perfect Plan

**Location:** Blue Door Folio, chapter close.
**Narrative job:** Advance the central mystery with evidence about the former visitor’s purpose, not a personal clue.
**Authentic language need:** Read a short margin note using the chapter's planning language.
**Learning evidence:** `activity-purpose-youni` and `activity-write-shared-plan` as recap references; no new assessment.
**Intent ID:** `folio.read-handover-principle`

_The fifth card contains a pencilled practice line in the former visitor’s hand:_

> `次の人が 迷わないように、地図を 書く。雨なら、近くで 会う。`
>
> _Write a map so the next person does not get lost. If it rains, meet nearby._

**LEO:** `この人も、「もし」を書いています。`
_This person wrote “what if,” too._

**RIE:** `だから、このフォリオは答え集ではないのかもしれません。試すための紙です。`
_That may be why this folio is not an answer book. It is paper for testing._

**Chapter recap:**

- Listen first for a plan’s purpose and usable details.
- `なら` offers a next step from shared context.
- `ありませんか` checks availability politely.
- `ように/ないように` explains why a support action exists.
- A practical plan writes a support and a fallback for another reader.
- Kanji 7 lives inside that plan, and reflection chooses rehearsal rather than grading a person.

**Handoff:** Campus; Chapter 6 final guide task; **Continue learning** remains persistent.
