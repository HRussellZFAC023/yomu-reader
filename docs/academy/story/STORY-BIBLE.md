# Yomu Academy: One Term

## The class that decided to go to Japan

**Status:** This is the narrative canon for every Academy story document. **It replaces the earlier "Noticeboard Term / Open Door Desk / Blue Door Folio / Rain Page" material and its cast (Suzu, Leo, the folktale/mystery frame).** Do not retain those names, locations, the noticeboard-repair plot, or any of that framing. Where an older script still references them, the new frame in this bible wins.

**One-line promise:** One term of a real adult evening Japanese class — every Thursday at seven, a room of busy grown-ups learns Japanese from Rie-sensei and slowly becomes the kind of friends who plan a trip to Japan together.

**The turn:** Halfway through term, one of them gets a job offer in Japan. The class decides to throw him a surprise send-off — and then, quietly, to actually go and visit. You learn N5→N4 not to pass an exam, but so you can be there for people, in their language.

This is story-first, not a story gate. Campus, Continue learning, Review, Access, and any direct activity are always reachable. Reading a scene, taking a Study Link, or making a choice never records mastery, changes your route, or locks any language.

## 1. Tone: Persona bones, Ghibli heart

- **Persona bones** give it the shape of a real game: a term is a calendar, evenings are lessons, the space between is for **Study Links** (bonds) with classmates, each tied to the wholesome thing they love. The campus is the hub. There's always a clear next thing to do.
- **Ghibli heart** keeps the stakes human-sized and warm: weather, food, the last train, lamplight, someone leaving. Nobody saves the world. The whole drama is "don't let anyone sit alone," "get the plan clear enough that a tired friend doesn't have to guess," "say the kind thing in Japanese for once instead of English."

The feeling we're chasing: the specific coziness of a night class in winter — coats steaming on the radiator, a kettle that won't commit to boiling, someone brought biscuits, and for two hours the week goes quiet and you're just people learning a language together.

## 2. The cast (see `src/academy/cast.ts` — that file is the character bible)

Everyone is a first-name classmate defined **only** by a warm hobby. No jobs, addresses, relationships, or private history. They are affectionate caricatures, never mockery.

- **Rie-sensei** — warm, funny, impossibly busy; works what feels like nine jobs, so you keep bumping into her everywhere. The heart of the room. Starts from meaning, never rules.
- **Jodi** — used to live in Tokyo years ago; the class's quiet heart. Her stories are the ember that becomes the Japan dream.
- **Alex** — the quiet senpai who climbs mountains (yes, Fuji). **He's the one who gets the job offer in Japan.** Says little; every word lands.
- **Robert** — the bon vivant who organises the pub trip. Makes sure nobody drinks alone.
- **Shin** — the kanji wizard; always knows a better ramen place.
- **Sam** — Saturday tennis and a religious belief in okonomiyaki.
- **Angel** — already has a colour-coded spreadsheet for the trip nobody's booked yet.
- **Henry** — built a study app instead of doing his homework. (A soft wink: this is why "Yomu" exists.)
- **Tom, Francis, Xingyu, Jenny, Mika, Sophie, Stasi, Ruparna, Pho, Aakash, Christian** — the rest of the room, each anchored by one hobby. Use them for Study Links and warm crowd texture; give scene focus to whoever the chapter's grammar naturally belongs to.
- **Miller-san & Tawapon** — textbook ghosts who materialise to deliver one flawless coursebook sentence, then vanish. Comic relief only. Miller is always "going to Kobe."

Casting principle: **the grammar picks the person.** Inviting? Sam or Robert. Kanji? Shin. Feelings? Francis or Jodi. Plans and logistics? Angel. Never invent a biography to make a scene work.

## 3. The through-line

**Act I — a room of strangers (Chapters 1–2).** You join mid-term. Rie welcomes you like she's been waiting. Introductions, getting around, the first after-class pub trip. The class stops being coursemates and starts being people.

**Act II — a family, quietly forming (Chapters 3–4).** Ramen before class, okonomiyaki after tennis, biscuits in the break. Then a softer evening: Jodi talks about the Tokyo she lived in, and for the first time someone says out loud, "we should go."

**Act III — the plan (Chapters 5–6).** Alex tells them he's taking a job in Japan. First the class rehearses a small thing — a Sunday send-off lunch, and what to do if it rains (**this is the shipped Lesson 9**). Then Angel opens the spreadsheet for real. The finale is a surprise party and the trip itself: the class in Japan, using everything they learned, saying goodbye and hello at once.

**Emotional engine (not a mystery):**
1. Someone can't act on a vague sentence, or a feeling they can't say yet.
2. The class learns a warmer, more useful way to say it.
3. The message gets clearer; the evening gets kinder.
4. The people in the room get a little braver about asking for what they need — and a little closer.

Feeling is earned through practical care, never melodrama: a route reworded so a late friend doesn't guess; a rain plan that includes a chair, not just "indoors"; a goodbye said in Japanese because English felt too small for it.

### How the shipped Lesson 9 fits

Lesson 9 (`unit-level-3-plus-lesson-09`, Chapter 5) is the class **rehearsing a Sunday group lunch and a plan for rain** — the small, real logistics that later scale up to the Japan trip. The language (`なら`, polite `〜ありませんか`, purpose `〜ように`, listening for detail, planning writing, Kanji 7, reflection) all lives inside that one warm, ordinary task: *pick a time and place, ask if there's something everyone can eat, and have a backup if it rains, so nobody's left standing in the wet guessing.* Keep the eight activities and their order exactly as `src/academy/content.ts` defines them.

## 5. The special scenes (canon detail)

- **The pub with sensei (Ch1).** Robert: "授業のあと、一杯どう？" Half the class assumes teachers don't do this. Rie turns up, orders, and turns out to be the funniest person there. The warmth is that a teacher chose to stay. No drinking is required to attend; there's tea.
- **The konbini gag (Ch2).** Rie's running gag made real: you're buying an onigiri at midnight and the person at the till is Rie-sensei, delighted and exhausted, quizzing you on the counter for rice balls while she scans them. Never played as sad — played as "of course she is, and of course she's kind about it."
- **Ramen (Ch3).** Shin reads the menu like a picture book and teaches you a kanji as a tiny story before class. It always, annoyingly, works.
- **Jodi's Tokyo (Ch4).** Not a flashback dungeon — just an evening where Jodi shows a few photos and the room goes quiet in the good way. This is where "we should go" is first said.
- **The job offer (Ch5).** Alex, who never makes a fuss, mentions he's moving to Japan for work like it's a weekend errand. The class's reaction *is* the scene.
- **The surprise party (Ch6).** For Alex. Angel organised it (obviously). Everyone writes him one line in Japanese. This is the N4 payoff: you can finally say the warm thing without English.
- **The trip (Ch6).** The class in Japan — a ryokan, a shinkansen, a temple, a street at night. Small, real, Ghibli-quiet. Not a highlight reel.
- **HelloTalk side-route (optional, Ch4/Ch6).** Wholesome only. Mika (the shy polyglot) finds a kind language-exchange penpal in Japan; you can opt in to a gentle written back-and-forth that pays off when they meet you in Tokyo. Never romance, never a stranger-danger beat, never required. It's "a friend you made by being brave enough to type first."

## 6. Study Links (Persona confidants)

Each classmate has a bond thread tied to their hobby (defined in `cast.ts` under `studyLinks`). They're optional, warm, and drive practice, never mastery gates: Aakash's night drive (likes/adjectives), Shin's ramen counter (kanji/menus), Sam's grill night (inviting), Jenny's "one row at a time" (asking after people), Angel's shared doc (dates/plans), Jodi's Tokyo (past tense/memory), and so on. A Study Link is time with a friend that happens to teach you something.

## 7. Authoring rules

- The learner may stay unnamed forever. Never ask for a real name, pronouns, job, history, contact details, a microphone, or a reason for studying. Unnamed is the normal path.
- Supporting characters are present-tense people with small wants. No secret biographies to solve, no romance routes, no guilt hooks, no urgency, no scores, no consequences for leaving. Come back next Thursday.
- All Japanese, English, model answers, and art briefs are original Yomu Academy writing. Curriculum sources (Minna, Genki, JLPT scope) guide sequence only — never lift example sentences or wording.
- The campus is a warm fictional place. It may take broad geographic cues from a real city, but **no institution is named, endorsed, or represented in user-facing copy** — it's "your class," "the classroom," "class order," never "UCL."
- Voice is enforced by [VOICE.md](./VOICE.md). Read it before writing a single line.
- Every shippable asset records provenance and clearance; a source link is evidence of scope, not text to copy.

## 8. What changed from the old canon

Removed: the Open Door Desk, the noticeboard-repair plot, the Blue Door Folio and its "unfinished last sentence" mystery, the Rain Page, and the invented cast (Suzu, Leo). Removed the placement test as a story beat — there is no test; Rie just welcomes you in. Kept and deepened: the real first-name cast, Rie's warmth and her nine-jobs running gag, the Thursday-night classroom, and the practical-care emotional engine — now hung on a story with a destination: Japan, together.
