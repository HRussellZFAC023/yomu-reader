## 1. Who they really are

Sophie is the quiet powerhouse of the class — a heritage/near-native speaker who functions as the group's unofficial **second sensei**, and who would be mortified to be called that out loud (Aakash calls her "sensei"; she answers "no promises 👁👁"). She is from **Singapore** — when someone hadn't heard of CNA she explained it's "the equivalent to sky/bbc in Singapore" — and she is **part Hong Kong**, with a **+852** number that rotates in and out of her contact card. She is the person who, in the first week, actually **figured out which room the class was in and how to get into the building**, and she has quietly stayed the most reliable helper for **homework logistics** ever since: the audio nobody could find, the page reference, the "it's 5時半 くらい から, right?" that turns out to be exactly right.

Her real texture is precise and endearing. She keeps **colour-coded, multi-colour notes** for everything. She is, affectionately, the **teacher's-pet type** — top of the class, finishing before you've read the question, the classic 「もう終わりました。次は？」 She translated **Alex's** Japanese work emails about **pig-iron imports** for him, and she's **Aakash's** go-to grammar checker. She bribes the group into showing up — **"i bought kitkats so yall better come next week"** — and shares **anime reels** and study resources (she's the one who dropped the **Tofugu kana quiz** in chat and explained the anime-episode-intro phrase). She once claimed, self-deprecatingly, that she "struggled making the group name."

The heart of her, and the thing her four-season arc actually turns on: **she wants to be finished and still be liked.** Fast and correct is her comfort, and she quietly hopes that "already done" doesn't land as showing off. That's not a fragility to cure — it reads as generosity held a little too tight. The class becoming a genuine team is what loosens her grip on perfect; she learns to be **kinder to herself** and to others (she's the one who eventually gives a tired classmate the *five-minutes-a-day* advice). This deepens — and never contradicts — the canon anchor: her precision was always real, but the story teaches her that precision **includes stating its own limits**, and that a corrected inference costs her nothing she can't afford.

> **Reconciliation with the anchor.** Everything real above serves the canon line "prefers evidence over atmosphere." Her colour-coded notes are the visible form of "names distinctions and confidence levels." Her being the person who found the room, the audio, the right particle is exactly why the class trusts her enough that her *one confident wrong inference* (Ch 14) matters — and why her becoming the **calibration voice** is a promotion, not a demotion. She was never the anxious one; she was the fast one learning that "I'm about 70% on this" is a stronger sentence than a guess delivered at full volume.

## 2. Four-season arc across her five beats

Her deeper arc — **from "the right answer, fast" to "the honestly-calibrated answer, shared"** — plays beat by beat. She is `continuity-only` by default; these five beats are her guaranteed presence, enriched with real texture and the callbacks she owns.

### Beat 1 — Arrival · Ch 1 `s1e01-the-blank-atlas`
She is already there when the learner arrives at the one open chair — because **she's the one who found the room.** Her first act is small and true to life: she quietly tells the learner (and half the class) where things are, in a mix of easy Japanese and English, and lands the classroom-repair phrase before Rie has to. Seed of the anchor: she notices *which* detail is load-bearing (the door code, the floor, the time) and states it cleanly. No spotlight; she'd hate a spotlight. Arrival seeds one attention detail: **the colour-coded notebook already open on her desk.**

### Beat 2 — Contribution (LEAD) · Ch 14 `s1e14-two-answers` — *Two Answers, One Context*
Her chapter. A grammar question the class treats as having one right answer — **two plausible particles** — and everyone turns to Sophie the way Aakash always does, expecting the verdict. She gives it, fast, confidently: 「もう終わりました。次は？」 **And she is only partly right.** Both particles are defensible; the context, not the rule, decides — and there is no single universal answer to hand out. This is the **confident wrong inference** the canon promises she survives. The repair isn't humiliation (canon: humour from timing/callback, never humiliation); it's Rie and the learner putting **both answers on contextual trial**, and Sophie discovering that "evidence + explanation" beats "verdict." She seeds `callback:useful-number-notebook` here: a number gets **recorded with its context** in her notebook rather than displayed as *the answer*. Her colour-coding gets its first real job — a colour for "sure," a colour for "depends." The teacher's-pet learns that the best note isn't the fastest; it's the one that marks how sure you are.

### Beat 3 — Limit · Ch 27 `s3e03-helpful-rewrite` — *The Helpful Rewrite*
Season 3, register and authorship. A polished edit — the kind Sophie is *superb* at, the same instinct that let her translate Alex's pig-iron emails into clean English — makes **Peter's** contribution "sound like nobody." Her limit is exactly her gift over-applied: she can repair an error so thoroughly that she repairs the person out of it. Canon job: **Sophie distinguishes error repair from voice replacement.** She learns to fix the mistake and keep the voice — to correct *kindly and accurately*, softening a sharp edit instead of overwriting. This is where her real "accurate AND kind" texture becomes a plot mechanism, not just a trait.

### Beat 4 — Return · Ch 38 `s4e02-map-of-claims` — *A Map of Claims*
She and **Henry** build the provenance evidence map. This is her arc's payoff surface: the colour-coded notes and the number notebook **transform** into an evidence map that **marks confidence and source kind instead of hiding gaps** (`callback:useful-number-notebook` transform). The girl who once wanted every cell filled and correct now builds a map whose most rigorous move is a visible blank labelled "we don't know this yet." Calibrated certainty, in colour. She has become the **calibration voice** the anchor promised.

### Beat 5 — Future · Ch 47 `s4e11-atlas-closes` — *The Atlas Closes*
In the final synthesis, provenance resolves **only to the level the record supports** (`callback:useful-number-notebook` payoff), and Rie names the ordinary mechanism. The season-4 behavioral callback fires: **"Sophie states confidence"** — she is the one who says, plainly, what they established, what they inferred, and what they still cannot claim, and is at peace with the last category. Her future is not "the perfect student graduates"; it's the person who taught the class that an honest *"about 70%"* is a finished, giftable thing. She passes on the tired-learner **five-minutes-a-day** kindness as her closing note.

## 3. The Japanese her story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

**Learning speciality:** *grammar precision + source comparison + calibrated confidence.* Sophie is the cast's home for **hedging and evidentiality** — the exact grammar of "how sure am I, and on what evidence?" Where Shin decomposes a form and Ruparna judges a framing, Sophie's scenes make the learner **state a claim at its true confidence level and give the evidence for it.** Her real habit — *accurate AND kind* — anchors a second speciality: **register-preserving correction** (softening a command into advice with 〜ほうがいいと思います).

Each beat makes specific Japanese *necessary*, hosts a specific activity type, and ties to the registered reservoir.

### Ch 1 — Arrival · reservoir `lesson:foundation-00` + classroom-expression shard
- **Language function(s):** location & existence (ここ / そこ / あそこ, 〜は〜に あります), giving simple directions, and the classroom-repair phrase (もう一度 おねがいします).
- **Activity type:** listening + speaking (live directions), the foundation transfer "greet, follow an instruction, and ask for repetition in the live room."
- **Why the real texture makes it necessary:** she *is* the classmate who found the seminar room — so the authentic reason to produce "where is X / it's on the Nth floor" Japanese is that Sophie genuinely holds that information and the learner genuinely needs it to sit down. Directions aren't a drill; they're how you get in the door on night one.

### Ch 14 — LEAD · reservoir `l1-l21` → transfer "use a number notebook as evidence rather than an answer display"
- **Language function(s):** **particle contrast under context** (two defensible particles judged by situation, not by a universal rule); **opinion + confidence markers** 〜と思います / 〜でしょう / たぶん; **giving a reason/evidence** for a grammar choice (〜から / 〜という理由で); **recording a quantity with context** (counters, 〜くらい, e.g. 5時半 くらい から).
- **Activity type:** grammar + **writing** (the annotated notebook) + **speaking** (explain *why*, don't just declare). Follows the beat pattern: Need → Input → Lesson → Repair (both answers tried) → Transfer (notebook survives after the surface disappears) → Consequence → Return.
- **Why the real texture makes it necessary:** the class already treats Sophie as the human answer key (Aakash's "sensei," the go-to grammar checker). The scene weaponizes that trust: her 「もう終わりました。次は？」 reflex meets a question where *both* answers are right depending on context, so the learner is forced off "what's the answer?" onto "what's the evidence, and how sure are we?" — which is the whole point of と思います vs でしょう vs a flat assertion. Her colour-coded notes give the learner a concrete tool: a colour for certain, a colour for "depends."

### Ch 27 — Limit · reservoir Season-3 N3 family (reported speech, implication, **paraphrase, and register-preserving edit**; four-skill proof "compare two written paraphrases; explain what changed; produce a bounded rewrite")
- **Language function(s):** **paraphrase & register** (casual ↔ polite ↔ formal), **error repair vs voice replacement**, and **softening a correction** — the signature move 〜ほうがいいと思います, turning a bare command into advice with room to decline (task hook `ext-sophie-soften-advice`, N4: "Rewrite one command with ほうがいいと思います").
- **Activity type:** **reading** (two paraphrases side by side) + **writing** (a bounded rewrite that fixes the error but keeps Peter's voice) + **transfer**.
- **Why the real texture makes it necessary:** Sophie *really does* rewrite other people's Japanese — she cleaned up Alex's pig-iron-import emails. That competence is exactly what makes the register lesson land: the learner must feel the difference between "I fixed your mistake" and "I replaced you," because Sophie's instinct is to smooth everything, and smoothing a person's voice away is the failure mode the grammar is teaching them to avoid.

### Ch 38 — Return · reservoir Season-4 N1 family (synthesis, source quality, **calibrated certainty**; proof "synthesize three accounts; defend uncertainty")
- **Language function(s):** **evidentiality & hedging** — 〜そうだ / 〜らしい / 〜ようだ, source attribution 〜によると, and marking a claim's confidence explicitly (the canonical diagnostic line ここは「そうらしい」までかな — "this only goes as far as *apparently*"); **nominalization / source description** for the map's cells.
- **Activity type:** **reading** (weighing source quality) + **writing** (the evidence map, colour = confidence) + **transfer**, built jointly with Henry.
- **Why the real texture makes it necessary:** her colour-coded notes literally *become* the tool — colours now encode confidence and source kind. The authentic reason to reach for らしい / によると / ようだ instead of a flat statement is that the class is trying to attribute a real caption and *cannot* honestly claim more than the evidence supports. The grammar of hedging is the only grammar that lets the map be both useful and true.

### Ch 47 — Future · reservoir Season-4 integrated N1 (final synthesis and **uncertainty statement**; "produce the final bounded contribution")
- **Language function(s):** concluding **to the level the record supports**, distinguishing established / inferred / unestablished in speech, and stating what cannot be claimed without apology.
- **Activity type:** **speaking / integrated production** — the behavioral callback "Sophie states confidence" performed live; plus a short **writing** hand-off (her five-minutes-a-day note).
- **Why the real texture makes it necessary:** the fast, perfect student closing the atlas by saying out loud "this part we don't know" is the emotional and pedagogical peak of hedging — the learner sees that N1 mastery is *knowing the shape of your own uncertainty*, not eliminating it.

**Cross-beat learning spine:** confidence markers (Ch 14) → register-preserving softening (Ch 27) → evidentiality & source hedging (Ch 38) → calibrated uncertainty statement (Ch 47). Every beat requires the learner to *produce* the level of certainty the situation warrants — the single thread that turns Sophie's precision into a teachable skill.
