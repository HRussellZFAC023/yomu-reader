## 1. Who they really are

Nanako is the one person in this room who isn't studying Japanese — she *is* the Japanese the rest of them are chasing. A native speaker who spent about four months in London and met Aakash and Tom during that stretch, she now splits her life between the UK and Japan, having gone back after her London spell, travelled a little of Europe, and returns periodically. In the group she is the beloved informal sensei-figure: the person the class thanks with "授業お疲れさま" and "so much Japanese, thank you," even though she never once stood at a whiteboard. Her Japanese sounds *lived-in*, not instructional — and that is precisely her value to a learning game. She models the gap the textbook can't teach: the distance between what is technically correct and what a person would actually send.

She is Henry's girlfriend, met on HelloTalk — the emotional engine of his whole motivation to learn ("working hard to be with her someday"). She helps him read *Yotsuba&*, nudges his homework, and is the quiet reason the group frames itself as a Japanese circle rather than just friends who eat well. The romance itself lives on the HelloTalk route and stays off the class floor (all in-class bonds are platonic) — but her *presence*, her visits and reunions and the occasional bilingual message in the group chat, gives invitations, welcomes, and natural-message tasks a human reason to exist.

Her manner is gentle, gracious, and self-effacing. She is chronically the one apologising for being a little late — "ごめんなさい、少し遅れます🥲" — which fits a group whose entire comedic engine is that everyone is always late ("late is just a mindset"). She celebrates people without fuss, answers teasing with soft humour rather than defence, and deflects rather than deflates: when Henry's phone autosuggests 👵 for "Nana" and the old-woman joke starts, she folds herself into it — "それなら私もおばあさんだよ👵" — refusing to be above the bit. She and Aakash are near-birthday twins (both 27 in 2025, birthdays about three weeks apart, hers around mid-March), so the "are we old?" 若い-vs-おじいさん/おばあさん teasing is a shared, affectionate thing between them, never at anyone's expense.

And she is naturally bilingual in the chat: she'll post in Japanese, then quietly gloss it in English so no one is stranded, and sign off in the spirit of "日本語頑張ってください." Central to the group's affection while slightly in the background of its logistics — that combination is the whole character. She is not the one running the karaoke argument or the room-number scramble; she's the warm centre everyone orients toward.

**How this deepens (never contradicts) the voice anchor.** The anchor gives us four load-bearing verbs — *close cleanly, correct gently, repair socially, rule quietly.* Every real detail sharpens one of them rather than overwriting it:
- *Correct gently / rule quietly* → her "quiet ruling on dubious claims" is the real running joke: the class presents a shaky "Japanese" idea, Nanako corrects it without display, and the ruling is treated as final. She is the arbiter of whether a phrase is one someone would actually send.
- *Repair socially* → the always-late apology, the English gloss so no one is excluded, welcoming a newer member in.
- *Close cleanly* → gratitude and sign-offs; she is the one who lands the end of an exchange with the right warmth.

Nothing here touches the four-season arc, her occasional cadence, or her non-classmate status. Her rarity is a story function, not a gap to fill.

## 2. Four-season arc across her five beats

Her deeper arc is a single graceful line: **the sensei-figure learns the limit of her own authority.** She begins as the person whose ruling is final, and ends having taught the class that being the source of a language — or a memory — is not the same as owning the right to publish it. Her beats fire through Henry's orbit, the group chat, and visits/reunions, never the classroom curriculum.

### Beat 1 — Arrival · Chapter 23 · `s1e23-farewell-rehearsal` (LEAD)
Her guide role is ending; her time in the UK is winding down and the group plans a farewell meal — which, being this group, falls apart in real time (everyone late, the plan hedged and re-hedged). This is her one lead. The scene's emotion is **intentions, before/after, and gratitude, with postponed words left unfinished.** She tries to say the thank-you and the goodbye properly and keeps getting interrupted by logistics; the meal that was meant to hold the words never quite convenes. The learner's job is to help carry the meaning she can't finish saying herself.
- **Seed (attention detail):** her habit of glossing her own Japanese into English — the small kindness of never leaving anyone stranded. Also seeds the 👵 deflection as a light attention-beat, suspended the instant any real feeling enters.
- **Callback family:** *relationship / class-continuity.* This arrival seeds one attention detail (her clean, considerate closings) that later beats echo and transform.

### Beat 2 — Contribution · Chapter 28 · `s3e04-terms-of-invitation`
The venue asks for a public version before every contributor has agreed; Sam leads the consent language, and **Nanako contributes the pragmatic edge** — the difference between an invitation that technically asks and one a person could actually, comfortably decline. Her bounded competence alters shared work: she tunes the group's requests so they carry a real, easy exit. This is consent-as-craft, and it's exactly her lane (an invitation states the activity and an easy way to say no; one clear "no" ends persuasion).

### Beat 3 — Limit · Chapter 33 · `s3e09-what-we-can-say`
The hinge of her whole arc. Alongside Jodi and Mira, she separates **memory, permission, and public claim** — and this is where her signature running joke *transforms.* All season she has been the final ruling on what is and isn't real Japanese. Here she rules on something harder and finds the ruling turns back on her: *lived experience is not automatic permission to publish it.* Being the native source of a phrase, or the person who was actually there, does not grant the right to put it on a wall. Her authority meets its ethical boundary, and she is the one who names it. Evidential language and hedging are the tools; her gentleness is what lets the class hear a hard limit without a fight.

### Beat 4 — Return · Chapter 41 · `s4e05-left-unsaid`
She returns — a visit/reunion, not a curriculum slot — for a quiet rehearsal, and asks the sharpest small question of the season: *what does not belong on stage?* The learning is **implication, ellipsis, and private/public register: omission becomes authored meaning.** This pays off the Arrival seed with changed behaviour: the woman who once glossed everything into English so nothing was lost now teaches that some things are *chosen* to be left unsaid — that leaving a line off the page can be the most deliberate authorship of all. The postponed farewell words of Chapter 23 find their echo here: not everything unfinished needs finishing in public.

### Beat 5 — Future · Chapter 48 · `s4e12-next-page`
At graduation her thread resolves without demanding an outcome. She is one of at most three elective payoff lines plus the class-continuity montage — a bounded, changed-behaviour beat, not a roll-call thank-you. Her future stays open: still splitting UK and Japan, still the warm reason Henry's own "someday in Japan" has a direction. She closes her exchange cleanly, the way she always has, and leaves the blank page under the class's permission terms without insisting anyone repeat her.

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION)

**Speciality: pragmatics and register — social repair, and the gap between technically-correct and what-you'd-actually-send.** She is the natural home for *natural* Japanese: reported/relayed meaning, softened requests, hedging, gratitude, and the private/public register split. Where a textbook character validates a form, Nanako validates *whether a native speaker would actually say it that way.* Every one of her scenes is built to make the learner **need** register, not just conjugation.

Her lead sits on the **Chapter 23 reservoir `l2-l26`–`l2-l30`**, whose transfer functions are: *pass on meaning, follow a model, express means/absence, and revise conditions.* That reservoir is almost eerily tailored to a farewell scene where words must be relayed, a model followed, and a plan repeatedly re-hedged.

### Beat-by-beat learning integration

**Chapter 23 — Arrival / LEAD — reservoir `l2-l26`–`l2-l30`**
- **Language function(s) the scene makes the learner NEED:**
  - *Pass on / relay meaning* (reported speech — 〜と言っていました): the farewell keeps failing to convene, so words meant for Nanako have to be **carried** by the learner to her and back. You literally cannot resolve the scene without relaying what someone else said.
  - *Intentions & before/after* (〜つもり; 〜前に / 〜てから): the group is trying to do things "before she leaves," in the right order, and the order keeps collapsing.
  - *Gratitude* (〜てくれてありがとう / お世話になりました; 授業お疲れさま): saying the thank-you *properly* — the thing the whole scene is straining toward.
  - *Means / absence* (〜で by means of; 〜なくて / 〜ないで — "without having said…"): the postponed words are an *absence*, and the learner names it.
  - *Revise conditions* (〜たら / 〜なら): the plan re-hedges live ("if she's late, then…"; "if the restaurant's full, then…") — the group's signature rescheduling comedy rendered as grammar.
- **Activity type:** listening + speaking + transfer (relay task). Read/hear what one person wants said; reproduce it to Nanako in natural register; adjust when the plan changes. A writing micro-task: draft the gratitude line that never got spoken aloud.
- **Real-life texture → authentic reason:** *everyone is always late* and *the farewell meal falls apart* are not invented drama — they are this group's actual comedic engine. That chaos is the pedagogical gift: it forces relayed speech and re-hedged conditionals because no one is ever all in one place at one time. The learner practises "passing on meaning" because Nanako, gracious and half-apologising for her own lateness, is never quite in the room when the words are ready.

**Chapter 28 — Contribution — reservoir band `28–30` (N3 conditions, refusal, role negotiation, conflicting plans)**
- **Language function(s) needed:** softened conditional invitation (〜ませんか; よかったら〜; 〜たら) and *respectful, face-saving refusal* (〜はちょっと…; 申し訳ないけど…). The learner must build an invitation that carries a genuine, easy exit — and recognise a softened "no" when it's given.
- **Activity type:** speaking + writing (produce a bounded invitation; decline or renegotiate orally). Four-skill proof: *hear a softened condition; decline or renegotiate; write a clarified plan.*
- **Real-life texture → authentic reason:** consent is a story behaviour in this project — invitations state the activity and an easy way to decline; one clear "no" ends persuasion. Nanako, the group's warmest connector and the person who welcomes newer members in, is the natural authority on making an ask *comfortable to refuse.* The learner needs the grammar because Nanako's whole contribution is proving that "would you like to?" and "you can absolutely say no" must live in the same sentence.

**Chapter 33 — Limit — reservoir band `31–33` (N2 quotation, translation choice, evidentiality, hedging)**
- **Language function(s) needed:** evidential marking (〜そうだ / 〜らしい / 〜みたい — distinguishing observed vs heard vs inferred) and hedging (〜と思います / 〜かもしれません / はっきりとは言えない). The learner must state *how* they know a thing, and mark the seam between "I remember it" and "I may share it."
- **Activity type:** reading/listening annotation + writing calibrated claims. Four-skill proof: *distinguish observation / hearsay / inference; write calibrated claims.*
- **Real-life texture → authentic reason:** Nanako is the group's final ruling on what is real Japanese — so she is the *only* person whose reversal lands here. When the arbiter herself says "being the source is not permission to publish," the learner feels why evidentiality and hedging matter: they are the grammar of *not overclaiming.* Her gentleness is the delivery mechanism — she models that you can hold a hard line in soft language.

**Chapter 41 — Return — reservoir band `40–42` (N1 perspective, compatible accounts, implication)**
- **Language function(s) needed:** implication and ellipsis (leaving the second clause unsaid; 〜けど… trailing off) and the private/public register split (casual vs keigo, and *what simply gets omitted*). Omission as authored meaning.
- **Activity type:** reading + writing (interpret what is deliberately left unsaid; compose a line whose meaning lives in its omission). Speaking: rehearse a version that says less on purpose.
- **Real-life texture → authentic reason:** the Arrival seed was Nanako glossing everything into English so no one was lost. Her Return inverts it: the same person now teaches that *choosing not to say* is a skill. That personal reversal is what makes ellipsis stick — the learner has watched the most inclusive, everything-glossed speaker decide, on purpose, to leave a line off the stage. The postponed farewell of Ch23 resolves as "some words stay private, and that's authorship, not failure."

**Chapter 48 — Future — reservoir band `46–48` (integrated N1, graduation composition)**
- **Language function(s) needed:** graduation composition and a final choice of *tone* — non-comparative future plans (〜たいと思っています; まだわからないけど), open-ended and unranked.
- **Activity type:** integrated production (the learner writes/records one bounded contribution). Nanako's payoff is a short changed-behaviour beat, not a lesson.
- **Real-life texture → authentic reason:** her real life *is* an open future — UK/Japan, back and forth, no tidy ending. She anchors the game's "someday in Japan" aspiration precisely because her own story doesn't resolve into a destination. The learner produces open-future Japanese because Nanako proves an open future is a complete one.

## 4. Elective bond route (CONDITIONAL — not yet `bond-authored`)

> **Authorization note:** Nanako is `continuity-only` and occasional; private-source restrictions remain binding, and no roster status grants an elective appointment. This route is a **proposal for a future reviewed `bond-authored` manifest entry**, not a live bond. If authored, it fires through Henry's orbit / visits / the group chat, never the classroom, and stays inside her rarity. Its payoff cannot resolve the atlas, another person's boundary, or a lesson requirement. All appointments answer in Japanese.

Theme: **reading *Yotsuba&* together, and learning to sound like a person instead of a textbook.**

1. **Recognition (ordinary task).** Read a page of *Yotsuba&* aloud with her; she doesn't correct grammar, she flags one line that's "correct but not what someone would say." *Function:* casual register recognition — spotting textbook-stiff phrasing (〜dictionary form vs natural contraction).
2. **A different route (reframe).** She asks you to *rewrite* a stiff message as something you'd actually send — same meaning, real register. *Function:* pragmatic paraphrase; social repair; casual sentence-final particles (〜よ / 〜ね / 〜っけ).
3. **Reciprocity.** You teach *her* something back — a study tool, a kanji trick, an English idiom she half-knows — and she glosses her thanks bilingually. *Function:* gratitude + explanation (〜てくれてありがとう; 〜という意味です).
4. **Friction.** You "correct" her on a point of grammar you learned in class; she's right and you're textbook-right-but-wrong. The friction is gentle — no display, just her quiet ruling — and it stings a little. *Function:* evidentiality / conceding (〜と思ってたけど…; 実は…); accepting a correction gracefully.
5. **Repair.** She apologises for the sting she didn't intend (she always over-apologises), you repair it back, and you settle the difference between *rule* and *usage.* *Function:* apology + reassurance (ごめんね / 気にしないで; 〜なくてもいいよ).
6. **Future.** A reunion beat: you send her the first fully-natural message you've ever written unaided, and she doesn't gloss it into English — because she doesn't need to. *Function:* open-future plans + register mastery (また会えたら〜; unglossed natural Japanese as the payoff).
