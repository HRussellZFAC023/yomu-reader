## 1. Who they really are

Sam is the person who has already put the drinks on the table before anyone has agreed where they're going. That single image is his whole voice: generosity that arrives *before* the plan, warmth expressed as logistics. Canon calls him the one who "checks who has not answered and translates enthusiasm into a clear invitation" — and the real Sam is exactly that, the classmate who notices the quiet person at the end of the table and says the sentence that lets them in.

The real texture deepens the anchor rather than fighting it:

- **Food is how he shows he likes you.** His stated favourite is okonomiyaki — the Christmas okonomiyaki, the "okonomiyaki and tennis, I would love nothing more" energy. He genuinely believes okonomiyaki is a personality, and the group has quietly concluded he might be right. In the Academy this becomes his *reason* to speak Japanese: he is always feeding the table, so he is always the one who has to **invite, count, offer, and request**.
- **The Saturday athlete.** Tennis every Saturday, then okonomiyaki afterward, no exceptions. This is not a quirk to mock — it is a *fixed point*, and fixed points are what make an invitation hard to answer. His signature comic engine is that the group plan keeps landing on Saturday, straight into a standing commitment he will not move.
- **The one who bows out gracefully but leaves the door open.** The real Sam stepped back from the tail of the course rather than attend on terms he couldn't keep, and made a point of staying in the food-and-friends loop — a warm "ともだち" send-off with a promise of okonomiyaki and tennis, not a disappearance. In canon this is *not* written as him leaving the class (he is present through Ch 46). It becomes the **emotional grain** of his arc: he is the character who understands that presence is negotiated, that a "not this time" is not a "no to you," and who therefore has the most to teach about inclusion and consent.
- **The reliable, understated helper.** He sends the homework photo without being asked, thanks people warmly ("Anytime :)"), photographs and shares the handout, and is gently self-deprecating about his handwriting next to Aakash's. Aakash added him to the group; they swap notes. His register in Japanese is practical and bright — すごいですね!, メリークリスマス, クラスであいましょう — a doer's Japanese, not a grammarian's.
- **Sometimes away.** Work sits near the university and occasionally takes him elsewhere. In-fiction this is only ever "Sam can't always be here," never a hard biographical claim (see §6) — but it is *why* his lane is invitations that survive an absence and plans that hold when one person can't come.

**Reconciliation with canon:** nothing above contradicts the four-season arc. The real "generous host who bows out kindly" is the same person as the canon "asks once and hears the answer." The tennis/food routine supplies the concrete detail the callback ledger already reserved as "Saturday conflict" and "overbuilt schedule." Every real detail is load-bearing for a language function he already owns.

## 2. Four-season arc (his five continuity beats)

Sam's deep arc is a single movement: **from inclusion-as-persistence to inclusion-as-one-clear-invitation-and-a-heard-answer.** His pressure behavior (canon) is asking a second inclusion question too quickly; his growth is asking once. The beats stage that shift concretely.

### Beat 1 — Arrival & Lead · Ch 6 `s1e06-invitation-chain`
His practice invitation accumulates so many options that nobody can answer it — day, time, place, food, whether tennis comes first — and the more he adds to be inclusive, the more he makes the invitation unanswerable. This is the **seed of `callback:overbuilt-schedule`** and the **first explicit consent beat** of the whole story. The learner's job is to help Sam collapse the sprawl into one clear invitation with a real opening and an easy way to say no. Real texture: the sprawl is honest — it's the Saturday-tennis-then-okonomiyaki fixture colliding with a group plan, so *every* option is genuinely on the table.

### Beat 2 — Contribution · Ch 12 `s1e12-the-vanishing-course`
Jenny loses every serving cue and pulls the class into the scene; this is the first ensemble-callback payoff. Sam contributes the way the real Sam contributes — he is already feeding the table. His bounded competence is **stocking, offering, and counting**: what's here, how many are needed, who still doesn't have one. The drinks-already-on-the-table instinct becomes materially useful in a failed service plan, and the homework-photo generosity becomes "here, I photographed the handout, everyone has it." He doesn't lead the rescue; he makes it possible.

### Beat 3 — Limit · Ch 28 `s3e04-terms-of-invitation`
The venue asks for a public version before every contributor has agreed, and **Sam leads the consent language** (canon). This is his Limit — the growth beat. Under pressure he reaches for a second inclusion question, a re-ask, one more option to make the "yes" easier. The scene's turn is that he asks **once**, states the scope, and *accepts a "not yet."* His whole gift (getting people to say yes) has to survive learning that a heard "no" is also inclusion. The comedy suspends here (per ledger rule); this beat is played straight and warm.

### Beat 4 — Return · Ch 34 `s3e10-empty-microphone`
A host drops out and the group must redistribute public speaking **without coercion**; quiet roles count as full participation. This proves the ensemble heard Sam's limit — and proves Sam heard it in himself. He offers a role with a built-in exit ("only if you want it; the quiet job counts too"), the exact opposite of the Ch 6 over-invitation. The person who used to ask twice now hands someone an out and means it.

### Beat 5 — Future · Ch 46 `s4e10-public-evening`
The public evening succeeds after one live misunderstanding forces a transparent repair (integrated N1). Sam's **behavioral callback "Sam asks once" pays off**: he issues one clean, audience-aware invitation into a room, and — because it is finally *one* clear invitation with room to decline — it lands. His open-door, "okonomiyaki and tennis afterwards, whoever's free" warmth becomes the after-event gathering the group actually attends. His future is one voluntary next appointment, offered the grown-up way.

### Callback appearance — Ch 39 `s4e03-the-polite-no` (payoff of `overbuilt-schedule`)
Not one of his five continuity beats, but his material: the venue asks for a cleaner, more inspirational ending than the evidence supports, and **Robert and Sam keep the relationship while holding the boundary** — the over-optioned Ch 6 invitation has become the ability to decline a bad plan with *one* clear alternative and room for an answer. The sprawl inverted into a clean no.

## 3. The Japanese his story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — the core section)

**Learning speciality:** the *social-transaction* band of Japanese — time, routine, invitation, offer, request, and the pragmatics of consent (softening, declining, and hearing a decline). Sam is the character who makes the learner *need* ますか/ませんか, 〜から〜まで, counters, and the polite "no." He is also the game's designated teacher of **consent as a language behavior**: an invitation that names the activity and an easy way to decline, and one clear "no" that ends persuasion.

Every beat follows the CONTENT-LINKAGE order (Need → Input → Lesson → Repair → Transfer → Consequence → Return) and pulls from the registered reservoir. Learning is the point; the food-and-Saturday texture exists to make the language unavoidable.

### Ch 6 — reservoir `l1-l08`–`l1-l09` · transfer: *state time, opening, and a clear invitation*
- **Language functions the scene makes the learner NEED:** telling time (いま〜じ / 〜じ〜ふん); days of the week (〜ようび); availability/opening ranges (〜から〜まで); the invitation forms ませんか / ましょう(か); and the first **polite decline / soft refusal** (いいですね、でも… / ちょっと…) so the learner can both extend and *hear* a "no."
- **Activity types:** vocabulary (time, days, meals) → grammar (〜から〜まで, ませんか vs ましょう) → **speaking** (issue one clean invitation aloud) → **listening** (recognize a hedged decline as a decline) → short **writing** (one-line class-thread invitation).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** Sam's fixed "tennis Saturday, then okonomiyaki" is exactly why the naïve invitation over-branches. The learner cannot solve it by adding options — they must *state one time*, *name the activity*, and *leave a door*. The lesson's constraint is the character's real life.

### Ch 12 — reservoir `l1-l17`–`l1-l18` · transfer: *describe what is present and ask how many are needed*
- **Language functions:** existence あります / います; counters (〜つ, 〜まい for handout photos/plates, 〜にん for people); adverbs of quantity (もっと, 〜が たりない); offers and requests (〜を ください / 〜て ください / 〜ましょうか).
- **Activity types:** vocabulary (tableware, food, counters) → grammar (existence + counter + quantity) → **listening/speaking** transfer in a live service scramble (offer what's here, ask what's short).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** Sam feeds the table and shares the handout photo. In a failed service plan he *has* to inventory and offer — the counting isn't a drill, it's him being the person he is. "Drinks already on the table" becomes ここに おちゃが みっつ あります、あと ふたつ たりません.

### Ch 28 — reservoir: N3 conditions / formal request / scope / refusal (chapters 28–30 family)
- **Language functions:** conditional 〜たら / 〜ば / 〜なら (framing an offer's terms); keigo requests 〜て いただけますか / 〜て もらえますか; **scope-setting** (この けんに ついては… / 〜の はんいで); and **respectful refusal / boundary-holding** — both producing a polite no and *accepting* one.
- **Activity types:** grammar (conditional + request keigo) → **listening** (detect a softened, indirect "not yet") → **writing** (compose one scoped consent request) → **transfer** (ask once; accept the answer without re-asking).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** consent is Sam's whole growth. The scene needs the exact grammar that lets a warm person set scope and hold a boundary — asking clearly enough that a "no" is easy to give and easy to hear.

### Ch 34 — reservoir: N2 formal event / role negotiation (chapters 34–36 family)
- **Language functions:** role negotiation and volunteering; offering **with an explicit opt-out** (むりを しなくても いいですよ / できる はんいで); spontaneous repair.
- **Activity types:** **listening** (a negotiation without pressure) → **speaking** (offer a role and name the exit) → transfer (redistribute a public job so quiet participation still counts).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** Sam who once re-asked now must build the *out* into the sentence. The grammar of "only if you want to" is the audible proof of his growth.

### Ch 46 — reservoir: integrated N1 public inquiry (chapters 46–48 family)
- **Language functions:** audience-aware invitation; live listening-and-response; transparent repair after a misunderstanding.
- **Activity types:** integrated **speaking + listening** production (one clean invitation into a live room; repair on the fly).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** the "okonomiyaki and tennis afterwards" open door finally becomes a single, landable invitation at full ability — the arc's language and its heart resolve in the same sentence.

*(Ch 39 callback appearance draws on the N2/N1 keigo-concession-refusal family: decline the venue's cleaner plan with one clear alternative — the "polite no" as a produced skill.)*

## 4. Elective bond route — CANDIDATE (`ext-sam-grill-invitation`, not yet `bond-authored`)

> **Status flag:** No character is `bond-authored` in the current manifest, and the authoring plan recommends continuity-only for the first pass. This route is a *candidate sketch* for owner review — Sam is a strong candidate because the evidence already seeds `ext-sam-grill-invitation` (N5) and his food/tennis/consent material is unusually rich. It ships only once a separate reviewed manifest entry (six ordered packages, fallback lines, voice-card revision, owner correction path) passes. Each appointment names activity, place, mode, and an easy defer, and never displays affection points.

Anchored in his real interests: okonomiyaki, Saturday tennis, feeding the group, "ともだち" warmth. The route's spine is `ext-sam-grill-invitation`'s own logline — *invite Sam, then move the time once, keep the plan* — inverted so the **learner** practices consent-shaped Japanese.

1. **Recognition (ordinary task).** Sam is short one plate at the okonomiyaki grill; the learner counts and requests what's missing. *Function:* existence + counters + 〜を ください (ホットプレートが ひとつ、おさらが ごまい たりません).
2. **A different route (his method).** Sam plans the grill night by *feeding first, deciding later* — the learner sees invitation-by-generosity rather than invitation-by-schedule, and drafts the invite his way. *Function:* ませんか / ましょうか + 〜から〜まで opening.
3. **Reciprocity.** Saturday tennis runs long; the learner must **move the time once** and keep the plan intact rather than cancel. *Function:* 〜たら / 〜なら conditional rescheduling (おそく なったら、〜に しましょう).
4. **Friction.** Sam, being Sam, wants to re-ask a quiet friend who already said "maybe not." The learner and Sam disagree about the second ask; neither position is flattered. *Function:* indirect refusal comprehension + そのままで いい (leave the "maybe" as an answer).
5. **Repair.** They rebuild the night so the friend can drop in with zero pressure — one invitation, one door, no chase. *Function:* offering with an opt-out (むりを しないで、きたく なったら きて).
6. **Future.** Sam proposes a standing okonomiyaki-after-tennis as an open invitation to the class — named, bounded, decline-friendly. *Function:* recurring-plan invitation + まいしゅう / 〜ごとに frequency.

Friction and repair show as *changed work* (the redesigned night), never a confession. Wrong Japanese triggers repair, not a colder Sam.
