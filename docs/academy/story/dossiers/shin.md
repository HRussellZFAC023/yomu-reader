## 1. Who they really are

Shin — also spelled **Xin** — is the class's kanji wizard, and the joke that follows him everywhere is that he is *right*. Point at the most intimidating character on a menu, a poster, a museum placard, and he tilts his head, half-smiles, and says 「その漢字、簡単だよ」 — *that kanji? easy* — and then, annoyingly, it is. He does not memorise characters as flat pictures; he reads them like a picture book, cracking a scary compound into tiny stories (this radical is a person, this one is a roof, so together it's someone under shelter). Watching him decode is the pleasure — the visible delight in components is his signature, not the answer at the end.

The in-universe reason he "knows all the kanji" is warm and real: **Shin is a heritage reader of Chinese characters** (the owner note trails off *"as he is from…"*). This is a genuine head start on *shape and meaning* — but it is emphatically **not** a head start on Japanese *readings*, okurigana, or nuance, and that gap is the engine of his whole arc. A character he can read on sight in one language can carry an unfamiliar 音読み, a false-friend meaning, or a politeness register he has to *learn* rather than *recognise*. So his confidence is honest at the level of form and quietly overclaims at the level of use. The class both leans on him (he's an informal tutor, in the same lane as Sophie and Xingyu) and teases him, and both are affection.

He is one of the **majime crew** in spirit — dedicated, a little smug about the smaller ambitious class — but he still confesses to skipping homework like everyone else. Real texture the chapter authors may use, warmly and lightly:

- **Ramen is his one word in the likes circle.** There's a canonical *ramen-before-class* ritual — he'd rather decode the shop's hand-brushed wall menu than order off the picture card. Food is where his kanji obsession and his appetite are the same hobby.
- **He has a girlfriend.** Keep it a settled, unremarkable background fact — warmth, never gossip, never a plot lever. (Romance in Yomu Academy is HelloTalk-route only and never in-class; Shin's bond is strictly tutor/friend.)
- **Totoro / Ghibli fan**, and he **visited the Nintendo Museum** — both are diegetic interest hooks, not trivia: they exist to generate a task (describe a favourite, explain an exhibit, compare a character).
- **Sprite direction:** some looks without glasses; keep a unique prop that is *his* and not a default hat/earring set. His accessory home is small and kanji-adjacent (a component note-card, a favourite mechanical pencil) rather than a costume.
- **Group texture:** he's the one who takes the entrance photo "so nobody gets lost," and he's *Kanji-7 at the table* — the reliable seat where the hard character gets solved.

The reconciliation with the anchor is clean: his *delight in components* is the surface; the *arc is learning to expose uncertainty and show his route* rather than dropping a finished answer. Every real detail above deepens that and none of it contradicts the four-season shape.

## 2. Four-season arc (his five continuity beats)

Shin's throughline is the cleanest "expert humbled into honesty" arc in the cast: he begins by *knowing*, and he ends by *showing his working and naming what he cannot know*. The seasons literally take his skill — decoding marks on a surface — and keep raising the stakes until decoding alone is not enough and provenance, permission, and uncertainty are.

**Beat 1 — Arrival / LEAD, Ch 8 `s1e08-menu-without-pictures` (Food, counters, kanji; expertise includes visible uncertainty).**
The class hits a pictureless menu and stalls. Shin arrives as the answer man — reads it "like a picture book," breaks each dish into components aloud, and the room relaxes. The chapter's job is that even here his expertise *carries a visible uncertainty*: he nails 肉料理 (meat dish) instantly but hedges on a reading or a preparation he's only half-sure of, and — crucially — he can decode *what a dish is* but not *what a given person wants*. His real dietary-clue beat lives here: 「肉があるね — お肉がだめな人、何か頼める？」 *("There's 肉 — anything a non-meat-eater could order?")* His skill only becomes useful when he pairs it with someone else's preference. Seeds the running joke; seeds his delight-in-components voice.

**Beat 2 — Contribution, Ch 18 `s1e18-the-memory-card-museum` (Stasi's funniest mnemonic proves false; kanji evidence and revision — accuracy need not flatten expression).**
Stasi's best, funniest mnemonic turns out to be *wrong*. Shin is the one with the evidence to correct it — and the chapter's tension is that his correction must **not flatten the joke**. His contribution matures the Ch 8 skill: he doesn't just say "wrong," he *revises the story* so the mnemonic survives with a true reading attached. This is the first time his decomposition serves someone else's expression rather than his own scoreboard. His running joke is present but earns its first crack: the "easy" character here was one where his first instinct needed evidence.

**Beat 3 — Limit, Ch 26 `s3e02-caption-without-owner` (Rose reveals the old caption; Henry finds no author field. Nominalization and source description; provenance is absence, not a puzzle shortcut).**
The heart of his arc. An old handwritten caption surfaces and everyone turns to Shin — *he* reads anything. And he *can* read the characters… but reading the marks does not tell him **who wrote them or why it was left unfinished.** For the first time the answer man hits a wall that decomposition cannot break: provenance is an *absence*, not a locked box he can crack. His consent note — *expertise includes visible uncertainty* — becomes plot. The comedy 「その漢字、簡単だよ」 is **suspended from this beat** (callback load rule: a comedy callback is dead from the first refusal/failure until the owner changes behaviour). He has to say, out loud, the thing his whole identity resisted: *I can read it. I don't know where it came from.*

**Beat 4 — Return, Ch 35 `s3e11-names-in-the-margin` (Paper, ink, version traces show the page accreted across several classes. Source comparison and qualified conclusion; the mystery shifts from author to stewardship).**
The class *heard* his limit, and the ensemble hands the decoding problem back to him in the right shape. Now his skill is aimed not at meaning but at **evidence**: comparing hands, inks, stroke habits, and version traces across the page to reach a *qualified* conclusion — "these margins were added by at least three different writers, at different times; I can't name them, but I can order them." This is his growth made mechanical: same decomposition muscle, now producing a *hedged* finding instead of a triumphant answer. The joke can re-enter here **transformed** — "easy" now means "the *reading* is easy; the *history* is not," and he says it himself.

**Beat 5 — Future, Ch 47 `s4e11-atlas-closes` (Rie confirms she made the blank template; an earlier learner added the first route; later classes layered the rest. Final synthesis and uncertainty statement; provenance resolves without claiming every mark).**
Payoff. The provenance resolves exactly to the level the evidence supports and **no further** — and Shin is the person who can state that with integrity, because he learned to. He delivers a final synthesis that *names what is known and leaves the unclaimable marks unclaimed*. The man who opened the story with 「簡単だよ」 closes it by being comfortable saying 「ここまでは分かる。これ以上は分からない」 (*this much we know; beyond this we don't*) — and it lands as mastery, not defeat.

**Callbacks he rides (he owns none in the ledger).** Shin is not a listed owner of any plot callback. His arc runs *alongside* `callback:useful-number-notebook` (Sophie/Rose/Henry) — "provenance is concluded only to the level supported by the record" at Ch 47 is his Future beat's substance — and touches `callback:paper-cat` (Tom/Felix/Stasi) at Ch 35 where physical marks become version evidence. Author him as the *reader of the evidence* those families produce; do not create a new plot-callback seed for him (budget/ownership rules forbid it).

## 3. The Japanese his story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

**Speciality:** kanji as *decomposable, decodable form* — reading by components; distinguishing a character's **shape/meaning** (his heritage strength) from its **Japanese reading, register, and nuance** (the learner's real work). His scenes make the learner practise *how to attack an unknown character* and, later, *how to state the limits of what a source proves.* His pedagogical gift to the game is the honest gap between "I recognise this" and "I can use this correctly."

**Lead-chapter reservoir (canon):** Ch 8 → **`l1-l12`**, *"express likes and preferences around a pictureless menu"*; grounded location `ramen` / `cafe`. Kanji set the scene surfaces: **肉 料理 野 半 大 小** (plus 魚・野菜 for contrast).

Per-beat learning contract (function → activity → why the real texture makes the Japanese *necessary*):

- **Beat 1 · Ch 8 (`l1-l12`).**
  *Functions:* express likes/preferences (〜が好きです / きらい), food & counters ordering (ひとつ・ふたつ / ラーメン一杯), and **kanji-by-component decoding** of a picture-free menu; a negotiated dietary request (「肉が入っていないもの、ありますか」).
  *Activity types:* **kanji** (component decomposition drill) → **vocabulary** (food) → **speaking/transfer** (order aloud with a counter; ask for a no-meat option).
  *Why it's real:* the ramen-before-class ritual gives an authentic reason to read a hand-brushed wall menu with no pictures. Shin models the *route* ("肉 is the meat radical → this dish has meat → so a non-meat-eater needs another line"); the learner then transfers it to order for themselves. The scene proves the point of the beat: decoding form is useless until it meets a *preference* — so the learner must supply the 好き.

- **Beat 2 · Ch 18 (kanji-evidence family; nearest registered content `l2-l23`–`l2-l25`, "use kanji in a useful message").**
  *Functions:* **kanji evidence and revision** — confirm/correct a reading, attach a *true* reading to a memorable mnemonic without destroying it.
  *Activity types:* **kanji** (reading verification) + **writing** (rewrite a mnemonic so it stays funny and becomes correct).
  *Why it's real:* Stasi's mnemonic is *better* comedy than the truth; the learner practises the delicate move of *correcting without flattening* — a genuinely N4 social-language skill (soften, credit, then fix).

- **Beat 3 · Ch 26 (N3 family, `content contract` rows 25–27: reported speech / implication / paraphrase).**
  *Functions:* **nominalization** (〜こと／〜の) and **source description** — describe *what a source is and is not* ("これは誰が書いたか分からない", "作者が書いてある場所がない").
  *Activity types:* **reading** (decode the caption) + **speaking/writing transfer** (state an *absence* precisely).
  *Why it's real:* Shin can read every character and still cannot answer "who / why." The learner is forced past decoding into *describing a gap* — language for "the author field is missing," not "here is the answer." This is where reading-comprehension confidence meets the limit of comprehension.

- **Beat 4 · Ch 35 (N2 family, rows 34–36: source comparison / permission scope).**
  *Functions:* **source comparison and qualified conclusion** — compare handwriting/ink/version traces and produce a *hedged* finding (「〜と思われる」「少なくとも三人」「断定はできないが」).
  *Activity types:* **reading/analysis** + **speaking** (present a calibrated conclusion) + **writing** (a bounded claim).
  *Why it's real:* his heritage eye for stroke habits is a plausible in-world reason he can *order* the writers without *naming* them — the learner practises evidential hedging on concrete physical evidence rather than abstract debate.

- **Beat 5 · Ch 47 (N1 integrated, rows 46–48).**
  *Functions:* **final synthesis + uncertainty statement** — 「ここまでは確かだ／これ以上は分からない」; claim exactly what the record supports.
  *Activity types:* **transfer/production** (extended spoken/written synthesis that *withholds* the unprovable).
  *Why it's real:* the whole game has trained the learner to want the answer; Shin's payoff models the mastery of *stopping at the evidence*. The person who said "easy" first is the one who can now say "I don't know" with authority.

**Class-thread / transfer flavour** (any season): a kanji-help exchange where Shin gives the learner a *route* not an answer ("look — what's the left half? now the right?"), so the learner decodes, then teaches it back. This satisfies the Ch-8 function in a changed context without re-gating the lesson.

## 4. Elective bond route — **PROPOSAL ONLY (not yet `bond-authored`)**

> Shin is `continuity-only` in the matrix; this route is a sketch for a future reviewed `bond-authored` manifest entry, not a live appointment. Three required Fire-Emblem-style steps map on as marked: **Recognition (A1) · Friction (A4) · Support (A6)**. All exchanges answered in Japanese; strictly platonic (he has a girlfriend; romance is HelloTalk-route only).

1. **A1 · Recognition — "Read this with me" (ordinary task).** Learner brings a kanji they can't crack; Shin teaches the *component route* instead of the answer. *Function:* ask for/receive an explanation; describe parts of a whole (これは何ですか／この部分は…). *Hanamaru on a correct self-decode.*
2. **A2 · The wall menu (different route).** Ramen-before-class; the learner must order for the group off a pictureless menu using Shin's method. *Function:* preferences + counters + a dietary request (肉なしで…／〜杯). Reciprocity begins — the learner does the reading this time.
3. **A3 · Museum placard (reciprocity).** Totoro/Ghibli or a Nintendo-Museum exhibit: Shin explains a placard, then asks the learner to explain their *own* favourite back to him. *Function:* describe and compare a favourite; give one reason.
4. **A4 · Friction — "That's not the reading" (required friction).** Shin confidently gives a reading; the learner (or evidence) shows it's a false friend from his heritage language. *Function:* soft correction / disagreement (〜じゃなくて〜だと思います). His 「簡単だよ」 is the thing that trips him — comedy suspended at this beat.
5. **A5 · Repair — "Show me your route" (repair).** He owns the miss and does the arc's core move: explains *how* he got there so the error is legible, not hidden. *Function:* narrate a process / nominalize a mistake (〜と思ったのは、〜だから). Learner acknowledges + hedges with him.
6. **A6 · Support — "Some kanji, no history" (future).** Callback to the caption work: Shin and the learner read something old together and comfortably reach a *qualified* conclusion. *Function:* calibrated certainty + future intention (ここまでは分かる／これから調べたい). Optional payoff line, fallback-safe.

## 5. Comedy & callbacks

**Owned running joke:** 「その漢字、簡単だよ」 — *"that kanji? easy."* It always, annoyingly, is. Companion beats: **ramen paired with unexpectedly hard nuance** (the food is simple, the sentence about the food is not) and **effortless kanji recall**. He owns **no plot-callback family** in the ledger, so treat the joke as a *comedy thread* on his own five beats, obeying the ledger's comedy rule (suspended from the first failure/refusal until he changes behaviour):

- **Seed — Ch 8:** 「簡単だよ」 lands as pure delight; he decodes the menu and is right. (Also his dietary-clue line establishes the *hard-nuance* half.)
- **Echo — Ch 18:** the joke gets its first friendly crack — an "easy" character needed *evidence* before it was safe to correct Stasi.
- **Suspend — Ch 26:** the joke goes **dead**. Faced with the ownerless caption, "easy" would be a lie; he cannot say it. This is the refusal/failure beat that silences the comedy per load rules.
- **Transform — Ch 35:** it returns *changed* — "easy" now means "the **reading** is easy; the **history** is not," said by Shin himself. Meaning has moved, not merely recurred.
- **Payoff — Ch 47:** he closes the atlas with 「ここまでは分かる。これ以上は分からない」 — the inversion of his opening line, delivered as mastery.

**Real-world callback the learner may earn (Japan / ramen-counter):** at a ramen counter in Japan, Shin (or a message from him) asks 「あの漢字、まだ覚えてる？」 — *"still remember that kanji?"* — paying off the very first menu he decoded. Use it as warmth, not a comprehension gate; fallback line required for learners who skipped the seed.

**Cross-links (rides, does not own):** `callback:useful-number-notebook` (provenance to the level the record supports, Ch 47) and `callback:paper-cat` (marks become version evidence, Ch 35). Author Shin as the *reader* of that evidence.
