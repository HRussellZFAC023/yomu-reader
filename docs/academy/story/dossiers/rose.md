## 1. Who they really are

Rose has brown hair, has done farm work, and lived in Japan before this class ever met. She was added to the cast after the original brief — and that late arrival is itself in-character: she enters the story as someone who was already elsewhere, already experienced, and slots in without needing the room to catch her up.

She is the class's **grounded one**. Where Henry reaches for a mechanism, Sophie for a distinction, and Jodi for two overlapping memories, Rose reaches for **the object in front of her** — what the material world will actually support — and only then widens into the long view. Her canon voice anchor is exact and load-bearing: *"grounded practical observation followed by long-view comparison; identifies material evidence and keeps several memories in the atlas; nature, work, lived comparison; solves oddly practical problems."* Everything below deepens that anchor rather than replacing it.

Two real threads feed one voice:

- **Farm work** gives her the concrete register — seasons, weather, soil, tools, doing things in the right order, preparing before you need to, not wasting words while your hands are busy. This is why her Japanese *starts* physical: she describes what she can see, touch, and sequence before she describes what she thinks.
- **A past life in Japan** gives her the reflective register — the *long-view comparison* half of the anchor. She has a lived "before" to compare the present against, so she is the character who says *"this is how it was / this is how it is now"* without dramatizing either. Farm work + lived memory of Japan are the two ends of her arc, and the story's job is to connect the practical to the reflective in her mouth.

Her running joke is warm and reliable: **she produces surprisingly practical solutions.** The class over-thinks a problem — projection mapping, version control, whose-memory-is-canon debates — and Rose quietly points out that the paper is the wrong weight, or that you could just walk the route in the order the work would actually happen. It lands because it deflates cleverness with observation, never with put-down. (This must stay affectionate: she is the person who is *right about the small physical thing*, not the person who makes others feel foolish.)

Voice contrast to preserve (from VOICE-AND-DIALOGUE): she is paired with **Jodi** — both compare accounts, but *Jodi holds remembered and present detail together at once, while Rose tests what the material world supports.* Rhythmically **Rose begins concrete and widens slowly** (Jodi layers two times immediately). Keep that rhythm: Rose's turns open on a physical noun and only reach comparison at the end.

Her binding consent note: **practical evidence does not authorize other people's stories.** This is the ethical spine of her whole arc — she is the class's best evidence-reader, and the drama is that being right about the *material* does not make her right about the *meaning*, or entitle her to narrate someone else's memory. Do not let her competence tip into authority over people.

## 2. Four-season arc (her five continuity beats)

Rose's deeper arc: **the material-evidence reader who learns that physical proof settles provenance but never authorship.** She moves from "I can see what's true here" to "I can say exactly how far the evidence reaches, and no further." Concretely, beat by beat:

**Beat 1 — Arrival · Ch 20 · `s1e20-map-from-memory` (she LEADS).**
The rebuilt route has to be reconstructed from several people's half-memories. The clever instinct is to restore one perfect canonical copy. Rose instead **gathers the overlapping accounts and keeps them all**, laying them side by side the way you'd compare this year's field to last year's. Learning/relationship job: *change, work, comparison, and sequence; shared memory stays plural.* Her farm register drives it — she treats the map as work to be sequenced, not a truth to be voted on. This is also the **echo of `callback:useful-number-notebook`**: a number/account is *counted with its context, not voted into being the answer.*

**Beat 2 — Contribution · Ch 26 · `s3e02-caption-without-owner`.**
In the atlas backing, Rose **reveals the old unattributed caption** — the one the projection never displayed (seeded at the Season 2 end state). Henry searches for a reliable author field and finds none. Rose's contribution is exactly her competence: she found the *thing*, physically, where clever tooling missed it. Learning job: *nominalization and source description; provenance is absence, not a puzzle shortcut.* The scene's whole point is that the object exists but its owner does not — her first collision with her own consent note.

**Beat 3 — Limit · Ch 33 · `s3e09-what-we-can-say`.**
Alongside Jodi, Nanako, and Mira, the class separates **memory, permission, and public claim**. Rose's *Limit* is the hardest one for her personality: she can prove what the page is made of, when it was written, and in what order — and that is **still not permission to tell the person's story.** Her practical certainty runs into a wall it cannot climb. Learning/relationship job: *evidential language, hedging, and pragmatic messages; lived experience is not automatic publication permission.* The ensemble does not punish her for wanting to publish what she found — it changes its action to hold the line with her.

**Beat 4 — Return · Ch 35 · `s3e11-names-in-the-margin`.**
Rose returns changed: she now aims her material-evidence method at the *right* question. **Paper, ink, and version traces** show the page accumulated across several classes — the mystery flips from *who is the author* to *how was this stewarded.* Learning job: *source comparison and qualified conclusion.* This is her method vindicated **within its proper limit**, and it is the chapter where her physical-evidence eye also powers the **transform of `callback:paper-cat`** (paper, ink, and placement become version evidence, not a mascot) — she owns the reading even though the callback belongs to Tom/Felix/Stasi.

**Beat 5 — Future · Ch 47 · `s4e11-atlas-closes`.**
Rie confirms she made the blank template; an earlier learner added the first route; later classes layered the rest. Rose's evidence map is what lets the class **conclude provenance only to the level the record supports** — this is the **payoff of `callback:useful-number-notebook`** (provenance is concluded exactly as far as the notebook can defend, no further). Learning job: *final synthesis and uncertainty statement; provenance resolves without claiming every mark.* Her open future: she is the person who taught the class to say "here is what we can establish, and here is where we stop," and she leaves with that intact — no forced departure, no tidy biography.

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

**Rose's learning speciality: evidential and material Japanese — the language of physical observation, sequenced/prepared work, and *calibrated claims about sources.*** She is the class's bridge from concrete N4 sequencing to N2/N1 evidentiality: she teaches the learner to (a) describe what the physical world supports, (b) say only as much as the evidence allows, and (c) mark the exact border between *observed / heard / inferred.* Her farm register makes the concrete end authentic; her lived-Japan memory makes the comparison end authentic. Every beat makes the learner *need* a piece of this ladder.

**Beat 1 — Ch 20 · reservoir `l2-l16`–`l2-l18` · transfer: "prepare in advance, sequence action, and say enough without narrating everything."**
- Language functions the scene forces: **te-form sequencing** (〜てから, 〜たあとで), **preparation-in-advance** (〜ておく), and **economical description** (say enough, don't narrate every step). Plus **change/comparison** vocabulary (work verbs, before/now).
- Activity type: **grammar + speaking (production) with a reading/transfer close.** The learner reconstructs the route by ordering steps and stating what was prepared — then must *stop* at the point the accounts agree, practising restraint, not completeness.
- Why the real texture creates the need: farm work is literally sequenced, prepared labour — you 〜ておく before the season, you do things 〜てから the ground is ready. Rose's hands-busy economy is *why* "say enough without narrating everything" is a real speaking constraint here, not a style note.

**Beat 2 — Ch 26 · reservoir (N3 family 25–27: reported speech / implication / paraphrase / register-preserving edit) · transfer: nominalization and source description.**
- Language functions: **nominalization** (こと / の / 〜という + noun) and **source description** — naming *where information came from* when there is no owner (〜によって書かれた, 誰が書いたか分からない, 出所). Provenance-as-absence.
- Activity type: **grammar + reading + writing.** The learner reads the ownerless caption and writes a description of it that nominalizes the content ("the thing that was written here") without inventing an author.
- Why the real texture creates the need: Rose *physically found* the caption. The learner inherits her problem — the object is concrete, the author is absent — so the Japanese must describe a source that cannot be pointed to. That gap is what makes nominalization and source-description necessary rather than decorative.

**Beat 3 — Ch 33 · reservoir (N2 family 31–33: quotation / translation choice / evidentiality / hedging) · transfer: evidential language, hedging, pragmatic messages.**
- Language functions: **evidentiality** — distinguishing observation, hearsay, and inference (〜そうだ hearsay, 〜らしい, 〜ようだ / 〜みたい, 〜と聞いた) — and **hedging** (〜かもしれない, 〜と思う, 〜のではないか).
- Activity type: **listening + speaking with a writing check.** The learner hears/reads several accounts of the page and must *tag each claim* by its evidence type, then compose a pragmatic message that states only what is warranted.
- Why the real texture creates the need: Rose can prove the *material* facts but hits the wall that lived memory ≠ publication permission. The learner is forced to separate "I observed this ink" from "I was told this story" from "I infer this meaning" — the exact grammatical machinery of evidentiality — because Rose's Limit *is* that boundary.

**Beat 4 — Ch 35 · reservoir (N2 family 34–36: formal event language / source comparison / permission scope) · transfer: source comparison and qualified conclusion.**
- Language functions: **source comparison** (〜によると, comparative structures across records) and **qualified conclusion** (calibrating 〜に違いない vs 〜かもしれない vs 〜と考えられる).
- Activity type: **reading + writing (kanji-supported).** The learner compares paper/ink/version traces across records and writes a conclusion that is qualified to the strength of the evidence — the mystery shifts author → stewardship in the *phrasing*, not just the plot.
- Why the real texture creates the need: this is Rose's method at full power. Comparing two fields' soil to judge a season is the same act as comparing two ink samples to judge a page — her lived, physical comparison instinct is the authentic engine for "according to X… therefore, to this degree, we can conclude…"

**Beat 5 — Ch 47 · reservoir (integrated N1 family 46–48) · transfer: final synthesis and uncertainty statement.**
- Language functions: **N1 synthesis** and **explicit uncertainty statement** — nominalized abstract claims, concessive structures (〜わけではない, 〜とはいえ), and calibrated certainty across a whole record.
- Activity type: **integrated reading + speaking (extended production).** The learner synthesizes three layered accounts into one conclusion that names its own limits — "we can establish this much; these marks remain unattributed."
- Why the real texture creates the need: Rose's whole arc has trained the learner to stop exactly where the evidence stops. The finale demands the highest-band form of that discipline, and her notebook payoff is the thing that makes the uncertainty statement *earned* rather than a hedge.

**Reservoir tie-through:** Ch 20 draws `l2-l16`–`l2-l18` (sequence/preparation). Ch 26 → N3 25–27. Ch 33 → N2 31–33. Ch 35 → N2 34–36. Ch 47 → integrated N1 46–48. Rose owns `callback:useful-number-notebook` (echo Ch 20 → transform Ch 38 → payoff Ch 47) and drives the `callback:paper-cat` transform reading at Ch 35.
