## 1. Who they really are

Rie is the sensei — slightly older than the class, charming, and the still point the whole room turns around. Her defining move, and the thing the canon anchor already names, is that she watches for **what lets the room keep going** and hands you one usable repair *before* she explains the grammar behind it. She teases; she never scolds. Her ambition for a class is small and total at once: everyone should leave a little lighter than they came in, and not one student should be dropped while she keeps every plate spinning.

The real texture the owner authorized deepens that anchor instead of fighting it:

- **She saved the learner a seat before they arrived.** This is not a garnish — it is literally `callback:open-chair`, the story's arrival bridge. The real detail (she kept a chair for you) *is* the mechanism that lets a stranger enter without owing anyone a backstory. Canon and evidence agree here; use it verbatim as the emotional seed of Chapter 1.
- **Tea that goes cold.** She keeps a cup at her elbow and quietly lets it cool while she keeps every corner of the room moving. That is `callback:cold-tea`, seeded in Chapter 1. It reads as warmth, not martyrdom — she is not suffering, she is *busy being kind*.
- **Too many jobs.** The running joke that she seemingly works nine jobs — you spot her on the midnight konbini till, and later at the station kiosk — is her signature comedy engine. Play it as the owner framed it: *always delighted, always exhausted, always kind.* She is never embarrassed to be caught working; she waves like it's the best surprise of the night.
- **Natto, tea, cup noodles.** Her home-cooking-and-comfort palette. Natto she makes herself; tea she loves; cup noodles she is unashamed of. These are her go-to concrete referents when a lesson needs an ordinary object to name, describe, or offer.
- **The 花丸 (hanamaru).** She grades by hand-drawing a flower-circle. This is her, and it doubles as the game's completion stamp — when the learner finishes a task, *Rie's* mark is what appears. Her feedback is a drawn flower, not a red X.
- **The dead marker.** Her whiteboard marker gives up on 「どこ」 again — a tiny, real, affectionate prop that turns a vocabulary word into a recurring gag.
- **Remembers what each student is scared of.** Small acts of care are her love language; she knows which learner freezes on which sound and pre-empts it.
- **Funniest person in the room at the pub.** Off the teaching surface she is the one who lands the joke. The pub-with-sensei scene is a special unlock; when it happens, let the procedural teacher relax into the wittiest voice at the table.

The one piece of private biography — *her husband still can't read kanji* — stays strictly as **her own self-deprecating gag**, volunteered by Rie, never narrated as fact about a real marriage (see §6). She tells it to make a beginner feel better: even the people closest to her sensei can't read this, so relax.

**Reconciliation with the anchor:** none of the real detail contradicts the canon movement. The nine jobs and the cold tea are the *outward* form of "notices what lets the room continue"; her arc — learning to accept a handoff and let students own the class — is exactly what a perpetually-overstretched, everyone-else-first sensei would have to learn. Under pressure she becomes *more* procedural (canon), which is the busy-sensei reflex turned up, not a personality swap.

## 2. Four-season arc across her five beats

Her deeper arc is **constancy that finally learns to be relieved**: she anchors every route stage, then, across four seasons, moves from *doing every job herself* to *accepting a specific handoff, sharing authorship, and closing the atlas without finishing her students for them.*

### Beat 1 — Arrival · Chapter 1 `s1e01-the-blank-atlas` (LEAD)
She introduces the stalled lantern-atlas exhibition and its one empty route, and she unlocks as a character. The real details do the heavy lifting: the **open chair she saved** lets the learner enter; her **tea goes cold** as she keeps the room moving; she gives the learner's first repaired greeting a **花丸**. She also delivers the opening AI-fiction disclaimer once (canon: it is her spoken notice, replayable in About/Credits). Seeds `callback:open-chair`, `callback:cold-tea`, and `callback:first-lantern` (one repaired greeting lights one route point). This is her at full anchor: one usable repair, then the explanation.

### Beat 2 — Contribution · Chapter 3 `s1e03-route-zero`
Aakash leads the route test; automatic labels vanish. **Rie's contribution is register and permission** — she is the one who converts panic into a bounded, askable action. When the 「どこ」 label dies (and so, on cue, does her marker), she models the exact phrase to *ask where something is* and *ask permission to fix it*, so "asking for help becomes competence" (canon job). Her competence here is not knowing the map — it's knowing the sentence that lets a beginner request help without shame. Echoes `callback:open-chair` as a useful action (the seat she kept now has a current use).

### Beat 3 — Limit · Chapter 16 `s1e16-the-night-the-map-went-dark`
Angel's literal tool instruction erases the visible route; the map goes dark. **This is Rie's limit: she takes on one job too many and becomes procedural** (canon pressure behavior). Faced with the blackout she tries to hold *everything* — the display, the class's morale, the lesson — herself, and the seams show. The class preserves the knowledge through **teach-back** rather than by waiting for sensei to fix it single-handed. Her growth is not yet complete; the limit is simply visible. The nine-jobs comedy is **suspended** here — you do not joke about her many jobs in the beat where being over-extended is the actual wound.

### Beat 4 — Return · Chapter 34 `s3e10-empty-microphone`
A public-event host drops out and speaking roles must be redistributed without coercion. **Rie returns changed: she accepts help.** This is the transform of `callback:cold-tea` — several people offer to help and, instead of saying she's fine, *she assigns jobs.* She parcels out the microphone, the reception, the backstage cue; she does not swallow the gap herself. The class learns quiet roles count as full participation, and Rie learns that delegating is not abandoning her students. Her Return proves the ensemble heard the Chapter-16 limit.

### Beat 5 — Future · Chapter 47 `s4e11-atlas-closes`
Rie confirms she made the original blank atlas template as an early teaching exercise; an earlier learner added the first route; later classes layered the rest. **She closes the atlas without claiming every mark** — final synthesis with an explicit uncertainty statement. This is the payoff of `callback:first-lantern` (the ordinary mechanism is named; the magic was people coordinating) and it sets up the Chapter-48 payoff of `callback:cold-tea` (someone *else* closes the room while Rie finally drinks the tea before it cools) and `callback:open-chair` (a blank page offered under terms, no person assigned to fill it). Her Future is not a finished life; it's a teacher who has learned to hand the class its own authorship.

*(Texture note — Chapter 40 `s4e04-three-true-versions`: Rie is one of three people who describe the page's beginning differently, with no omniscient correction. Not one of her five scheduled beats, but her voice belongs here as the calibrated, non-defensive account.)*

## 3. The Japanese her story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

**Rie's learning speciality:** *repair, register, and feedback.* She is the class's model of natural correction — the person who shows that fixing a sentence is not rejecting the speaker, that there is a register for asking and a register for offering, and that partial output is enough to act on. Pedagogically she is the **repair-phrase engine** of the whole game: every time a scene breaks, Rie's job is to hand the learner the exact phrase that makes the break usable. Her 花丸 is the completion signal; her one-usable-repair-before-explanation is the correction model the learner internalizes.

Each beat makes specific Japanese *necessary*, not decorative:

### Chapter 1 — greetings, classroom commands, requesting repetition
- **Language function(s) the scene makes the learner NEED:** exchange greetings/self-introduction; comprehend and follow a classroom instruction; **ask for repetition/clarification** (`もう一度おねがいします`, `ゆっくりおねがいします`).
- **Activity type:** live listening + speaking (classroom scenelet); vocabulary (greetings) with immediate spoken response.
- **Lesson reservoir:** `lesson:foundation-00` + the classroom-expression support shard. Transfer function: *greet, follow an instruction, and ask for repetition in the live room.*
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** the saved chair means the learner has already been welcomed *before speaking* — so the only thing standing between them and belonging is producing one repaired greeting. The need is emotional, not abstract: say the phrase, get the 花丸, be in the room.

### Chapter 3 — location/direction, から origin, permission-asking
- **Language function(s):** ask where someone/something is from and **distinguish identity from location** (`〜は〜です` vs `〜に/で〜`); use place particles (`に`, `で`, `へ`); **ask permission** to act (`〜てもいいですか`).
- **Activity type:** grammar + speaking transfer (directions/particles), hosted as a live repair when the 「どこ」 label dies.
- **Lesson reservoir:** `l1-l03`–`l1-l04`. Transfer function: *ask where someone or something is from and distinguish identity from location.*
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** her dead marker giving up on 「どこ」 turns the vocabulary item into the scene's actual problem — the class literally cannot find where the route goes, so `どこ` and `〜てもいいですか` are the tools that reopen it. Rie's warmth makes asking-for-help the competent move, so the learner practices requesting rather than freezing.

### Chapter 16 — te-form cause/sequence, teach-back, opinion
- **Language function(s):** sequence and cause with te-form and `〜から` (`ボタンを押して、画面が消えて…`, `〜から、〜`); **teach-back** instructions (giving a procedure so others can reproduce it); compare then/now and **state an opinion** (`〜とおもいます`).
- **Activity type:** grammar + speaking (teach-back is productive output); light technology vocabulary; reading/opinion on the "then vs now" notebook.
- **Lesson reservoir:** `l2-l01` support shard (no completion gate) + `l2-l02`–`l2-l04`. Transfer function: *open the next notebook, compare then/now, and state an opinion.* Bible job: *technology and cause; the class preserves knowledge through teach-back.*
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** the map goes dark *because* Rie tried to run every job at once. The only recovery is te-form procedure spoken back by students — so her over-extension (the limit) is exactly what makes teach-back grammar necessary. The learner literally has to sequence the steps `〜て、〜て` because sensei can't hold them all alone tonight.

### Chapter 34 — role negotiation, requests/offers, register shift
- **Language function(s):** negotiate and **assign roles** in a formal-event register; offer and request help (`〜ましょうか`, `〜てもらえますか`, `おねがいできますか`); accept help gracefully; spontaneous repair.
- **Activity type:** speaking/transfer under event pressure (N2 formal register); listening for who has *not* been assigned.
- **Lesson reservoir:** Chapters 34–36 content family — *N2 formal event language, source comparison, and permission scope* (negotiate roles; four-skill proof includes "negotiate roles" orally).
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** `callback:cold-tea` transforms here — Rie *assigns jobs instead of saying she's fine.* The scene needs the grammar of delegation and acceptance (`〜てもらえますか`, not `だいじょうぶです、わたしがやります`) because her arc-beat is precisely learning to say "please take this one." The register shift is the character growth made grammatical.

### Chapter 47 — calibrated certainty, embedded quotation, synthesis
- **Language function(s):** synthesize multiple accounts; state **calibrated uncertainty/hedging** (`〜かもしれません`, `はっきりとはわかりませんが`, `〜と思います`); **embedded quotation** (`〜と言っていました`, `〜という`).
- **Activity type:** integrated N1 listening + speaking (public inquiry, synthesis); reading provenance records.
- **Lesson reservoir:** Chapters 46–48 content family — *integrated N1 public inquiry, mediation, synthesis, and graduation composition.*
- **Why the real texture forces the Japanese:** she made the template but *not* every mark, and she refuses to claim what she didn't do. That refusal is only sayable in calibrated Japanese — hedges and embedded quotation are the exact tools that let her attribute honestly ("I made this part; someone said they added that part; the rest I can't be sure of"). Her whole voice-anchor (one honest repair, no over-claim) becomes an N1 grammar lesson in provenance.

## 4. Elective bond route — "Office Hours" (bond-authored, pending manifest entry)

Rie starts closest to the learner of anyone (office-hours warmth), and the evidence carries an authored `ext-rie-office-hour` appointment, so she is sketched as `bond-authored`. Six platonic, task-named, deferrable appointments in a recurring place (the classroom after class, tea cooling on the desk). *This route requires a confirmed `bond-authored` relationship-manifest entry with six ordered packages before delivery.*

1. **Ordinary task — "Wipe the board."** The learner helps her reset the classroom; she offers tea (or cup noodles). *Function:* offering/accepting, simple `〜ましょうか`, everyday-object vocabulary (tea, natto, marker). *Reusable state:* the learner's drink/snack preference is remembered.
2. **Different route — "One thing first."** She watches the learner try to fix a whole paragraph and stops them: *"You don't have to fix everything. First, pick the one thing you want to say."* *Function:* `〜から` reasons + `〜ように` purpose; prioritizing one intention. A new support style unlocks: *repair-one-thing* pacing.
3. **Reciprocity — "The scared sound."** She remembers the exact sound the learner freezes on (her real trait: she knows what each student fears) and pre-teaches it before it can trip them. *Function:* pronunciation/repair register; a callback (that sound) enters the shared voice set as the learner's now-owned strength.
4. **Friction — "Nine jobs."** The learner, worried, suggests she's doing too much / should drop a job. Rie deflects with warmth but the crossed assumption is real: her busyness is *hers to choose*, and neither "you should rest" nor "you're fine" is the flattering answer. *Function:* softened disagreement, `〜んじゃない?` / `でも` concession; consent-shaped — one clear deflection ends the pushing.
5. **Repair — "Let me carry that."** After the friction, Rie *hands the learner a specific task* rather than absorbing it — trust shown as changed work, not a speech. Mirrors her Chapter-34 growth in miniature. *Function:* delegating requests `〜てもらえますか`; accepting help.
6. **Future — "See you at the till."** She invites the learner to actually stop by wherever she's working next (the konbini, the kiosk) — one voluntary appointment beyond class. *Function:* casual future plans + place/time; graduation/alumni wording can acknowledge the route.

Every appointment states its activity and an easy way to defer; deferring or failing an activity never makes Rie colder.

## 6. Do-not

- **Do not turn "her husband can't read kanji" into biography.** It is allowed only as **Rie's own volunteered, self-deprecating gag** to relax a beginner ("even he can't read this — so don't worry"). Never narrate it as fact about a real marriage, never spin it into relationship commentary, never name or characterize a real spouse. Canon consent note is explicit: *approved teacher role and likeness; no private biography.*
- **Do not make the nine-jobs gag pitying or embarrassing.** She is *delighted, exhausted, and kind* — never a cautionary tale about overwork, poverty, or being taken advantage of. Keep it warm; suspend it in vulnerable beats.
- **Do not let her over-extension read as martyrdom.** The cold tea is warmth-in-motion, not suffering. Her Chapter-16 limit is procedural over-reach, not a breakdown.
- **Do not have her scold.** She teases and repairs; she never shames. One-usable-repair-*before*-explanation is the fixed order.
- **Do not resolve the atlas through her alone or make her omniscient.** In Chapters 40 and 47 she gives *one true account among several* and states uncertainty; she must not claim every mark or deliver an omniscient correction of the others.
- **Do not have her finish her students.** Her whole movement is accepting handoffs and sharing authorship. Any scene where she saves everyone single-handedly reverses the arc.
- **Do not reproduce real photos, chats, itineraries, or a runtime portrait of anyone but Rie.** She is the only likeness-approved cast member, and only for the exact approved asset revision; a completed sprite never upgrades the registry.
