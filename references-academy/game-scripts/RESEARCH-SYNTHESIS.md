# Game-script craft synthesis

This document contains derived observations only. It intentionally contains no source dialogue.

## Corpus measurements

The non-adult Rosebleu subset contains 1,735 scene files and 142,161 utterance/narration records across ten games. Across titles:

- median utterance length is 27–32 Japanese characters;
- the 75th percentile is 39–47 characters;
- median scene size is 54–91 records;
- median named speakers per scene is six;
- adjacent records change speaker 71.5–83.0% of the time.

The smaller character-conversation game contains 787 records across five files. The Ojousama set contains 202 prompt/response pairs and is useful for seeing both the strength and danger of a highly marked idiolect.

These are descriptive reference points, not Yomu targets. Academy scenes share time with learning and should usually be shorter.

## Pacing

- Pokemon's map-scoped text makes most encounters complete one job quickly: orient, characterize, gate, reward, or foreshadow. Repeated verbs and nouns create natural memory without stopping for recap.
- Persona's bond ranks are discrete appointments. A rank tends to reopen a familiar situation, introduce one pressure, offer a small stance choice, and end on a changed understanding or future appointment.
- Long Japanese VN scenes earn length through rapid speaker alternation, physical micro-actions, and reversals between ordinary and consequential beats. Monologue-heavy exposition feels longer than its record count.
- Academy should use 18–36-turn lesson scenelets, 36–60-turn bond scenes, and at most 70 turns for a season hinge. A learning interaction may split a scene but must not split a sentence, emotional disclosure, or consent decision.

## Bonds and linkage

- A bond feels cumulative when each episode has a local activity and a changed relationship state; affinity numbers alone do not create continuity.
- Recurring places do useful compression. The learner sees what changed without a narrator listing it.
- The strongest links are triangular: a character goal, a practical shared action, and a pressure from the main plot. Any two can carry a quiet scene; all three belong at a turning point.
- Academy bonds must affect later phrasing, who asks for help, optional scene order, and callbacks. They must not branch the finite ending or award intimacy for guessing a preferred answer.

## Voice

- Durable voice comes from a small bundle: preferred sentence shape, attention target, social tactic, and one contradiction. Catchphrases alone flatten a person.
- Marked endings or register can identify a speaker quickly, but a line should still reveal intent when the marker is removed.
- Ensemble readability improves when adjacent speakers differ in both rhythm and what they notice. Do not alternate several characters who all summarize the same fact.
- A character's language may simplify for the learner without losing their attention pattern. Sophie's precision, Sam's inclusion, and Henry's tool-first thinking should survive every language layer.

## Callbacks

- Seed callbacks in ordinary action, echo them once without emphasis, transform them under pressure, then pay them off through changed behavior.
- Utility callbacks outperform trivia: a phrase first used to repair classroom audio can later repair a friendship or public event.
- Limit a normal scene to one new seed and two echoes. More produces self-reference instead of forward motion.
- A finale payoff should resolve a choice or relationship, not merely repeat a familiar joke.

## Scene rhythm

The reusable rhythm is: concrete arrival, immediate want, small obstruction, language-bearing attempt, response, changed action, exit image. Comedy works best as a pressure release before or after the turn, not inside a vulnerable admission.

Academy adds a pedagogy-aware variant:

1. A person wants something legible.
2. The learner encounters Japanese before being tested.
3. A choice or production changes the attempt.
4. Repair happens in the same situation.
5. The scene reacts visibly.
6. One relationship, plot, or world fact changes.
7. A later hook is planted without announcing review.

## Language-level progression

- Beginner writing needs concrete referents, recoverable ellipsis, short adjacency pairs, and repeated functional language. It does not need childish stakes.
- Intermediate writing can move the missing information off-screen: motives, conflicting plans, reported speech, and assumptions.
- Advanced writing should make uncertainty meaningful. Speakers can hedge, imply, quote selectively, or disagree about what evidence warrants, while the scene preserves more than one defensible reading.
- Difficulty should rise through syntax, inference, register, audio conditions, and production demand. It should not rise by replacing useful language with rare words.

## Anti-patterns carried into the bible

- encyclopedic NPC exposition;
- dialogue choices with one flattering answer and two punishments;
- ten bond scenes that repeat the same vulnerability;
- callbacks used as applause signs;
- plot scenes that arrive before the lesson language needed to act in them;
- every ensemble member commenting in every scene;
- a translated line masquerading as language-level adaptation;
- endless postgame plot that reverses graduation.
