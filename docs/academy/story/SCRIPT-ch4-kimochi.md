# Chapter 4 - 「きもち」 Quiet Room, Clear Request

**Core question:** A sign says "quiet room," but does a new visitor know what the room is for, whether they may enter, or how to ask for a small adjustment?
**Story change:** Nori turns a well-meant but vague sign into a clear invitation to rest, read, or ask for help.
**Language family:** `い/な` adjectives, `〜と思う`, `〜から`, `〜たことがある`, `〜てもいいですか`; bridge and N4 repair language.
**Runtime status:** planned high-N5/bridge content. No scene requires personal disclosure, speaking, or an audio route.

## S4.1 - The Sign That Sounds Like a Rule

**Location:** Quiet Table, L05.
**Narrative job:** Show that a calm space can be welcoming or forbidding depending on its wording.
**Authentic language need:** Describe the room and ask what a visitor may do there.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-quiet-room-description` - matching and `select-one` adjective practice.
**Intent ID:** `quiet.describe-room-purpose`

_The card reads only `しずか`. A first-time visitor could read it as “be quiet,” “this room is quiet,” or “do not come in.” Nori holds it at arm’s length, listening to all three meanings._

**NORI:** `この部屋は、しずかです。でも、「入って いいですか」は 書いてありません。`
_This room is quiet. But it does not say, “May I come in?”_

**RIE:** `言葉の温度を見ましょう。説明だけなら、冷たく聞こえることもあります。`
_Let us look at the temperature of the words. An explanation by itself can sometimes sound cold._

**Teaching card - describe before deciding:**

| Form           | Example                       | Practical meaning    |
| -------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------- |
| `い` adjective | `この部屋は あたたかいです。` | This room is warm.   |
| `な` adjective | `この部屋は しずかです。`     | This room is quiet.  |
| `な` + noun    | `しずかな 場所です。`         | It is a quiet place. |
| reader action  | `ここで 休んでも いいです。`  | You may rest here.   |

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                            | Bridge                                    | N4                                                 |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `ここは、しずかな 場所です。` | `ここは、少し 休める しずかな 場所です。` | `ここは、少し休みたい人が静かに過ごせる場所です。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _“Quiet” describes a room; a useful card also says what a visitor may do there._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-quiet-room-description`.

## S4.2 - An Opinion Can Be a Useful Reason

**Location:** Quiet Table.
**Narrative job:** Give Nori a way to argue for the card without turning preference into a confession.
**Authentic language need:** State what the group thinks will help a visitor and why.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-thought-and-reason` - short-text and matching practice for `と思う` and `から`.
**Intent ID:** `quiet.explain-reader-care`

_Suzu proposes `休んで ください`. It is clear. Nori reads it, then leaves a deliberate space before answering._

**NORI:** `いいと 思います。でも、「どうして」も 少し 書きたいです。`
_I think it is good. But I would like to write a little of “why,” too._

**SUZU:** `理由が あると、カードが 長く なります。`
_If there is a reason, the card becomes longer._

**RIE:** `一つの理由なら、長くありません。読む人に、ここが自分のためか分かります。`
_One reason is not too long. It helps the reader know this place may be for them._

**Explanation:**

> `ここは 役に立つと 思います。` - I think this is useful.
> `しずかだから、休めます。` - Because it is quiet, a person can rest.

`と思う` makes a considered opinion softer. `から` gives the practical reason beneath it. Neither asks the learner to disclose a feeling.

**Practice prompt:** Pair a reason with a reader action: `しずかだから` -> `ここで 少し 休めます。`

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                         | Bridge                                   | N4                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `ここは いいと 思います。` | `しずかだから、ここは いいと 思います。` | `静かに休めるので、この場所は役に立つと思います。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _An opinion plus one reason can tell a reader why an option exists._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-thought-and-reason`.

## S4.3 - A Past Experience, Kept Small

**Location:** Quiet Table card test.
**Narrative job:** Make `〜たことがある` a practical way to recommend a route, not an invitation to expose history.
**Authentic language need:** Say that a person has used a room or route before and can describe its function.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-experience-and-recommendation` - select-one and short-text response for `〜たことがある`.
**Intent ID:** `quiet.recommend-from-tested-experience`

_Leo volunteers to sit at the Quiet Table for ten minutes while the others work nearby. Afterwards, he returns with a single observation, as if reporting from a very small expedition._

**LEO:** `このテーブルを 使った ことが あります。静かで、カードが 読みやすいです。`
_I have used this table before. It is quiet, and cards are easy to read there._

**RIE:** `それは、初めての人に役に立つ経験ですね。大きい話でなくていいんです。`
_That is experience useful to a first-time person. It does not need to be a big story._

**Teaching card:**

> `この場所を 使った ことが あります。` - I have used this place before.
> `この道を 歩いた ことが あります。` - I have walked this route before.
> `一人で 休んだ ことが あります。` - I have taken a break alone before.

**Authoring boundary:** The learner may choose a fictional or neutral scenario. No answer asks whether the learner has experienced anxiety, loneliness, a diagnosis, or any private event.

**Recap if skipped:** _`〜たことがある` lets a speaker offer a small, tested recommendation without telling a life story._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-experience-and-recommendation`.

## S4.4 - The Request That Makes the Room Work

**Location:** Quiet Table and Desk.
**Narrative job:** Give a new visitor permission to ask for a small adjustment without apology theatre.
**Authentic language need:** Ask whether it is okay to rest, read, or get help.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-quiet-request` - context-appropriate reply and short-text request.
**Intent ID:** `quiet.ask-permission-and-help`

_Nori adds a final line to the card, then crosses it out. The line says `何も 話さないで ください`. It is precise, but it has closed the room._

**NORI:** `静かにしたい人もいます。でも、助けを聞く人もいます。`
_Some people want quiet. But some people also need to ask for help._

**RIE:** `では、禁止ではなく、使い方を書きましょう。`
_Then let us write how to use the room, rather than a prohibition._

**Useful request set:**

| Reader need      | Japanese                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| ask to rest      | `ここで 少し 休んでも いいですか。`        |
| ask to read      | `ここで カードを 読んでも いいですか。`    |
| ask for help     | `分からない ときは、聞いても いいですか。` |
| offer permission | `はい、もちろんです。`                     |

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                             | Bridge                              | N4                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `ここで 休んでも いいですか。` | `ここで 少し 休んでも いいですか。` | `少し休みたいのですが、ここを使ってもいいですか。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _The room works when its card says both how to rest and how to ask for help._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-quiet-request`.

## S4.5 - Optional Study Connection: Window Rehearsal

**Location:** Window Rehearsal, L07.
**Narrative job:** Let Nori practise a clear request in the safest available modality.
**Authentic language need:** Choose a request, read it, type it, or optionally record it locally.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-quiet-request`; text rehearsal is complete evidence.
**Intent ID:** `nori.rehearse-clear-boundary`

_Nori has prepared three versions of the same line. No microphone appears until a learner explicitly chooses the optional local recording path._

**NORI:** `小さい声で言う練習をしてもいいですか。`
_May I practise saying it quietly?_

**RIE:** `もちろんです。文字だけでも、同じ練習になります。`
_Of course. Text alone is the same practice._

**Learner choice - cosmetic and modality-safe:**

- Read the line silently with furigana and gloss.
- Type the line into a draft bubble.
- Record locally after explicit consent; show Delete beside the recording.

**Connection recap:** _Nori practised a request without needing an audience. The line remains available as text._
**Optional payoff:** Quiet Table Slip recap.

## S4.6 - The Quiet Card Says What It Means

**Location:** Quiet Table, chapter close.
**Narrative job:** Complete the fourth card and make Nori's boundary visible, without pathologising quiet.
**Authentic language need:** Write a concise, practical card with description, purpose, and request route.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-write-quiet-card` - long-text self-review with model and reader-care rubric.
**Intent ID:** `folio.publish-quiet-card`

_The card is now short enough to scan and specific enough to be used._

> `ここは、しずかな 場所です。少し 休んだり、カードを 読んだり できます。しずかだから、考えやすいと 思います。わからない ときは、デスクで 聞いて ください。`

**NORI:** `「しずか」だけより、入りやすいです。`
_It is easier to enter than “quiet” by itself._

**SUZU:** `説明が あります。でも、命令では ありません。`
_It explains. But it does not command._

_The folio's original pencilled note reads: `静かな場所は、説明がないと「だめ」の場所になる。` - “Without an explanation, a quiet place becomes a place you think you must not enter.” The chapter closes the question without inventing a private past for its author._

**Chapter recap:**

- `い/な` adjectives describe a space; `な` joins a noun such as `しずかな 場所`.
- `と思う` offers an opinion softly; `から` gives one practical reason.
- `〜たことがある` can offer small, tested experience without demanding a personal story.
- A clear request lets a room support both quiet and help.

**Handoff:** Campus; Chapter 5 current N4 plan rehearsal; **Continue learning** remains direct.

## Chapter 4 author notes

- Do not write a disclosure scene, a trauma metaphor, or a romance-adjacent two-hander here. The emotional movement is from vague permission to useful permission.
- Nori is allowed to want a boundary. The story must not praise them for erasing it in order to be agreeable.
- If any implementation makes recording required for the connected activity, this scene cannot claim accessibility parity. The author panel must surface that mismatch as a publish block.
- Rain, steam, and window ambience are entirely decorative and static under reduced motion. Captions/transcripts carry every relevant detail.
