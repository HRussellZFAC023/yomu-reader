# Chapter 6 - 「あたらしい話」 A Map Others Can Use

**Core question:** What does a welcome guide need in order to help a person the group has never met?
**Story change:** The Blue Door Folio's handover note resolves the mystery. The group writes the last clear sentence and tests the final guide against the fictional Open Rooms Afternoon.
**Language family:** N4 consolidation - summary, comparison, connected reasons/conditions, practical writing, revision, and self-review.
**Runtime status:** planned N4 integration content. It must not be marked runtime-publishable until matching activities, outcomes, assets, and placement references exist in `academyContentGraph`.

## S6.1 - Before the Doors Open

**Location:** Open Door Desk and fictional Open Rooms Afternoon route.
**Narrative job:** Let the group prepare for a modest event without turning it into a spectacle or deadline.
**Authentic language need:** Review a guide in the order a first-time reader needs it: where, when, who, options, fallback.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n4-guide-walkthrough` - sequencing and reader-care checklist.
**Intent ID:** `guide.test-reader-order`

_The fictional Open Rooms Afternoon is deliberately small: a Desk, a route card, a quiet table, a weather fallback, and a few low-pressure activities. The group has arrived early enough to test the guide, not early enough to worry about being impressive._

**RIE:** `今日は、足りないところを見つける日です。直せばいいので、急ぐ必要はありません。`
_Today is for finding what is missing. We can revise it, so there is no need to hurry._

**SUZU:** `読む順番は、場所、時間、デスク、選べること、雨ですね。`
_The reading order is place, time, Desk, choices, then rain._

**NORI:** `「休みたいときは」も、早いほうが いいと 思います。`
_I think “if you want a break” should appear earlier too._

**Practice prompt:** Order five guide cards for a new arrival. More than one reasonable order may be accepted if it preserves a first actionable step; the explanation tells the learner why the recommended order reduces guesswork.

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5 support                  | Bridge                                       | N4 target                                                          |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `まず、デスクへ 行きます。` | `まず、デスクへ 行って、カードを 読みます。` | `初めて来る人が迷わないように、最初にデスクと次の行動を書きます。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _The group arranged the final guide around the first actions a new arrival needs, not around the order in which the team made it._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n4-guide-walkthrough`.

## S6.2 - What Changed, Exactly?

**Location:** Card Rack.
**Narrative job:** Turn reflection into concrete comparison rather than vague celebration.
**Authentic language need:** Compare the first card with the revised guide and state why one is more useful.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n4-summary-comparison` - matching, short text, and explanation response using `より/のほうが/一番`.
**Intent ID:** `guide.compare-before-and-after`

_Rie sets the original `ここに 来て ください` card beside the final route card. They look almost comically different in size. The difference is not the number of words; it is the number of questions a reader no longer has to ask._

**RIE:** `前のカードより、今のカードのほうが、何が分かりやすいですか。`
_Compared with the earlier card, what is clearer in the current one?_

**LEO:** `今のカードのほうが、入口が分かりやすいです。目印があるからです。`
_The current card makes the entrance easier to understand because it has a landmark._

**SUZU:** `一番 大きい違いは、「次に何をするか」です。`
_The biggest difference is “what to do next.”_

**Teaching card:**

> `前のカードより、今のカードのほうが 分かりやすいです。`
> _The current card is clearer than the earlier card._
>
> `一番 大きい違いは、雨のときの場所が 書いてあることです。`
> _The biggest difference is that it names the place for rain._

**Recap if skipped:** _The group named specific improvements: a landmark, a helper, a choice, and a fallback._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n4-summary-comparison`.

## S6.3 - The Handover Note

**Location:** Blue Door Folio, back cover.
**Narrative job:** Resolve the central mystery cleanly and without turning the former visitor into a hidden character.
**Authentic language need:** Read a short practical handover note and identify its request.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n4-read-handover-note` - graded reading, gist/detail, and revision selection.
**Intent ID:** `folio.resolve-handover-note`

_As Leo straightens the folio, the back cover opens around a loose handover note. It is not locked. It was simply stuck behind the fold. The blue half-door on its back has a pencilled arrow pointing outward._

**RIE:** `最後のメモです。読む前に、何が書いてあると思いますか。`
_It is the last note. Before reading, what do you think it says?_

**The handover note - original Yomu Academy text:**

> `はじめて来た日に、わたしは どこへ行くか 分かりませんでした。`
>
> _On my first day, I did not know where to go._
>
> `だから、次の人が 迷わないように、最初の案内を 書きました。`
>
> _So I wrote the first guide so the next person would not get lost._
>
> `試す前に、ここを 離れなければ なりませんでした。`
>
> _I had to leave before I could test it._
>
> `わたしのために 終わらせないでください。次の人のために、書いてください。`
>
> _Please do not finish it for me. Write it for the next person._

_There is no signature, address, date of departure, or clue to pursue. The mystery is answered by the request itself._

**NORI:** `だから、半分のドアだったんですね。次の人が、もう半分を書けるように。`
_That is why it was a half-door. So the next person could write the other half._

**RIE:** `そうですね。これは、残された問題ではありません。渡された仕事です。`
_Exactly. This is not a problem that was left behind. It is work that was handed forward._

**Recap if skipped:** _The folio was left unfinished so a later reader could test and revise it. The author’s identity is not the story’s answer._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n4-read-handover-note`.

## S6.4 - The Last Clear Sentence

**Location:** Quiet Table.
**Narrative job:** Let the learner author a useful ending without turning it into autobiographical free writing.
**Authentic language need:** Write a short Open Rooms guide with a welcome, action, support, and fallback.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n4-open-rooms-guide` - long-text self-review; planned writing model and rubric.
**Intent ID:** `guide.write-for-next-arrival`

_Rie places a fresh card beside the final folio page. She does not take the marker._

**RIE:** `この文は、だれかの気持ちを当てる文ではありません。次の人が動ける文です。`
_This sentence is not for guessing anyone’s feelings. It is a sentence that lets the next person act._

**RIE:** `短くても大丈夫です。場所、次の行動、助け、もしもの時。その中から、必要なことを書きましょう。`
_It may be short. Place, next action, help, and what-if. Write what is needed from those._

**Writing prompt:**

> Write a 90-140 character Japanese guide for a fictional first-time Open Rooms visitor. Include:
>
> 1. where to begin and one clear action;
> 2. a helper or support action using `ように/ないように` where appropriate;
> 3. one choice or polite availability question;
> 4. a weather or schedule fallback.
>
> After drafting, use a separate self-review note to compare this guide with the first card or explain one reason it is useful. The visitor-facing guide itself should not refer to a draft they have never seen.

**Model answer - available after first attempt:**

> `入る前に、まず案内カードを読んでください。初めて来る人が迷わないように、六時にオープン・ドア・デスクへ来てください。りえ先生がデスクにいます。デスクでは、「野菜の料理はありませんか」と聞けます。雨なら、近くのカフェに変えませんか。`

**Self-review rubric - reader care, not personal disclosure:**

| Criterion                     | 0                               | 1                              | 2                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| First action                  | Not findable                    | Place or action is clear       | Place, action, and helper are clear           |
| Support and fallback          | Missing                         | One useful support or fallback | Both support and practical fallback work      |
| Language in service of reader | Forms absent or obscure meaning | One target form is useful      | Condition or purpose helps another person act |

**Authoring rule:** A finished draft, partial draft, or saved outline counts as a completed writing route. No model response is a socially “better” ending.

**Recap if skipped:** _The final guide task asks for practical reader care, not a learner’s personal story._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n4-open-rooms-guide`.

## S6.5 - Optional Special Scene: Open Rooms Walkthrough

**Location:** L01 -> L07, static accessible text route available.
**Narrative job:** Let each cast member find one final, ordinary revision through use.
**Authentic language need:** Confirm, clarify, and revise a guide in the order a new arrival would need it.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n4-guide-walkthrough`; direct task is always separately available.
**Intent ID:** `guide.test-and-revise-in-context`

_The group follows the fictional guide as if none of them had made it. Each carries only the card, not a memory of the room._

**SUZU:** `「デスクの近く」より、「デスクの前」が分かりやすいですね。`
_“In front of the Desk” is clearer than “near the Desk.”_

**LEO:** `青いベンチは、雨の日でも見えます。目印にできます。`
_The blue bench is visible even on a rainy day. It can be a landmark._

**MIKA:** `雨なら、カフェです。これで、次の場所が一つです。`
_If it rains, it is the cafe. Now there is one next place._

**NORI:** `「少し休んでもいいです」を、早く読みたいです。`
_I want “you may take a short break” earlier in the guide._

**RIE:** `では、直しましょう。いいガイドは、使ったあとにもう一度よくなります。`
_Then let us revise it. A good guide gets better after it is used._

**Optional payoff:** Blue Door Tag and the final campus panorama.
**Connection recap:** _The guide remained unfinished in the useful sense: it can be revised by the next reader too._

## S6.6 - Rie Leaves the Marker Down

**Location:** Open Door Desk, term close.
**Narrative job:** Complete Rie's arc by letting the group own the final language.
**Authentic language need:** Summarise what changed and choose a next rehearsal without ending learning.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n4-final-reflection` - self-assessment and optional next-step selection.
**Intent ID:** `term.reflect-without-finality`

_The blue half-door has been completed with one clean pencil line. Rie picks up the marker, checks it, and then sets it back in the drawer unopened._

**RIE:** `これは、みなさんの文です。わたしの見本にしなくていいです。`
_This is your sentence. It does not need to become my model._

**SUZU:** `でも、次の人が直しても いいです。`
_But the next person may revise it too._

**RIE:** `それが、一番いい終わり方ですね。`
_That is the best kind of ending._

**Final folio card:**

> `初めて来る人へ。入る前に、まず案内カードを読んでください。ここはオープン・ドア・デスクです。分からないときは、デスクで聞いてください。雨なら、近くのカフェに行きます。少し休みたいときは、静かなテーブルを使ってもいいです。`

**Final reflection choices:** `I want the model and transcript`, `I can do it with a few notes`, `I want a new practical task`. These direct practice/review recommendations only; they never alter completion or the ending.

**Term recap:**

- A welcome card names who can help.
- A route starts where the reader stands, with a landmark and time.
- An invitation offers a place at the table and a way to decline.
- A quiet option is clear about what it is for and how to ask for help.
- A plan explains purpose, checks options, and carries a fallback.
- A useful guide is compared, tested, revised, and handed forward.

**Final state:** The Blue Door Folio is complete as a term arc and open as a reusable practice object. Review, new lessons, route switching, and direct navigation continue without a narrative reset.

## Chapter 6 author notes

- The mystery is resolved in S6.3. Do not add a later identity reveal, private backstory, or contact route for the former visitor.
- The learner's final writing is practical and local-first. It must not ask for autobiography, future travel plans, work, relationship status, or contact information.
- The final scene has no departure gate, farewell plot, romance confession, or “you changed someone” climax. The emotional payoff is collective competence and a usable thing left for another person.
- The panorama must be static under reduced motion and have a text equivalent. It is a recap surface, not a completion gate.
