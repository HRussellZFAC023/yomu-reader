## 1. Who they really are

Xingyu introduces herself as **シンユ / Xingyu** and she is the class's steady daylight. The evidence is consistent from three independent sources: she is *always happy*, she is *soft-spoken, kind, encouraging* — quick with a 良いですね or an English "good luck!" — and she is, quietly, **the glue that keeps the class together**. The line that recurs across the logs is the truest thing about her: it is *impossible to have a bad evening around her*. She doesn't hold the room by being loud (that's Aakash's lane, and Robert's); she holds it by making the low-drama, found-family warmth feel effortless, so nobody notices they're being carried.

Her one inseparable habit is **music**. She likes **Hatsune Miku** and **hums Vocaloid/Miku straight through every listening exercise** — an offbeat, half-under-her-breath melody the class has stopped trying to stop. This is her whole learning fingerprint: she processes Japanese *through how it sounds aloud*. She learns a line by singing it back, by karaoke-ing the shape of it, by **ながら-study** (reviewing while a track plays) — two things at once, sound and study, always braided. Where Sophie color-codes and Shin decomposes components, Xingyu **revises by ear**: she'll try a line, hear that a syllable sits wrong, and fix the rhythm before she can tell you the rule.

She also carries a light, specific **regional-media literacy** — she knew *Channel News Asia* and weighed in that "CNA is a big one." It's a small, real detail, and it places her in the **East/Southeast-Asian heritage cluster with Xin and Sophie**, the informal advanced-speaker corner who back each other up. Around them she's less the cheerleader and more a peer; around the wider class she's the reliable source of とても良いですね！ and bilingual well-wishes (メリクリスマス, 頑張って) dropped into English banter with a pile of emoji.

**Reconciling real texture with the canon arc.** The anchor's phrase *"lets joy coexist with vulnerability"* is the load-bearing one, and the real Xingyu earns it precisely because her happiness is *not* a performance to see through — it's genuine. Her four-season movement is therefore **not** "the mask cracks." It is subtler and kinder: she learns that **sharing something unfinished** — a flawed chorus, a line she can't quite land — is itself a gift to the room, and that joy is *deepened*, not endangered, by letting people see the seams. The Miku humming is the perfect vehicle: it starts as ambient sunshine and, over the arc, **changes function** — from private comfort, to a shared listening tool, to a deliberate cue the class relies on. Same habit, growing meaning. That is her whole story in one gesture.

**Diegetic pop-culture note.** Miku is a real, load-bearing interest, not trivia. Every Miku/Vocaloid reference must *create a task* — transcribe a name into katakana, interpret a lyric, mark where a mora is wrong — never a name-drop. Academy art/UI stays original; real titles live only in dialogue.

Her limit is not a confession or a breakdown; it's a *corrected assumption* — she learns that memorability can lie, and that her ear, which she trusts, needs the seventy-percent honesty Ruparna and Sophie insist on (`callback:seventy-percent` is transforming in this same chapter). ### Beat 5 — Future (Ch 46, `s4e10-public-evening`) — **`callback:quiet-count` PAYOFF**

## 3. The Japanese her story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — most important)

**Learning speciality:** *sound-first Japanese* — katakana/loanword transcription, mora and rhythm accuracy, listening for a target line, and casual spoken reaction (相槌 / aizuchi). Her real habit (humming Miku, learning by singing lines back, ながら-study) is the authentic engine that makes each of these *necessary*, not assigned. **Miku (初音ミク), Vocaloid names, and song titles are written in katakana** — so her interest and her lead lesson (`l1-l22`–`l1-l26`, katakana completion + sound/shape repair) are the same object. That is the tightest real-detail-to-curriculum lock in her file; build the lead scene on it.

### Beat 1 — Ch 1 (`lesson:foundation-00`, classroom-expression shard)
- **Language function the learner NEEDs:** greet, react naturally, and **ask for a line to be repeated** (もう一回お願いします) so you can catch its sound.
- **Activity type:** listening + speaking (aizuchi seeding: いいですね / もう一回).
- **Real-texture reason:** she's already humming; the learner's first authentic need around her is to *ask to hear it again* — repetition-request is born from her music, not from a drill.

### Beat 2 — Ch 15 LEAD (`l1-l22`–`l1-l26`: complete the katakana sequence and repair a sound/shape error in context)
- **Language functions the learner NEEDs:** (a) **transcribe a name/loanword into katakana** and complete a katakana sequence; (b) **repair a sound/shape error** — fix the wrong mora / minimal-pair confusion (long vs short vowel, ツ/シ, ソ/ン shape, small ャュョ) inside a real line; (c) **casual reaction** to an imperfect attempt that supports rather than mocks (おしい！/ そこ、ちょっと違うかも / もう一回いい？).
- **Activity types:** **katakana writing/transcription**, **listening** (find the hole in the sung line), **speaking** (sing it back corrected), **transfer** (the "chorus with a hole" is the transfer object).
- **Real-texture reason → why the Japanese is unavoidable:** Xingyu built a *Miku-style study chorus* to hold katakana loanwords, and the one syllable she always flubs **is** the sound/shape error the lesson targets. The learner literally cannot repair the chorus without doing katakana mora-repair — the fiction and the exercise are the same act. Because it's *sung*, the wrong mora is audible, memorable, and low-stakes to fix: imperfection offered, not ridiculed. Use built-in Yomu audio for the vocabulary-sheet readings so the learner hears the corrected line.
- **Callback tie:** `callback:quiet-count` echo — Mika's Ch 4 count locates the entrance; Xingyu's chorus makes it memorable-but-unprovable, so the *words* still need katakana verification. The lesson is the proof step.

### Beat 3 — Ch 31 (`s3e07-under-the-subtitle`; higher-band, N3→N2)
- **Language functions the learner NEEDs:** **quotation and modality** — report what a speaker said while preserving uncertainty (〜と言っていた / 〜かもしれない / はっきりとは言っていない); distinguish a *memorable paraphrase* from an *accurate quote*.
- **Activity types:** listening (interview clip above comfort level), reading (subtitle vs source), transfer (rewrite a too-clean line to keep its hedge).
- **Real-texture reason:** Xingyu's instinct is to make it singable/memorable; the scene makes the learner feel the cost — the catchiest subtitle is the one that *deletes* かもしれない. Her regional-media literacy (she clocks broadcast register — "CNA is a big one") gives her a credible reason to be in the interview-clip room. The learner must produce Japanese that stays honest under the pull toward a neat line.

### Beat 4 — Ch 44 (`s4e08-last-revision`; N2→N1)
- **Language functions the learner NEEDs:** **editing/adaptation register** — argue an artistic rationale in Japanese (この方が覚えやすいけど、〜は残さないと), justify a constraint, choose phrasing that is both vivid and faithful.
- **Activity types:** writing (revise the public line), speaking (defend the choice), reading (check nothing withdrawn creeps back).
- **Real-texture reason:** her ear is now the *accuracy* tool — "sing it; does it still hold if we cut that clause?" The learner uses her method to test whether a memorable rewrite smuggles back withheld detail. Constraints create the final form.

### Beat 5 — Ch 46 (`s4e10-public-evening`; integrated N1)
- **Language functions the learner NEEDs:** **transparent live repair** — stop, acknowledge a misunderstanding, re-state clearly, and negotiate with an audience (すみません、今のは違いました。もう一度言います…); integrated listening + response.
- **Activity types:** listening + speaking under live pressure; transfer (the quiet-count cue as a real repair mechanism).
- **Real-texture reason:** the `callback:quiet-count` payoff — the same quiet, sung cue that once just made a line memorable now buys the class a beat to repair honestly in public. The learner performs repair Japanese with a built-in, character-owned tool.

**Learning-priority alignment:** per owner order (architecture > performance > JA content > exploration > atmosphere > graphics > story), every Xingyu beat above is a *language function first*; the Miku/humming texture exists to make the function necessary and memorable, never to carry plot on its own.

3. **Reciprocity — sing it back.** The learner brings a line *they* can't land; Xingyu karaoke-repairs it by ear and hands the method back. *Function:* casual reactions + request-for-repetition (もう一回 / おしい / そこ！) as a two-way exchange.
