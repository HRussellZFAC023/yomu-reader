# Japanese Folktale Motifs for Yomu Academy

**Status:** research input for the story and content teams. This is a source-and-mapping brief, not shippable content. Nothing here overrides the originality and rights boundaries in [WORLD-BIBLE.md](../WORLD-BIBLE.md) and [USER-RESEARCH.md](../USER-RESEARCH.md).

## How folktales are allowed to be used here

The Academy world is **original**. Folktales are used in exactly two rights-clean ways, never as a rewritten franchise:

1. **As emotional motifs (undertones).** A tale's *shape of feeling* — a kind gift returned, a boundary honoured, a bittersweet parting — quietly underlays an original campus beat. The learner never meets Momotarō on campus; they help make a rain plan that *feels* like Kasa Jizō. The motif is a writers-room compass, not on-screen text.
2. **As optional, rights-cleared graded reading.** A separate, public-domain retelling can sit in the **Bag** or a reading corner as optional N5/N4 material. It is never required, never gates a task, and carries full asset-rights metadata like any other asset (origin, license, attribution, permitted uses, caption/transcript for any audio).

Guardrails that follow from the world bible:

- A folktale motif may inform tone, item flavour, and recap wording. It may **not** become a required scene, a placement input, a bond obligation, or a currency sink.
- Any on-screen retelling must be an **original or public-domain-sourced** text with cleared rights — not a modern copyrighted picture-book, translation, film, or stage version (see per-tale caveats below).
- Tales with violence or revenge (Kachi-kachi Yama, Saru Kani Gassen) are used **only** as light thematic hints or clearly-labelled advanced reading with a content note. The campus tone is "warm, local, human-scale; no danger, no punishment."
- Accessibility parity applies to any reading item: audio-off learners get the full text, songs/onomatopoeia get captions, and reduced-motion learners get static presentation.

## Public-domain status (summary)

**The tales themselves are public domain.** Every fable below is centuries-old anonymous folklore (Nara/Heian/Muromachi/Edo origins). There is no copyright in the underlying story, and it may be freely retold.

**Copyright lives in specific modern expressions, not the tale.** A particular 20th/21st-century *retelling, translation, illustration, film, or stage adaptation* can still be protected. Rules of thumb for asset clearance:

- **Safe to draw on:** the folklore itself; and pre-1929 English collections and Meiji-era Japanese editions that are out of copyright (see reliable sources below).
- **Do not copy:** living authors' picture books, modern anime/film versions, and named 20th-century dramatizations. The clearest trap is **Tsuru no Ongaeshi**: the *folktale* is free, but Kinoshita Junji's 1949 play *Yūzuru* (夕鶴) and its 1952 opera are **copyright-protected** — never lift that version's dialogue, staging, or character choices.

## The Fables

### 1. Momotarō 桃太郎 (ももたろう, "Peach Boy")

**Summary.** An old childless couple find a giant peach floating down the river; inside is a boy, Momotarō. Grown strong, he sets off to Onigashima to stop the marauding ogres, and along the way shares his mother's millet dumplings (kibi-dango) with a dog, a monkey, and a pheasant, who each join him for one dumpling. Together the small band defeats the ogres and brings the stolen treasure home to the village.

**Core theme / emotion.** Courage in the service of the community; a shared small gift that turns strangers into a team.

**Public-domain status.** Fully public domain; canonical modern wording is Iwaya Sazanami's 1894 *Nihon Mukashibanashi*, and Hasegawa's chirimen-bon Momotarō is a clean visual reference. Avoid the wartime-propaganda film versions.

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** The recruitment scenes are a gift for N5: each companion joins with the *same* repeated frame (「いっしょに 行きましょう」/「一つ ください」), so the learner meets one pattern three times with only the animal noun changing — ideal for plain-vs-polite past (行きました / もらいました) and counters (一つ, 二匹). Keep sentences short and dialogue-driven; N4 layer adds a reason clause (「おにを たいじする ために」).

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 4, Open Rooms Afternoon** — the beat where the cast finally rallies and several helpers each bring one small thing to make the shared afternoon happen. Kibi-dango ↔ the small notes/Margin-Mark tokens that align the group. (Secondary: Arc 1 "setting out".)

### 2. Urashima Tarō 浦島太郎 (うらしまたろう)

**Summary.** A gentle fisherman rescues a tormented turtle and is rewarded with a visit to the Dragon Palace beneath the sea, where Princess Otohime hosts him in timeless comfort. When he grows homesick and returns, centuries have passed and everyone he knew is gone; he opens the forbidden box she gave him and instantly becomes an old man. The tale ends on wonder edged with loss and the danger of the box you were asked not to open.

**Core theme / emotion.** Kindness to a small creature; the ache of time passing; returning to a world that has moved on.

**Public-domain status.** Ancient (references in the *Nihon Shoki* and *Man'yōshū*); fully public domain. Ozaki's 1908 "The Story of Urashima Taro" is a clean English retelling.

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** Strong for time and change vocabulary at N4 (「〜年 たちました」, 「もう 誰も いませんでした」, 「〜が かわりました」); the rescue opening works at N5 with simple past narration and adjectives. Trim the box's transformation to one clear sentence. Good for the returning-learner tone (see mapping) — do not moralize; let the wistfulness sit.

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 0 / placement, the returning-learner welcome** (persona P02, Dae — someone back after a long gap, reading kana slowly). The undertone "you were away, the world moved on, and you are still welcome" underlays the no-shame placement route. (Secondary: Arc 5 parting.)

### 3. Kaguya-hime かぐや姫 / Taketori Monogatari 竹取物語 ("The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter")

**Summary.** A bamboo cutter finds a tiny girl inside a glowing stalk and raises her; she grows into a woman of unearthly beauty, Kaguya-hime. She turns away five noble suitors with impossible tasks and even the Emperor, because she does not belong on Earth. On a moonlit night, celestial beings descend and she must return to the Moon, leaving her earthly parents and an elixir behind — a farewell no one can prevent.

**Core theme / emotion.** Bittersweet leave-taking; cherishing a person you always knew you could not keep.

**Public-domain status.** 10th-century text, Japan's oldest surviving prose narrative; fully public domain. F.V. Dickins' 1888 English translation is also out of copyright.

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** The core farewell is teachable at N4 with obligation/inevitability grammar (「帰らなければ なりません」, 「もう 会えません」) and giving/receiving (くれる/もらう) for the parting gift. The five impossible-request suitors are a natural, self-contained **Notice Repair** analogue at N5 ("this instruction cannot actually be done — what would make it doable?"). Keep the cosmology light.

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 5, A Map Others Can Use — the parting beat** (the brief's own example: *a friend leaving for Japan*). Underlays an end-of-term scene where someone the learner helped is moving on, and the learner leaves a clear "map" behind. (Secondary: N5 impossible-instruction repair puzzle.)

### 4. Tsuru no Ongaeshi 鶴の恩返し ("The Crane's Return of Kindness")

**Summary.** A poor man frees a crane caught in a trap. Soon after, a young woman arrives at his door and becomes his wife; she weaves astonishingly beautiful cloth on the one condition that he never watch her work. The cloth brings them money, but curiosity (or a greedy push to weave more) makes him look — and he sees the crane plucking her own feathers. Revealed, she thanks him and flies away for good.

**Core theme / emotion.** Quiet gratitude repaid; the cost of breaking a promise not to look; honouring another person's private space.

**Public-domain status.** Traditional folklore (retold since roughly the 15th century), fully public domain. **Caveat:** Kinoshita Junji's 1949 play *Yūzuru* (夕鶴) and its 1952 opera are **still copyrighted** — do not use that dramatization's dialogue, names, or staging. Use only the folk version.

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** Excellent for the negative request / prohibition forms at the N4 threshold: 「見ないで ください」 → 「見ては いけません」 → 「決して 見ないと 約束しました」. Reciprocity verbs (助ける → 恩を 返す) anchor the theme. At N5, keep to the rescue + the simple promise; save the reveal for the bridge/N4 layer.

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 3, Quiet Room, Clear Request — the honoured-boundary beat.** Underlays Nori's optional, local-only rehearsal ("practise privately; no one is watching") and the privacy contract (P09 Sana, P10 Tomas): a request to *not look* is respected, and gratitude is repaid quietly. Ties to the "don't force the microphone" rule.

### 5. Kasa Jizō 笠地蔵 ("The Straw-Hat Jizō")

**Summary.** On a snowy New Year's Eve, a poor old couple have nothing; the old man goes to sell woven straw hats but sells none. Walking home, he finds six stone Jizō statues bare-headed in the snow and covers them with his unsold hats — giving away his own scarf-wrap when he runs one short. That night the statues, come to life, haul a feast and treasures to the couple's door in thanks.

**Core theme / emotion.** Small, unrewarded kindness to someone left out in the cold; giving the last thing you have, expecting nothing.

**Public-domain status.** Traditional folklore, fully public domain. Widely anthologized; use a PD or original retelling for any asset.

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** Beautifully N5: repetition (one hat on each Jizō — counting 一つ…六つ), weather adjectives (さむい, ゆき), and simple giving (あげました). N4 layer adds the "even though / not enough" nuance (「一つ たりませんでした が…」) and the reciprocity payoff. Snowfall onomatopoeia (しんしん) needs a caption gloss for audio-off learners.

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 2, The Rain Page — the kind-shelter beat.** Directly underlays making a warm, thoughtful *weather fallback* for someone who'd otherwise be caught out (Mika's Rain Plan; the **Rain Note** item). Jizō in the snow ↔ a stranger left out in the rain that the learner's plan quietly takes care of.

### 6. Kachi-kachi Yama かちかち山 ("Kachi-kachi Mountain")

**Summary.** A cruel tanuki torments an old farming couple and (in the traditional tellings) does real harm. A loyal rabbit befriends the tanuki, then punishes him through a series of tricks — a firewood bundle set alight ("kachi-kachi"), a chilli-paste "medicine," and finally a mud boat that sinks. Justice is served, but the tale is unusually violent for the canon.

**Core theme / emotion.** Wrongdoing and consequence; loyalty avenged. (Tone is dark.)

**Public-domain status.** Traditional folklore, fully public domain; Ozaki 1908 tells it softened as "The Farmer and the Badger."

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** Only a *heavily softened* version fits the Academy tone. Usable as N4 cause-and-effect / sequence reading (「〜たら」, 「その あと」, onomatopoeia かちかち, ぱちぱち) **with a content note**, or as an optional advanced-reading item — not as a campus scene. Better to borrow just the harmless "kachi-kachi" sound-word motif for a listening/onomatopoeia activity than the plot.

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Not a primary underlay.** At most a light, abstract "a skipped step has a consequence" hint for a **Notice Repair** puzzle, or a labelled advanced-reading Bag item with a content caution. Flagged here because the brief listed it; recommend using its *sound-motif* and *cause/effect grammar*, not its cruelty.

### 7. Bunbuku Chagama 分福茶釜 ("The Lucky Teakettle")

**Summary.** A poor man frees a trapped tanuki (raccoon-dog), which repays him by turning into a tea kettle he can sell to a temple. When the priest sets it to boil, the half-transformed kettle sprouts legs and a head and scampers off. Reunited with the poor man, the creature performs rope-walking and tricks as a travelling show, making him prosperous, and is finally honoured as a temple treasure.

**Core theme / emotion.** Transformation and delight; an ordinary object becoming wonderful; kindness repaid through shared joy rather than gold.

**Public-domain status.** Traditional (tied to Morin-ji temple legend), fully public domain; Ozaki 1908 has "The Accomplished and Lucky Teakettle."

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** Great for transformation/change verbs and appearance (「〜に なりました」, 「あしが 出ました」) and for a light, low-stakes *performance* frame. N5 keeps the rescue + the funny kettle-with-legs image; N4 adds the show and the reason it delights the crowd.

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 4 flavour + the Window Rehearsal / Bag.** Underlays the idea that a plain campus notice or object can be made *delightful*, and pairs with Nori's low-pressure "practise a line before you perform it" rehearsal (a friendly, non-judgemental show). Fits collectible **flavour** items (F15), never a gate.

### 8. Shita-kiri Suzume 舌切り雀 ("The Tongue-Cut Sparrow")

**Summary.** A kind old man keeps a pet sparrow; his spiteful wife cuts out its tongue for eating her starch and drives it off. The old man searches, finds the sparrow's home, is feasted, and is offered a choice of two baskets — he humbly takes the small one, which is full of treasure. The greedy wife demands the big basket and hauls home a load of snakes and demons instead.

**Core theme / emotion.** Kindness rewarded, greed punished; the reward is in restraint, not in grabbing the bigger share.

**Public-domain status.** Traditional folklore, fully public domain; Ozaki 1908 "The Tongue-Cut Sparrow." (The mild snakes/demons ending is easily softened.)

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** The two-basket choice is a clean N5/N4 comparative unit (大きい/小さい, 「どちらが いいですか」, 「〜より」). Kindness/greed contrast is concrete and dialogue-light. Trim the tongue-cutting to a gentle "she chased it away."

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **The reward-economy recap beat (Arc 2-3 recaps / Margin Marks framing).** The "small basket over the big one" ethic mirrors the Academy's non-hoardable Margin Marks — the modest, kind choice is the good one. A quiet moral undertone for recap copy, plus a strong standalone N5 reading item.

### 9. Hanasaka Jiisan 花咲か爺さん ("The Old Man Who Made Flowers Bloom")

**Summary.** A kind old couple's little dog leads them to buried gold; a greedy neighbour borrows the dog, finds only rubbish, and kills it. From the dog's grave grows a tree; a mortar made from it yields treasure for the kind couple and filth for the greedy one, who burns it. The kind man scatters the ashes and dead cherry trees burst into bloom, delighting a passing lord — while the neighbour's copycat attempt only throws ash in the lord's eyes.

**Core theme / emotion.** Kindness makes the whole world flower; goodness beautifies the shared space, envy spoils it.

**Public-domain status.** Traditional folklore, fully public domain; commonly anthologized (and in Ozaki-era collections).

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** The blooming climax is a vivid N5 image with simple result grammar (「花が さきました」, 「きれいに なりました」); N4 adds the good/greedy parallel structure (「〜のに」 contrast). The repeated kind-then-greedy pattern reinforces one grammar frame twice. Soften the dog's death to "the dog was gone."

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 5, A Map Others Can Use — the flourishing/legacy beat.** Underlays the end-of-term campus panorama: the learner's accumulated small kindnesses "make the shared campus bloom" for the next newcomer (the Blue Door Tag / arc panorama). (Secondary: Arc 4 payoff.)

### 10. Issun-bōshi 一寸法師 ("One-Inch Boy")

**Summary.** A childless couple wish for a child even if tiny, and receive a boy no bigger than a finger. Undaunted by his size, Issun-bōshi sets off for the capital with a needle for a sword, a soup bowl for a boat, and a chopstick for an oar. He enters a noble's service, saves the daughter from an ogre by stabbing it from the inside, and — with a dropped magic mallet — grows to full size and marries her.

**Core theme / emotion.** Small beginnings and outsized courage; the newcomer who seems too small to matter, and matters.

**Public-domain status.** From the Muromachi-era *Otogi-zōshi*; fully public domain.

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** The improvised gear is a perfect N5 "X as Y" / instrument unit (「はりを かたなに して」, 「〜で」 for tools) and size adjectives (小さい, 大きく なりました). Short, brave, episodic — easy to keep each beat to two sentences. N4 layer adds purpose (「都へ 行く ために」).

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 0 / Arc 1 — the brave-newcomer beat.** Underlays onboarding and the opportunity statement "make a three-minute task feel like a meaningful contribution": the small, new learner sets out with modest tools and their first small effort genuinely counts (personas P01, P06). Pairs with the first **Folded Route Map**.

### 11. Omusubi Kororin おむすびころりん ("The Rolling Rice Ball")

**Summary.** A kind old man drops a rice ball that rolls into a hole; hearing mice singing merrily inside, he offers them more rice balls and is welcomed underground, feasted, and sent home with treasure. A greedy neighbour copies him but grabs and cheats — imitating a cat to scare the mice for their gold — and is left lost in the dark with nothing.

**Core theme / emotion.** A small accidental gift opens an unexpected warm world; generosity welcomed, grabbing punished.

**Public-domain status.** Traditional folklore, fully public domain.

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** Built around a **song** (the mice's "おむすび ころりん…") — a natural, joyful listening + rhythm activity, and a clean test of the audio-off contract (the song must be fully captioned/glossed so P03/P05 lose nothing). Onomatopoeia (ころりん, すっとんとん) and giving verbs at N5; the good/greedy contrast at N4.

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 2 kindness beat, delivered as an audio-off-safe listening item.** A small dropped thing leading to unexpected warmth mirrors the campus "a tiny helpful act opens a friendly moment." Chosen deliberately to exercise caption parity for the accessibility route.

### 12. Saru Kani Gassen 猿蟹合戦 ("The Battle of the Monkey and the Crab")

**Summary.** A monkey cheats a crab out of her rice ball, swapping it for a persimmon seed; she patiently grows the seed into a fruiting tree, but the monkey climbs it, eats the ripe fruit, and pelts her with hard unripe ones. Her small allies — a chestnut, a bee, a mortar, and cow dung (versions vary) — each hide and, in turn, ambush the monkey until he repents and makes amends. Teamwork of the unlikely and small sets the wrong right.

**Core theme / emotion.** Many small helpers each doing one thing to restore fairness; patience rewarded.

**Public-domain status.** Traditional folklore, fully public domain. **Caveat:** older versions kill the crab and end harshly; use the widely-taught softened "the monkey apologizes" ending, and note the content.

**N5/N4 retelling angle.** The ambush chain is a superb N5 "each one in turn" sequence (each helper hides 「〜に かくれました」 then acts — same frame repeated) and location particles (に, で). N4 adds the growth-and-patience arc (「〜ながら まちました」). Keep the softened ending.

**Chapter / emotional beat.** **Arc 4, Open Rooms Afternoon — the many-small-helpers coordination beat.** Even more on-the-nose than Momotarō for coordinating several notices/people: each helper contributes one small, ordinary thing and together the day works out. Underlays the Open Room Shift integrated task. (Use as motif; keep any reading item softened + content-noted.)

## Recommended usage for the content team

1. **Motifs first, reading items second.** Wire the emotional undertones into the arc beats now (they cost no rights and shape tone); add optional PD/original reading items later, each with full asset-rights metadata.
2. **Lead with the warm cluster** — Kasa Jizō, Momotarō, Issun-bōshi, Tsuru no Ongaeshi, Kaguya-hime, Hanasaka Jiisan — which map cleanly onto the campus's gentle, human-scale feeling.
3. **Handle the sharp-edged ones carefully.** Kachi-kachi Yama and Saru Kani Gassen carry violence/revenge; use only softened, content-noted reading or borrow their harmless *sound/sequence* mechanics.
4. **Exploit the repetition.** Momotarō (companions), Kasa Jizō (six Jizō), Saru Kani (ambush chain), Hanasaka (good/greedy pairs) all repeat one grammar frame — high-value, low-load N5 drilling that fits the three-minute-task design.
5. **Prove accessibility on the songs.** Omusubi Kororin (and any onomatopoeia) must caption/gloss every sound so the audio-off and reduced-motion routes lose nothing — a good, self-contained test of the parity contract.
6. **Clear rights per asset.** The tales are free; specific modern versions are not (esp. *Yūzuru* for the crane). Ship only original or verified-PD text and original art/audio, with provenance recorded.
