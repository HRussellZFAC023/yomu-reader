# Chapter 2 - 「まちのなか」 The Route That Holds

**Core question:** The second Blue Door card says "meet in the square." Which square, which entrance, and at what time?
**Story change:** Leo turns an attractive route sketch into a route another person can actually follow.
**Language family:** `どこ`, `ここ/そこ/あそこ`, `あります/います`, `に/で/へ`, direction words, time, and bridge sequencing with `てから`.
**Runtime status:** planned N5 and bridge sequence. `planned:` IDs are authoring anchors only.

## S2.1 - The Card That Starts Too Late

**Location:** Quad Meeting Point, L02.
**Narrative job:** Make a route failure kind, concrete, and fixable.
**Authentic language need:** Ask where a meeting point is before attempting to go there.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-place-where` - select and produce `〜は どこですか`.
**Intent ID:** `route.ask-meeting-place`

_Leo unfolds a route card. It has a handsome arrow, a little tree, and the words `スクエアで 会いましょう`. Suzu turns it ninety degrees. Then another ninety._

**SUZU:** `きれいです。でも、どの スクエアですか。`
_It is beautiful. But which square?_

**LEO:** `……ゴードン・スクエアです。書きます。`
_…Gordon Square. I will write it._

**RIE:** `まず、聞けるようになりましょう。「どこですか」は、地図の前の言葉です。`
_First, let us be able to ask. “Where is it?” is language you use before the map._

**Teaching card:**

> `ゴードン・スクエアは どこですか。` - Where is Gordon Square?
> `あそこです。` - It is over there.
> `デスクは どこですか。` - Where is the Desk?

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                      | Bridge                                   | N4                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `デスクは どこですか。` | `オープン・ドア・デスクは、どこですか。` | `オープン・ドア・デスクまで、分かりやすい道はどこですか。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _Leo's card named a square but not enough of a route. Start with the question that makes a place findable._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-place-where`.

## S2.2 - Where Things Are, Where People Are

**Location:** Fictional Bloomsbury location grid.
**Narrative job:** Separate location from destination without making particles abstract.
**Authentic language need:** Explain where the Desk, a sign, and a helper can be found.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-arimasu-imasu` - choose `あります/います` and location particle `に`.
**Intent ID:** `route.locate-place-and-person`

_Rie has made a small original map with five icons: desk, square, cafe, quiet table, and bus stop. Leo has given each an arrow. Suzu has numbered the arrows. Nori has added one chair and written “dry.”_

**RIE:** `物は「あります」。人は「います」。カードには、両方が いります。`
_Things use arimasu. People use imasu. A card needs both._

**NORI:** `静かな テーブルは、広場の 近くに あります。`
_The quiet table is near the square._

**SUZU:** `りえ先生は、デスクに います。`
_Rie is at the Desk._

**Practice card:** Match the reader need to the sentence:

| Need                  | Sentence                                            |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Find a physical sign. | `青い カードは、デスクの 上に あります。`           |
| Find a helper.        | `りえ先生は、デスクに います。`                     |
| Ask for either.       | `カードは どこですか。` / `りえ先生は どこですか。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _A route needs both a thing that is there and a person who is there._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-arimasu-imasu`.

## S2.3 - The Difference Between Going and Doing

**Location:** Two-Route Walk start, L01.
**Narrative job:** Give Leo an actual test for the card rather than a prettier drawing.
**Authentic language need:** Tell a visitor where to go and where an activity happens.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-destination-and-action` - `select-one`, matching, and route ordering for `に/へ/で`.
**Intent ID:** `route.distinguish-goal-and-action`

_Leo starts walking. His card says `スクエアで 行きます`. He stops halfway through reading it himself._

**LEO:** `あ。これは、ちがいます。`
_Ah. This is not right._

**RIE:** `いい修正です。行く場所と、する場所は、同じではありません。`
_That is a good repair. The place you go to and the place you do something are not the same._

**Explanation - practical contrast:**

| Reader action             | Form         | Example                           |
| ------------------------- | ------------ | --------------------------------- |
| go toward a destination   | `へ` or `に` | `ゴードン・スクエアへ 行きます。` |
| do an action at a place   | `で`         | `ゴードン・スクエアで 会います。` |
| find something at a place | `に`         | `カードは デスクに あります。`    |

**LEO:** `まず、スクエアへ 行きます。それから、ベンチの 前で 会います。`
_First, go to the square. Then meet in front of the bench._

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                      | Bridge                                        | N4                                                     |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `スクエアへ 行きます。` | `スクエアへ 行って、ベンチの 前で 会います。` | `スクエアへ行ってから、青いベンチの前で会いましょう。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _Use `へ/に` for the place you are going; use `で` for where the action happens._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-destination-and-action`.

## S2.4 - A Route Has a First Landmark

**Location:** Errand Loop, L06.
**Narrative job:** Let the learner see why directions need a testable starting point.
**Authentic language need:** Give and follow simple direction language.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-route-repair` - ordering and short-text direction response.
**Intent ID:** `route.give-first-landmark`

_The first version of Leo's route starts halfway down a street. Nori stands at the threshold card and looks at it with friendly, complete seriousness._

**NORI:** `ここから、まっすぐですか。`
_Is it straight from here?_

**LEO:** `はい。まっすぐ 行って、右です。`
_Yes. Go straight, then right._

**NORI:** `どこで 右ですか。`
_Where do I turn right?_

_Leo looks at the card. The arrow, for all its confidence, has no answer._

**Teaching card:** `まっすぐ` (straight), `右` (right), `左` (left), `前` (in front), `近く` (near), `目印` (landmark - bridge support).

**Learner practice:** Repair this route in three chunks:

1. `デスクを 出ます。`
2. `まっすぐ 行きます。`
3. `青い ベンチの 前で、右です。`

**Optional support:** A static map, transcript, and text-only list describe the same route. No spatial drag is required.

**Recap if skipped:** _A direction is kind when it begins where the reader is and names the first landmark._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-route-repair`.

## S2.5 - The Six-O'Clock Card

**Location:** Quad Meeting Point.
**Narrative job:** Turn an imprecise time into an adult-life courtesy, not a ticking plot device.
**Authentic language need:** Confirm a meeting time and create a bounded plan.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-time-and-meeting` - ordering response and short-text time confirmation.
**Intent ID:** `route.confirm-time-and-place`

_The next folio card says `夕方、広場で`. Mika studies it in the way someone studies a weather forecast: not alarmed, but unwilling to pretend it is a plan._

**MIKA:** `夕方は、何時ですか。`
_What time is “evening”?_

**RIE:** `そうです。親切なカードは、読む人に時計を探させません。`
_Exactly. A kind card does not make its reader hunt for a clock._

**Teaching card:**

> `六時に、ゴードン・スクエアで 会いましょう。`
> _Let’s meet in Gordon Square at six._
>
> `六時から 六時半まで、デスクに います。`
> _I will be at the Desk from six until half past six._

_Rie writes `6:00` on the old card. The final zero is drawn with the dead green marker; it comes out grey. She leaves it. Everyone can still read it._

**Variant spine - same intent:**

| N5                      | Bridge                          | N4                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `六時に 会いましょう。` | `六時に、広場で 会いましょう。` | `六時にゴードン・スクエアで会うことにしませんか。` |

**Recap if skipped:** _“Evening” became six o’clock, a place, and a clear window for help._
**Handoff:** `planned:activity-n5-time-and-meeting`.

## S2.6 - Optional Study Connection: Two-Route Walk

**Location:** L01 to L02.
**Narrative job:** Let Leo practise receiving evidence from another reader.
**Authentic language need:** Compare a route spoken aloud with a route drawn on a card.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-route-repair`; optional `planned:activity-bridge-sequence-route`.
**Intent ID:** `leo.test-route-with-reader`

_Leo offers two versions of the same route. One is shorter. One says where to start. He looks almost apologetic about making the second one longer._

**LEO:** `どちらが 分かりやすいですか。`
_Which one is easier to understand?_

**RIE:** `短いほうが、いつも早いとは限りません。`
_The shorter one is not always the quicker one._

**Learner choice - `practice-order`:** Follow the spoken route first / inspect the card first. Both use the same matching and rejoin.
**Connection recap:** _Leo learned that a route is not finished when he can see it; it is finished when someone else can follow it._
**Optional payoff:** Folded Route Map recap.

## S2.7 - The Second Card Opens

**Location:** Card Rack, chapter close.
**Narrative job:** Complete a useful route card and advance the folio without artificial suspense.
**Authentic language need:** Read a compact route that contains location, direction, and time.
**Learning evidence:** `planned:activity-n5-write-route-card` - long-text self-review with model and rubric.
**Intent ID:** `folio.publish-route-card`

_The repaired card is clipped beside the first. Leo has kept one arrow, but it is now attached to words that begin where a reader stands._

> `オープン・ドア・デスクを 出て、まっすぐ 行きます。青い ベンチの 前で 右です。六時に、ゴードン・スクエアで 会いましょう。`

**SUZU:** `これなら、初めての人も 来られます。`
_With this, even a first-time person can arrive._

_Inside the fold, the original card has a small pencilled note: `「近く」だけでは、来られない。` - “Near” alone does not get someone here. The line is a clue to the former visitor’s problem, not to their identity._

**RIE:** `次は、着いたあとです。来てもらうなら、席も用意しましょう。`
_Next is what happens after arrival. If we invite people, we should prepare a place for them too._

**Chapter recap:**

- Ask `どこですか` before assuming a place is findable.
- Use `に` for where something or someone is, `へ/に` for a destination, and `で` for an action place.
- A route begins where the reader is, names a landmark, and says when to meet.
- A good route is tested by another person, not admired by its author.

**Handoff:** Campus; Chapter 3 invitation/task; **Continue learning** opens the next unfinished activity.

## Chapter 2 author notes

- The map remains fictional and accessible through an equivalent text route. No real UCL map, shop, service, or route is recreated.
- Leo is not "bad at people." His arc is about testing a design with a reader rather than assuming a visual is self-explanatory.
- The route card appears as a recap item only. It cannot lock a location, activity, or review queue.
- Do not introduce a time pressure, missed bus, or penalty. A time can be useful without becoming a deadline.
