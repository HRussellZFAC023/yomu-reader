## 1. Who they really are

Robert is the **bon vivant** of the class — the one who turned a Thursday-evening language course into a social life. The real seed is a single message after class: 「授業のあと、一杯どう？」 ("drink after class?"). That line is his whole personality compressed: he can't bear the thought of the class dispersing into the London night without one shared table first. He is the person who makes the first pub happen, forwards the market and gig links, asks the local-savvy questions (how much, which landmark, where do we meet), and makes sure **nobody drinks alone**.

He joined a little later than the core (autumn), and he threw himself in with a gentle, earnest over-effort that is his most endearing trait. His Japanese is *ambitious for his level* — long, careful, slightly formal paragraphs where a shorter sentence would do, always closing with a self-deprecating thank-you for people's patience with his "poor speaking." He is a **culture-hound**: city pop ("I don't know if I'm the only sicko for city pop"), Gundam (he finished Zeta), Japanese markets and shotengai — he discovered the Kings Cross monthly market in August and became a regular, scouts the East London / Dalston Japanese Christmas market, photographs a giant ハニワ, and comes home with cat treats. Those treats are for **Borscht** (ボルシチ), his Russian Blue, named after the soup — a name that in romaji reads uncomfortably close to "Bullshit," which Tom noticed instantly and never let go of (Robert's canonical reply: 「ウワアアア！」).

He likes restaurants and fine dining, has **strong opinions about menus and stronger opinions about where to celebrate**, and is charmingly, reliably **late** — the classic "I am late but waddling down" text, usually because he stopped to walk a coworker to the station. (In a later term he is *in Japan* — "give Sensei my regards from Japan" — a benign real thread the story can nod to as an off-stage absence, never as a plot claim.)

**Reconciling the warmth with the canon arc.** The real Robert is pure generosity — so the danger is a flat "nice guy." Canon sharpens him correctly: his growth is not learning to be kind (he already is), it's learning that **hosting includes listening** — that a good host also hears *silence*, *refusal*, and *a changed plan*, and doesn't paper over a gap by adding a third option. His signature failure mode ("fills a gap with another option") is the loving over-provider who can't stop offering. His arc is the education of a host: from *itemizing everything* to *leaving room for the answer*. Every real detail deepens this rather than fighting it — the over-ordering that's "always fine," the menu with too many good choices, the perfect evening that fails and becomes better for it.

Voice fingerprint for writers: **open wide, then itemize.** He greets the room expansively (ようこそ、ようこそ), then immediately reaches for logistics — order, sequence, who wants what, where we meet. Under pressure he *adds*; in growth he *subtracts*. Keep the register a touch too polite for the room, keep the food/pub gravity well (every route he plans bends toward a table), and keep the apology-for-my-Japanese reflex as texture, never as a genuine deficit to be pitied.

## 2. Four-season arc (his five continuity beats, concrete)

His deeper arc — **"expansive welcome → hosting includes silence, refusal, and changed plans"** — is literally the shape of a host learning to hear "no." It plays out as: *offer more → offer one → hear no and redesign → hold a boundary politely → repair live → leave the gap on purpose.*

### Beat 1 — Arrival · Ch 13 · `s1e13-dinner-by-if` (LEAD, already authored)
Robert hosts the class's first rebuilt-map planning dinner as a **perfect if-then sequence**: the café's long table set not with food but with arrow-linked cards — soup → salad → pasta, each pinned to the next. He welcomes the room broadly, then lays out the whole chain (「スープができたら、サラダ。それから、パスタです」). One changed ingredient — the salad delivery comes up short — **breaks the chain**, and his instinct is to *fill the gap with more options* (another soup, bread, "I can make dessert too…"). Jenny, after watching quietly, makes **one precise request** that trades the broken sequence for a real choice: 「全部はいらないと思います。スープか、パンか、どちらか一つを選びませんか。」 Hosting becomes listening. This seeds his attention detail (*welcome-then-logistics, every route ends in food*) and **echoes** `callback:overbuilt-schedule`.

### Beat 2 — Contribution · Ch 24 · `s1e24-lanterns-return` (Mira leads; Robert contributes)
The exhibition finale. Mira realizes the map needs live narration, not another technical fix. Robert's contribution is the **reception around the exhibition** — he owns the food/hospitality plan that makes the event inclusive (the over-orders-and-it's-always-fine texture, tuned by his Ch 13 lesson). Crucially, he now *asks before proposing*: he checks one real need, then offers a workable plan, rather than presenting a finished nine-course itinerary. His hosting has visibly absorbed "listening." Language home here is the conditional/continuation cluster and **menu reading** (see §3).

### Beat 3 — Limit · Ch 39 · `s4e03-polite-no` (co-lead with Sam) — `callback:overbuilt-schedule` PAYOFF
The public venue asks for a **cleaner, more inspirational ending than the evidence supports** — essentially asking Robert to "fill the gap" at civic scale. This is his Ch 13 failure mode offered back to him as a temptation, and he **declines it**. Robert and Sam keep the relationship while holding the boundary: one clear alternative, stated in keigo, with **room left for the venue to answer**. The overbuilt schedule is finally declined instead of expanded. This is where "leaving the gap and redesigning after a no" stops being growth-in-progress and becomes who he is.

### Beat 4 — Return · Ch 46 · `s4e10-public-evening` (Return beat; Sam also returns)
Robert hosts the **reception of the public Japanese evening**. A live misunderstanding forces a **transparent repair** in front of an audience — and the old Robert would have smoothed it over by offering three replacements. The new Robert hears the misunderstanding, names it plainly, and lets one clear correction stand. Callback texture available: the pub set-piece — 「はじめての夜、おぼえてる？」 ("remember the first night?") — pays off the 授業のあと、一杯どう？ arrival, but *understated*, as a warm aside, never a nostalgic set-piece that stops the scene.

### Beat 5 — Future · Ch 48 · graduation (`callback:open-chair` montage; Robert "leaves space")
Per STORY-BIBLE line 234, the final season favours behavioral callbacks and **"Robert leaves space."** At graduation his contribution to the closing is *the gap he doesn't fill*: he sets the final table with one seat and one choice unmade, on purpose, offered under terms with no one assigned to occupy it. The host who once itemized every route now hosts by **leaving room** — the exact inverse of the Ch 13 arrow-chain. Food remains ordinary context; the change is entirely in what he chooses *not* to add.

## 3. The Japanese his story teaches (LEARNING INTEGRATION — most important)

**Learning speciality:** *the language of hosting.* Robert is the class's home for **invitations, requests, conditionals, and keigo** — and, uniquely, his arc bends that speciality toward the hardest social-Japanese skill: **receiving a refusal and renegotiating a plan.** Where other characters teach comprehension or production, Robert teaches *pragmatic hosting* — offering with room to decline, comparing options aloud, conceding, and refusing politely. His real-life texture (menus, the pub, over-ordering, "which do you want?", walking a coworker to the station) is a bottomless well of authentic reasons to use exactly this Japanese.

Design principle for every Robert scene (from his `ext-robert-table-plan` habit, N4+): **"Ask about one food need, then offer two workable options."** That single move is his pedagogical signature — it forces preference questions, `〜たら/〜ば` conditionals, `A か B か` alternatives, and offer-with-room-to-decline, all inside a reason the learner actually cares about (dinner).

## 4. Elective bond route (PROPOSAL — requires a `bond-authored` manifest entry + review before authoring)

> Robert is `continuity-only` today. This is a **sketch for the reviewed relationship-manifest**, not an authored route. It needs exactly six ordered packages, fallback presentation, a voice-card revision, and an owner correction path before any invitation issues (CAST-AND-CONSENT §Story eligibility; RELATIONSHIP-MATRIX §Finite-plot). Guardrails: platonic only, hidden-by-default, fully skippable with zero lost grammar, no romance/guilt/decay, bond only rises or holds.

Theme of the route: **you can be hosted by someone learning to listen.** Each appointment uses a real Robert interest and one clear language function; the route's turn is that his generosity learns restraint.

1. **Ordinary task — "Which one?" (the shotengai scout).** Robert forwards a Kings Cross / Dalston market link and asks the learner to help pick *one* stall to actually visit. **Function:** preference questions + compare two options (`どっちが…？`, `〜のほうが`). Sets his baseline: he'd happily list all twelve stalls.
2. **Different route — the menu with too many good things.** At a restaurant Robert wants to order everything; the task is to choose a *set* meal within a limit. **Function:** requests/offers + counters + `〜にします` (deciding). He over-provides; the learner practises narrowing.
3. **Reciprocity — Borscht & the cat treats.** Robert asks the learner's help reading a Japanese cat-treat package for his Russian Blue, Borscht. **Function:** reading (product kanji/katakana) + `〜てもいい？` permission/appropriateness ("is this okay for a cat?"). The Borscht/"Bullshit" romaji gag lands here as warmth, not mockery. Genuinely reciprocal: he's asking, not hosting.
4. **Friction — the perfect evening.** Robert plans a flawless celebration (city-pop bar → dinner → the pub) and one piece falls through. His reflex is to add a replacement; the learner (or a classmate) offers *one clear alternative* instead. **Function:** conditionals under change (`〜たら…けど`) + **declining an option kindly**. This is the route's mirror of Ch 13, at personal scale.
5. **Repair — "sorry I'm late, I walked them to the station."** Robert arrives late (waddling down), apologetic; the repair is not more apology but a re-set plan. **Function:** apology + reason (`〜て、遅れました`) + renegotiating time/place, *briefly*. Teaches that a good repair is short and forward-looking.
6. **Future — one seat kept open.** Robert plans a gathering and, for the first time, deliberately **leaves a choice unmade** for the learner to fill — the open offer. **Function:** polite negative-question invitation (`〜ませんか`) + offer with room to decline. Pays off his arc: the host who leaves space. (Optional Gundam/city-pop flavour: he asks the learner to *bring one thing they like* rather than curating it himself.)

Bond-route callbacks stay appointment-local (a scarf, a market stall, Borscht's treats) and **must not** resolve the atlas, another person's boundary, or a lesson requirement — each also needs an equally warm neutral fallback line for learners who skip it.
