# Review destinations own their schedules

The learner can keep an existing review workflow, including more than one SRS. Lookup sources, saved material and review destinations are separate roles; connecting one does not silently enable another. Yomu learning evidence does not replace the destination's schedule or create a second scheduled copy.

Defaults belong to tasks and supported item types. Every write names its destination and collection, distinguishes saving from reviewing, and preserves native grading semantics. Unsupported operations stay unavailable or open the provider's own experience. A successful connection probe is not integration acceptance; each advertised workflow needs end-to-end proof, including uncertain outcomes and reconnection.

Saving a looked-up word is that separate write. The lookup popup shows one "Add to deck +" beside the grades whenever one of the learner's enabled destinations can take the word, and never offers a destination that cannot. The learner's settings and what each service accepts decide which destinations can, not the dictionary that supplied the word. The default follows the grade row beside it, so a save lands where a grade would, without a schedule; when that service cannot take the word, Anki comes next, then the other enabled destinations. With both JPDB and Jiten connected the grade row uses the preferred grading service (ADR-0021), and so does the save: a word the other service identified is found there by exact spelling and reading first, Study's deck picker offers that service's decks (the ⇄ toggle switches both for one word), and a word it does not have is not saved anywhere ("Not saved: this word was not found in your preferred grading service."). Otherwise the grade row depends on the word's own identities and the learner's keys, so one learner's words can still default to different places: with a JPDB key, JPDB mining off, and Anki and the Yomu deck on, a JPDB-parsed word saves to Anki and a dictionary-only word to the Yomu deck. This is accepted until learners show it confuses them; the fix would be a learner-chosen default destination, not provider-specific rules. Jiten takes a saved word only into one of the learner's word lists (StudyDeckType 2, StaticWordList), so a save goes to the first word list in their Jiten deck order (`srs/reader-study-decks`) and Study's picker lists only word lists. A learner with no word list cannot save to Jiten: the save goes to the next destination, but when Jiten is the grade row's service, JPDB never stands in for it (ADR-0021). Study then says how to create a word list on jiten.moe; an ordinary page names no service (ADR-0020). A deck list Jiten fails to return is not "no word list": that save fails instead of going elsewhere. Subtitle batch mining follows the same rule: "Add selected" saves each word once to that same default, with no schedule, finding the batch's unidentified words on the grading service in one request. A word that service lacks is not added anywhere, and a word no enabled destination can take (including one Bunpro's catalogue lacks) is skipped; both are named in the result while the rest are saved. Batch grading keeps its own review rules.

A Practice Session owns its prepared material, purpose and durable responses. It has no provider-submission capability. Its current prompt belongs to that session, not shared settings or another tab's native queue. Practice feedback must not suggest a native review grade: the question and conditions may differ.

Answer visibility belongs to the current card in one surface. Shared view preferences cannot reveal or hide another tab's answer. A replacement card starts concealed, including after an empty queue.

This replaces the implicit assumption that a shared learner record requires one Yomu-owned review system. It also rejects a universal grade/action interface when providers mean different things by it. The conceptual decision is adopted for the rebuild; implementation and live-provider verification remain open.
