# Honen brief: Yomu Academy Day 0 and Day 1

## Purpose

Augment the existing Yomu Academy opening. Do not create a parallel generic course. The learner should experience a warm first morning with Rie, classmates, real props, short choices, immediate repair, and a clear reason for each piece of Japanese.

## Learner state

- The learner may know no hiragana or katakana.
- Kana support is optional and secondary until the learner chooses or completes the short kana bridge.
- Introduce only one useful unknown at a time.
- Never require an untaught script form to understand an instruction.
- English may explain the task; Japanese is the thing being noticed, heard, chosen, or produced.

## Existing opening sequence

1. Rie greets the learner and establishes the classroom.
2. The learner chooses how much kana support they need.
3. The learner writes their chosen class name in katakana. This is the naming moment; do not duplicate it elsewhere.
4. Rie requests a repetition with `もう一度お願いします`.
5. Rie uses two desk props to teach `しゅくだい` and `れい`.
6. The learner hears and matches each prop once.
7. The papers swap position for a changed-layout retrieval.
8. A wrong answer repairs only the current word.
9. Successful first retrieval seeds exact SRS evidence without duplicating it on transfer.
10. Review readiness recommends the next step, but the learner may always choose `Continue anyway`.

- answer-reveal timing;

## Voice and story guardrails

- Rie speaks plainly, warmly, and briefly.
- Prefer dialogue and visible action over narrator description.
- No corporate training voice, therapy slogans, inflated importance, or pretentious labels.
- Do not expose source-platform names to the learner.
- Do not turn source order into a page-long worksheet.
- Give the learner a useful payoff within two or three actions.
- Treat Honen drafts as proposals. Yomu implementation and learner-route proof are the acceptance gate.
