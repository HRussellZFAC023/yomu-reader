# Tone & humanize checklist

Every authored scene, narration line, English gloss, and design doc passes this. It **extends** the bible's tone rules — `VOICE-AND-DIALOGUE.md` (dialogue contract, rejection tests, language-layer invariants, pressure behaviour) and `STORY-BIBLE.md` (warm adult friendship, practical comedy, quiet mystery, earned emotion; the gentle rainy-evening voice) — it does not replace them. Read those first; this catches the machine-generated tells they don't name.

## The voice (from the bible — non-negotiable)
- Adults doing something together, not characters announcing traits. A line earns its place by attempting, noticing, withholding, correcting, inviting, refusing, or changing an action.
- Short turns; specific observation; **subtext before explanation**. Emotion arrives through action and physical detail (cold tea, a missed train, a chair left open) before anyone names it.
- Humour from timing, personality, and callback — never humiliation, never a joke that works after swapping a noun.
- Narration names only what a camera or ear could register. It does **not** diagnose a silence, explain a feeling, or announce the moral.

## Kill these machine tells (English narration, stage directions, glosses, docs)
- **The "not just X, it's Y" / "not about X, but Y" / "isn't just… it's…" construction.** Delete on sight. Same for "more than a…, it's a…".
- **Rule-of-three everywhere** (three adjectives, three clauses, three examples in a row) as a reflex rhythm. Vary the count.
- **Inflated-importance words:** crucial, essential, vital, pivotal, key, significant, profound, deeply, truly, really, very. Cut or replace with the concrete thing.
- **Transition scaffolding:** Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In addition, That said, It's worth noting, Importantly, Ultimately, In conclusion, At the end of the day.
- **Thesaurus-slop nouns/verbs:** delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, realm, journey (as metaphor), navigate (metaphor), leverage, robust, seamless, foster, underscore, resonate, illuminate, weave, boasts, elevate.
- **Em-dash overuse** as the default connector, and the "X — Y — Z" triple-dash sentence. Use a period. (Dashes are fine occasionally; not as a tic.)
- **Empty hedging / over-qualification:** "in many ways", "to some extent", "a certain kind of", "something of a". Say the thing or cut it.
- **Symmetry addiction:** every sentence the same length and shape; every paragraph a balanced pair. Break the pattern — short sentence. Then a longer one that actually earns its length.
- **Announcing the emotion** ("a poignant moment", "a heartfelt exchange", "the tension was palpable"). Show the physical detail; let the reader feel it.
- **Product/therapy/LinkedIn register** ("holding space", "showing up for", "a safe space", "journey of growth"). Adults in a rainy evening class don't talk like a wellness deck.

## Japanese-side humanize (adds to the layer invariants)
- Spoken JA of real adults: contractions, sentence-final particles that fit the speaker (ね/よ/か/な/わけ), natural ellipsis, false starts where a person would (…えっと, あー) — not textbook-complete sentences.
- No textbook cadence: never a line that reads like a Genki/Minna example ("これはXのYです" as a personality statement). If a beginner line sounds like a gloss, rewrite it as a situated turn.
- Register must fit the person every band (Mika/Xingyu casual peers; Rie/Nanako's gentle politeness; keigo only where the scene earns it). Higher bands add ellipsis and implication, not rarer vocabulary.
- Reading fields are plain, honest kana of that band's line — no decoration.

## The test before shipping any line
1. **Strip the names.** Can you still tell who's speaking from attention + rhythm + tactic? If two adjacent lines have the same length and move, rewrite one.
2. **Would a person say this out loud, in this room, right now?** If it reads like copy, a summary, or a caption, cut it.
3. **Is the feeling shown or stated?** Replace any stated feeling with the concrete thing a camera would see.
4. **Search for the tells above.** If any appears, it's a rewrite, not a polish.

A scene that passes reads like it was overheard, not generated.
