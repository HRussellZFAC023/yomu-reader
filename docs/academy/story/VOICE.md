# Voice

One rule under everything: **write like a warm, funny person talking to a friend at 7pm on a Thursday.** Not an app. Not a school. Not a compliance officer. A good teacher who is tired, kind, and glad you came.

If a line couldn't be said out loud by Rie-sensei in a real classroom without sounding like marketing, it's wrong.

## Rie-sensei in one breath

Rie is warm, quick to laugh, impossibly busy, and secretly proud of every one of you. She grades with a hand-drawn 花丸. She works what feels like nine jobs, so you keep bumping into her in strange places — the midnight konbini, the ramen counter — always delighted, always exhausted, always kind. She teases; she never scolds. She starts from meaning, never from rules.

Her tics: "まず、意味からいきましょう。" ("Let's start from the meaning.") A dead marker is a running joke, not a problem. She says こんばんは like she's been waiting for you.

## Do

- **Short.** One idea per line. If you can cut a word, cut it.
- **Warm and specific.** "Hang your coat anywhere" beats "Welcome to your learning space."
- **Talk to one person.** "Come in, come in." "Grab a seat." "What should I call you?"
- **Be funny the way friends are funny.** Rie's marker gives up on "where" again. Shin says a scary kanji is "easy, honestly." Christian produces the recorder; nobody asks.
- **Always say what happens next.** Every screen ends with a clear, small action: "Start the plan." "Write the message." "Say hello first."
- **Let Japanese carry weight.** JA lines are real, natural, and level-appropriate. English is the friend leaning over to help, not a translation robot.
- **Small stakes, real heart.** The point is never "master N4." It's "so a tired friend doesn't have to guess where to meet."
- **Kindness in feedback.** Name the one concrete thing to fix. "One detail's missing — check the time and place." Then move on.

## Don't

- **No uppercase kicker/eyebrow labels.** No "YOUR JOURNEY," no "LEVEL UP," no ALL-CAPS mini-headers.
- **No hollow disclaimers.** Never write "your data is stored only on this device," "wrong answers never count," "no pressure!" as a caption. If it needs saying, Rie says it once, warmly, in character ("No test tonight — promise").
- **No flowery obvious captions.** Don't narrate the picture. A lamp-lit classroom doesn't need "a cozy space where learning comes alive."
- **No AI-slop vocabulary.** Banned: journey, unlock, empower, seamless, tapestry, transformative, delve, "takes shape," "becomes useful," "dive in," "supercharge," "elevate," "curated," "at your own pace" (as filler). (The copy test enforces a chunk of this — keep it that way.)
- **No corporate warmth.** "We're excited to welcome you to..." — never. Rie doesn't have a brand voice; she has a kettle.
- **No UCL, no institutions in user copy.** It's "your class," "the classroom," "class order" — never "UCL," never "evening class order." The campus is warm and fictional.
- **No guilt, urgency, streaks-as-threats, or scores as worth.** Nobody is behind. Nobody failed. Come back next Thursday.
- **Don't ask for anything personal.** Name is optional. Never ask why someone is learning, their level as a verdict, their job, or a reason.

## Length & shape (hard rules)

- Every user-facing string ≤ 120 characters. Most should be far shorter.
- Button/action labels ≤ 24 characters. A verb and a noun: "Write the message," "Start listening."
- Pair JA + EN when both are shown; keep JA natural for the level, EN conversational.

## Feel-test (read the line aloud)

- Would Rie actually say this? → keep it.
- Does it sound like an app onboarding? → rewrite.
- Is it telling me the next thing to do? → good.
- Did it make me feel like I'm being *processed*? → delete and start over, warmer.

## Tiny before/after

- ✗ "Begin your personalised learning journey." → ✓ "Grab a seat. We start where the class left off."
- ✗ "Complete the placement assessment to unlock Lesson 9." → ✓ "No test tonight — promise. Just sit down."
- ✗ "This activity will help you become more confident." → ✓ "Two or three lines is plenty. Say it your way."
- ✗ "Great job! Your answer was correct." → ✓ "That works — the other person knows what to do next."
