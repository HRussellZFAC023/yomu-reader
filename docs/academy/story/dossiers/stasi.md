## 2. Four-season arc (beat by beat)

**Deep arc (from the bible):** they revise a vivid false memory and make the final public version precise without becoming plain. Their journey is from "the drawing is the truth" to "the drawing serves the truth" — expression that survives constraint.

### Beat 1 — Arrival, Ch 18 · `s1e18-the-memory-card-museum` (LEAD)
*Plot turn: Stasi's funniest mnemonic proves false.*

Stasi has built a personal wall of kanji memory-cards — each a little painting, each with a story for how the components hang together. The class treats it as a museum. Then their *funniest, most beloved* card — the one everyone quotes — turns out to be built on a component that isn't actually there: a radical misread into a better story than the truth. The comedy is that the false version is *genuinely more memorable*. The learning is that a memorable wrong shape still misleads.

Stasi does not scrap the card. They **revise** it — keep the vividness, correct the component, and prove the fix by visual comparison ("this stroke, not that one — 明るいし、間違えにくいし"). Direct preference stays; the *evidence* now backs it. This seeds their whole arc and their voice: expression is not the enemy of accuracy; sloppiness is.
*Seeds:* their "resists generic correct expression" stance; the visual-reasons (～し) habit; the museum-of-cards motif that returns in Season 3–4.

### Beat 2 — Contribution, Ch 24 · `s1e24-lanterns-return` (support to Mira's lead)
*Season-1 finale: the route needs live narration, not another technical fix.*

Stasi's contribution is the **visual layer** of the finale. When the group's map/route presentation needs to actually read to a live audience, Stasi is the one who lays it out — chooses the layout and defends it with two reasons (静かだし、見やすいし), matches colour to meaning, makes the thing legible under pressure. They meet Alex's plan halfway here (the C5.4 library-plan compromise): ambition tempered into something shippable tonight. Their role proves that a good drawing is production, not decoration.

### Beat 3 — Limit / Friction, Ch 31 · `s3e07-under-the-subtitle` (with Ruparna)
*Plot turn: a "clearer" subtitle erased uncertainty in an interview clip.*

This is Stasi's friction beat — and it's the deep one. Ruparna shows how a cleaner subtitle manufactured a certainty the source didn't have. Stasi's instinct — *make it read well, make it vivid* — is suddenly the **suspect** instinct. The exact same drive that made the memory-card better could make a translation lie. Stasi feels the pull between the vivid version and the true one, and has to sit in it. The Voice pair is the engine: "both care about representation; Stasi notices visual choice, Ruparna notices framing and alternate reading." Stasi gives direct preference; Ruparna preserves the alternate reading. The friction isn't a fight — it's Stasi discovering that their gift has an ethics attached.
*Consent guardrail here:* their visual preference must not be treated as factual memory. The scene names the difference between "I like this version" and "this version is what was said."

### Beat 4 — Return, Ch 44 · `s4e08-last-revision` (with Ruparna + Xingyu) — **paper-cat payoff**
*Plot turn: make the public version vivid without restoring withdrawn detail.*

The synthesis. The class has withheld material that isn't theirs to expose; Stasi's job is to make the final public version **vivid without restoring the withdrawn detail** — constraints create the final form. This is the arc landing: precise without becoming plain. They keep every ounce of expression they can and none of the content they may not use.

This is where Stasi **pays off `callback:paper-cat`**: the tiny paper cat that Tom seeded (Ch 5) as a marker, that Felix chased through mislabeled cards (Ch 17), that became version-evidence (Ch 35) — Stasi now uses **a new, neutral marker** to *preserve the place* of a withheld passage without exposing its content. The mascot becomes a discipline. A person who once believed the drawing was the truth now designs a mark whose whole job is to hold a space it must not fill.

### Beat 5 — Future, Ch 48 · `s4e12-next-page` (graduation)
*Graduation performance; class-continuity and elective payoffs; a final choice of tone rather than outcome.*

Stasi's future is open and expressive. At graduation their bounded contribution is, of course, **visual** — a final card, layout, or margin piece for the blank next-page that is precise, vivid, and leaves room. If their elective bond ran, its payoff line surfaces here (montage, not roll-call). They are last seen the way they arrived: a scarf, a doodle, and a reason — but the doodle now serves something larger than itself.

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

Real texture *creates the need*: a person who keeps a scarf for every mood and a wall of hand-painted kanji cards will *always* have a reason to compare two options and defend one.

The custom hook `ext-stasi-visual-reasons` (N4: "Choose a poster layout and give two reasons with し") is Stasi's signature exercise.
