## Authorization is not biography

`eligibility.story: true` authorizes a first-name-only fictional Academy portrayal within the evidence boundary. It does not certify every trait as true of a real person, grant rights to private messages or images, or turn invented events into claims.

Every named non-textbook scene carries these rules:

1. Events are fictional composites and are not presented as real history.
2. First names are exact; guessed surnames, kana spellings, nationalities, ages, jobs, diagnoses, relationships, travel records, addresses, and contact details are prohibited.
3. A recorded interest can motivate a fictional task. It cannot support a hidden personal history.
4. Raw chats and photographs never enter scripts, prompts, source control, generated art, or dialogue.
5. A private-source summary may shape cadence only at a high level. No message is reproduced, translated, or closely paraphrased.
6. The writer must be able to point to a public repository dossier field or mark the detail as invented fiction that carries no real-person claim.

## Portrayal states

Each authored scene records one portrayal state per named cast member:

- `fiction-cleared`: registry permits story and the scene uses only approved or invented non-claiming traits;
- `lesson-cleared`: registry permits lessons and the cast member has a defensible learning role;
- `likeness-cleared`: registry permits runtime portrait use for the exact asset revision;
- `name-only`: dialogue or text is allowed, but presentation must not imply likeness;
- `hold`: evidence, consent scope, or review is incomplete; the scene cannot ship.

The most restrictive applicable state wins. A completed sprite cannot upgrade the registry.

## Ensemble rotation

- One scene lead, one or two supports, and no more than two speaking background roles.
- No classmate leads more than two consecutive curriculum weeks.
- A person speaks only when their attention, relationship, or assigned task changes the beat.
- A crowd response is one stage direction or sound cue, not twenty interchangeable lines.
- Textbook legends never take a main-plot reveal, graduation payoff, or real classmate's learning ownership.
- Nanako and Mira remain occasional. Their rarity is part of their story function, not a deficit to fill.

## Diegetic consent

Consent is both a production rule and a story behavior.

- Invitations state the activity, audience, and easy way to decline.
- One clear refusal, hesitation, or changed condition ends persuasion.
- Supporting a person who declines means changing the plan, not finding a more flattering argument.
- Recording, public performance, name display, photo use, translation, and publication are separate asks.
- A group yes does not override an individual no.
- A learner choice cannot secretly waive a cast member's boundary.
- No affection, bond rank, reward, or required lesson depends on romantic compliance.
- Elective appointments are platonic, task-named, and deferrable. Story eligibility alone cannot generate one.
- A route may explore preference, disagreement, repair, or unfinished work; it may not invent trauma, diagnosis, family history, romance, finances, immigration history, or other private stakes to manufacture depth.
- The canonical story is platonic. Any future romance system requires explicit owner approval, learner opt-in, a separate safety contract, and routes that preserve equal curriculum access. It is outside this bible.

## Review and withdrawal

A cast owner correction is handled as a content migration:

1. mark affected scenes `hold`;
2. remove them from new delivery without deleting learner history;
3. replace factual-sounding details with approved fiction or retire the beat;
4. invalidate likeness assets independently from prose eligibility;
5. retain a private audit record without exposing the reason in product copy;
6. preserve generic class-continuity and appointment completion events while removing the retired expression;
7. ship a continuity-safe substitute that does not blame the person or imply withdrawal.

Completed learner events preserve generic completion and language evidence. They do not preserve withdrawn dialogue, thread messages, fictional props, audio, or portrait bytes in replay. A retired elective route falls back to a neutral shared-task memory; it never exposes the withdrawal reason.
