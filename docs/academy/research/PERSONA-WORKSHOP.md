# Personalization Persona Workshop

## Workshop question

What should Yomu Academy put in front of a learner first, and which supports should be easiest to reach, after five short onboarding questions?

The five answers are motivation, confidence, accessibility, available time, and preferred practice. Confidence and motivation choose a curriculum entry point. The other answers change the route through that material, not its academic level. Supports are deduplicated and sorted in one fixed order: access first, session shape second, confidence scaffolds third, practice format fourth, and goal framing last.

## Ten learner cards and outputs

### 1. Samira, fitting Japanese around the school run

Samira is starting from scratch and wants enough spoken Japanese to greet her partner's family without freezing. Most sessions happen on a phone for five minutes outside the school gates. She keeps sound off in public and would rather rehearse a line privately before hearing it.

- **Answers:** first conversations; new; text first; five minutes; speaking
- **Starting section:** `lesson-01-hajimemashite`
- **Support order:** text equivalent, short session, exact resume, kana scaffold, worked example, manual route switch, audio with transcript, private rehearsal, real-world scenario
- **Workshop output:** Open on one model introduction with visible Japanese and English. Let her type or mouth the reply, then offer audio. Save after every line.

### 2. Joel, returning after an evening-course gap

Joel finished an introductory course two years ago. He remembers greetings and particles when he sees them, but cannot tell what has stuck. He has fifteen minutes after dinner and likes a mixed set more than a long explanation.

- **Answers:** returning to study; rusty; no specific support; fifteen minutes; mixed review
- **Starting section:** `lesson-01-hajimemashite`
- **Support order:** checkpointed session, exact resume, returning-learner recap, manual route switch, mixed practice
- **Workshop output:** Begin with a compact recap and three varied checks. Keep “move ahead” and “review kana” equally visible so restarting does not feel like being sent back.

### 3. Mei, reading for games with a screen reader

Mei can hold a basic conversation and wants to read menus, item descriptions, and short dialogue without waiting for a translation. She studies at a desktop with a screen reader and keyboard, usually in a longer weekend block.

### 10. Dev, starting a structured JLPT habit

Dev has bought study books before but has not built a foundation. He wants JLPT progress because a dated goal feels concrete, yet weekday sessions need to fit into five minutes. Worked examples help him begin without guessing what an exercise expects.

- **Answers:** JLPT progress; new; no specific support; five minutes; guided
- **Starting section:** `kana-on-ramp`

## Decisions from the wall

1. **Time and access never lower placement.** A five-minute or text-first learner can enter the same section as anyone with the same motivation and confidence.
2. **Conversation is a valid early goal.** A new learner who wants first conversations can begin with introductions while kana help remains close at hand.
3. **Returning learners need exits in both directions.** Every rusty route pairs a recap with a manual route switch.
4. **Sound is additive.** Listening practice includes a transcript, while text-first and low-bandwidth routes remain complete without playback.
5. **Practice preference changes presentation, not entitlement.** Speaking stays private by default, writing models appear after an attempt, and reading aids remain adjustable.

## Follow-up workshop checks

- Ask learners whether “new”, “rusty”, and “steady” describe confidence clearly without sounding like a grade.
- Check whether the recommended starting section feels plausible after one activity, then make the route switch easy to find.
- Complete each support route with sound off, motion reduced, keyboard only, and a narrow connection.
- Compare five-minute completion estimates with observed sessions; split content at meaningful learning beats rather than arbitrary timers.
