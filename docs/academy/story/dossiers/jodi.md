## 1. Who they really are

Jodi is the oldest person in the room and the one who studies hardest — and those two facts are the same fact. She is not chasing a grade; she is chasing a memory. She lived in Tokyo once, a long time ago, and she carries a version of that city that only half-exists now: streets renamed, a station rebuilt, a temple lane she can still walk in her head but could no longer find on a current map. She reopened a Japanese textbook not to *start* the language but to catch up with a place that kept moving after she left. That is why she does the homework when the まじめ crew are cheerfully confessing they didn't: for her, every 文法 point is a tool for saying *this is how it was, and this is how it is now*, and holding both without deciding which one wins.

She is the class's quiet heart — warm and easy, present without performing. She doesn't push. She lights things and lets them catch. Her signature move is the old story produced on cue with surprising specificity: not a lecture, a small precise memory that turns out to be exactly the thing the room needed. The class trip in the back half of the story is, in-world, *her* fault in the gentlest way: a stack of old Tokyo photos passed hand to hand around the table until somebody says "we should go" — and then it stops being nostalgia and becomes a plan. When it does, her memory turns practical exactly when it counts: she is the one who says *I got lost at this station before; next time I'll check the map first* — past tense sliding into preparation.

**Reconciling real texture with the anchor.** The real detail (older woman, lived in Tokyo, studies hardest, memory not grade) *deepens* the canon anchor rather than replacing it. The anchor says she "compares present with remembered detail without rushing to resolve them" — the Tokyo-that-half-exists is the engine that makes that comparison emotionally load-bearing instead of a grammar drill. The anchor says she "separates memory, evidence, and permission" — that discipline is the mature, hard-won reflex of someone who *knows* her memory of a place is not the same as the place, and not the same as the right to speak for it publicly. She is generous with the memory and careful with the claim. That gap is her whole character.

**Real-detail guardrail:** "older" reads as *experienced, unhurried, the one who's seen more*, never as frail, confused, or a source of forgetting jokes. Her age is authority and warmth, not a punchline.

**Running jokes (canon-sourced):**
- **The wistful aside that becomes a plan:** 「昔ね、日本に住んでいたの。」 ("You know — I used to live in Japan.") It starts as a soft nothing dropped into a lull and, by the end of a scene, has quietly organized everyone.
- **The specific old story on cue:** whatever the topic, Jodi has a precise, small, true-feeling memory ready — gentle, never comic-at-anyone's-expense.
- **The temple-street callback** (fires only when the player's stated reason for learning is *for a person*): 「その人に、これ、話してあげてね。」 ("Tell this to that person for me, won't you.")

**Dynamics:** with **Angel** on trip logistics — Jodi supplies the memory ("the trains ran differently near there"), Angel converts it into a checked timetable; she volunteers to confirm ticket times herself (C5.8). With **Rose**, her structural foil — Jodi layers two times over one place; Rose asks what the ground still supports. With **Rie**, a peer register: two people who understand that a warm room and a careful claim are both things you have to *maintain*. With **the whole class**, she is the ember — the Tokyo photos are why everyone says "we should go."

## 2. Four-season arc (beat by beat)

Jodi's deep arc: **from a private memory, to a shared plan, to a public claim she refuses to over-state, to one of three true versions, to a page she leaves open for someone else.** She never resolves her two tenses into one; the story rewards her for *not* forcing it.

### Beat 1 — Arrival, Ch 9 · `s1e09-the-story-in-two-tenses` (LEAD)
Her attention pattern is established. The class is asked to caption something — a place, a photo, an object from the atlas. Jodi writes **two** captions for the same subject: one in the past ("there used to be a small shop here"), one in the present ("now it's a コンビニ"). She does not delete one. The scene's whole point is that *memory can remain plural* — past/present comparison and quoted thought sit side by side without a winner. This is where the learner first meets the idea that two true sentences about one thing is not a contradiction to fix but a form to master. Seed: her old story, her double caption.

### Beat 2 — Contribution, Ch 20 · inside `s1e20-map-from-memory` (Rose leads)
Jodi is not the lead here — Rose is — and that is exactly her contribution: she can hold a shared memory open without needing to own it. The class is rebuilding a district from overlapping, non-identical accounts. Jodi co-owns **`callback:useful-number-notebook`** (with Sophie, Rose, Henry); at Ch 20 the callback is at its **echo** state — *overlapping accounts are counted without voting one into truth.* Jodi models the move: when two people remember the same corner differently, she writes down *both* with their context instead of picking. Her Tokyo experience makes her the natural person to say "mine is also just one account." She prepares the group's memory-gathering (ておく / sequencing) rather than narrating everything she personally recalls.

### Beat 3 — Limit, Ch 33 · `s3e09-what-we-can-say` (co-lead: Jodi, Nanako, Mira)
Her disagreement beat — and it's the thematic heart of her whole character. The class wants to put Jodi's lived Tokyo memory into the *public* version of the exhibition, treating "she was there" as automatic authority. Jodi is the one who stops them. She separates three things the class had collapsed: **memory** (what she remembers), **evidence** (what can be shown), and **permission** (what may be publicly claimed and on whose behalf). Her limit is a refusal: *lived experience is not automatic publication permission.* Nanako gives the quiet ruling; Mira tests whether the resulting rule actually works in ordinary use. The ensemble changes what it does — it publishes the calibrated version, not the confident one — without punishing Jodi for her caution. This is the season's evidentiality/hedging turn.

### Beat 4 — Return, Ch 40 · `s4e04-three-true-versions` (Rie, former learner, current class)
Changed behavior after the limit. The page's beginning is described three different ways by three people, and *no omniscient narrator corrects them.* Jodi, who at Ch 9 wrote two captions for one place, now lives comfortably inside three compatible accounts of one origin. Her Ch 9 detail (two tenses of one thing) pays into the season's structural claim: perspectives can be incompatible in wording and compatible in truth. She is the person best equipped to hold that ambiguity gracefully, because she has been doing it with Tokyo the whole time.

### Beat 5 — Future, Ch 48 · `s4e12-next-page` (graduation)
The open next possibility. At graduation Jodi records or writes **one bounded contribution** — likely one caption, in her two-tense form, offered under the class's permission terms — and then helps leave the next page blank. Her Future is not "she goes back to Tokyo" or any finished life; it's that she has taught the class *how to leave a memory open for someone who wasn't there.* The temple-street callback can pay off here if the player's reason was for-a-person: she hands the learner the line about telling someone the story.

## 3. The Japanese their story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

**Jodi's learning speciality:** the language of **two times over one thing** — past/present comparison, quoted thought and reported speech, and (at the higher bands) the evidential and hedging machinery that lets you state *how sure you are and how you know.* Her arc is a single grammar spine — comparing, quoting, and calibrating — climbed from N5 to N1. She is the character who makes the learner *need* to say "it was X, now it's Y, and I only half-remember which."

### Ch 9 — Arrival · reservoir `l1-l13`–`l1-l14` ("state ability/understanding and give one reason")
- **Language function the scene makes the learner NEED:** past/present contrast on one referent (むかしは〜でした / いまは〜です), plus **stating ability/understanding and giving one reason** — わかります / できます + から, because Jodi's whole double-caption move is *"I can still picture it, because I lived there."*
- **Activity type:** **writing + speaking** (produce two captions for one subject), scaffolded by **vocabulary** (place words, あります/ありました existence in two tenses) and **grammar** (past-tense copula, 〜から for one reason).
- **Real texture → authentic reason:** the learner isn't drilling past tense abstractly; they're captioning Jodi's actual Tokyo photo, where the past sentence and the present sentence are *both* true and neither can be cut. The double caption is the exercise.

### Ch 20 — Contribution · reservoir `l2-l16`–`l2-l18` ("prepare in advance, sequence action, say enough without narrating everything")
- **Language function:** **〜ておく** (prepare the memory-gathering in advance), **〜てから / 〜たあとで** (sequence: gather, *then* compare), and the pragmatic skill of **saying enough without over-narrating** — the exact restraint Jodi models when she records two accounts instead of reciting her own at length.
- **Activity type:** **reading + writing transfer** — the number-notebook echo: learner logs overlapping accounts with their context (source + confidence) rather than voting one true. Hosts the `callback:useful-number-notebook` echo.
- **Real texture → authentic reason:** because Jodi *has* the richest memory in the room, she's the one who has to demonstrate leaving it partly unsaid. The learner feels the discipline of "prepare, sequence, don't over-narrate" as a social virtue, not a grammar rule.

### Ch 33 — Limit · reservoir: N2 evidentiality/hedging family (chapters 31–33 content contract: quotation, evidentiality, hedging)
- **Language function:** the full **evidential toolkit** — 〜そうだ (hearsay) vs 〜らしい vs 〜ようだ / 〜みたい (inference) vs 〜と言っていた (reported) vs plain assertion — and **calibrated claims** that distinguish *I remember / I was told / it appears / I can show.*
- **Activity type:** **listening + writing** — annotate a source for observation vs hearsay vs inference; then **write the calibrated public sentence** (the permission-scoped version) instead of the confident one.
- **Real texture → authentic reason:** this is the scene where Jodi refuses to let "I was there" become "therefore it's true and mine to publish." The learner *needs* hedging grammar because the alternative — Jodi's memory stated as flat fact in a public exhibition — is exactly what the character won't allow. The grammar is the ethics.

### Ch 40 — Return · reservoir: N1 perspective family (chapters 40–42 content contract: perspective, compatible accounts, embedded quotation)
- **Language function:** **embedded quotation and perspective** (〜という / 〜とのこと / 〜によれば), **synthesizing three accounts** into compatible truths, and **defending uncertainty** without flattening any speaker.
- **Activity type:** **reading + speaking transfer** — read three descriptions of one origin, articulate why all three can be true, produce one synthesis that preserves the plurality.
- **Real texture → authentic reason:** Jodi has been holding two tenses of Tokyo since Ch 9; here the learner inherits that skill and applies it to three voices. Her personal habit becomes the class's method.

### Ch 48 — Future · reservoir: integrated N1 graduation composition (chapters 46–48 content contract)
- **Language function:** **graduation composition** — one bounded, self-authored contribution under stated permission terms, choosing tone rather than outcome.
- **Activity type:** **writing (extended) + optional speaking**, integrating every prior function; her two-tense caption form is the model the learner may reuse.
- **Real texture → authentic reason:** Jodi's gift to the ending is a *form* for leaving a memory open. The learner writes their own bounded page in her style and leaves the next one blank.

**Learning-function spine (compiled):** state ability/understanding + one reason; past/present comparison on one referent; quoted thought / reported speech (〜と言っていた, 〜という); prepare-in-advance (〜ておく); action sequencing (〜てから / 〜たあとで); saying enough without over-narrating; evidentiality (そうだ / らしい / ようだ) and hedging / calibrated claims; embedded quotation and multi-account perspective synthesis; N1 graduation composition.

## 4. Elective bond route — "Two Tenses" (Recognition → Friction → Support)

Jodi is bond-authored. Six appointments, built from photography / Tokyo memory / comparison / diligent study. Appointment 4 (Friction) sits at/after her Ch 33 Limit; Appointment 6 is Season 4. Each hosts a language function; each answer is given in Japanese (Persona/FE support-conversation style).

1. **Recognition — "The one shop" (ordinary task).** Jodi shows the learner a single old photo and asks them to caption it. *Function:* past/present existence (むかし〜がありました / いま〜があります). Warm, low-stakes; establishes her attention pattern.
2. **Different route — "Yours, not mine."** Jodi turns it around: she asks the learner to describe *their own* remembered place, not Tokyo. *Function:* self past-description + one reason (〜から). The bond becomes reciprocal — she's a listener, not just a memory-keeper.
3. **Reciprocity — "Check the times" (with Angel).** Jodi trusts the learner with the thing she's careful about: verifying a real detail (ticket times, a station name) against the present. *Function:* asking/confirming factual information politely (〜か どうか 確認する; 〜ましたか). Pays into the trip-logistics dynamic.
4. **Friction — "That's not ours to say."** The learner (well-meaning) wants to publish one of Jodi's memories as fact. Jodi declines. *Function:* hedging and permission language (〜と思います / 〜かもしれません / まだ 言えません). The bond's disagreement beat; a clear "no" ends the persuasion — consent is modeled, not overridden.
5. **Repair — "Both captions."** After the friction, they co-write the public version *together*, keeping two tenses and a stated confidence level. *Function:* reported speech + calibrated claim (〜と 言っていました, but 〜ようです). Behavior changes: the learner now writes the careful version by choice.
6. **Future — "Tell them for me."** Jodi gives the learner a memory to carry forward, under terms. If the player's reason was *for-a-person*, this is where 「その人に、これ、話してあげてね。」 lands. *Function:* giving/receiving-for-someone (〜てあげる / 〜てくれる) and a bounded future promise. Open-ended, no finished life claimed.
