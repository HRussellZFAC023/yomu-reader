## 1. Who they really are

Ruparna is the class's quiet cinephile — the one who watches everything with the subtitles on, and then watches it again. The rewatch is the whole personality in miniature: the first pass is for the story, the second is for the seam between what was said and what the subtitle decided it meant. She reads a room the way she reads a subtitle track: she is the first to notice what changed — the moved chair, the closed blind, the name that got quietly dropped from a plan — and she names the change before anyone else has looked up.

Her signature move, straight from the canon voice card, is that she **turns an ordinary detail into a scene, then questions the cut**. Someone sets a cup down; Ruparna describes it like an establishing shot — and then asks what the shot left out of frame. This is not showing off. It's how she thinks: framing is an argument, and every frame excludes something. Her running line, `「この字幕、いいね」` ("nice subtitle"), is genuine praise for a translator who kept a good ambiguity instead of flattening it — and her sharper line, `字幕にすると、ちょっと言い切りすぎじゃない？` ("once you subtitle it, isn't that a bit too definitive?"), is the same instinct turned critical. She likes subtitles precisely because she distrusts them.

**Reconciling with the canon voice anchor.** The anchor (`CAST-AND-CONSENT.md`, `VOICE-AND-DIALOGUE.md`) is: *frames ordinary details as scenes and preserves alternate readings → turns subtitle confidence into ethical translation judgment.* Her real texture deepens this without contradicting it. The real "watches everything twice with subtitles on" is the lived habit under the abstract "preserves alternate readings" — the second watch is where the alternate reading is found. The real "first to notice what changed in a room" is the concrete engine under her Japanese habit `「字も消えてしまいました」` ("the writing disappeared too") — she notices a changed state and names the repair. Nothing here overrides the four-season arc; it supplies its ground truth.

**Contrast partner: Stasi.** Both care about representation; the canon splits their attention cleanly (`VOICE-AND-DIALOGUE.md`): **Stasi notices the visual choice and gives a direct preference; Ruparna notices the framing and the alternate reading, then questions the cut.** Stasi says which one is better; Ruparna asks what the chosen one made invisible. Keep this seam sharp in every shared scene — it is the engine of chapter 44.

**Under pressure** (extending the canon pressure pattern): Ruparna's stress narrowing is *over-preserving ambiguity* — she withholds a reading so long, out of fidelity, that the group can't act. Her growth is not becoming decisive-and-flat; it is learning to **state confidence in graduated terms** — "70% of this is solid, here is the part I can't establish" — so the class can move without pretending certainty it doesn't have. That is literally her lead-chapter thesis (ch 19) becoming her personal arc.

**Sprites:** already approved/good. Keep her prop lane unique to her — a subtitle/caption motif (a caption bar, a second-language track) rather than a generic camera; the camera is travel/photography's lane elsewhere in the cast.

## 3. The Japanese her story teaches — LEARNING INTEGRATION (most important)

**Learning speciality:** *subtitles, inference, and preserved ambiguity* — evidentiality (observation vs. hearsay vs. inference), hedged/graduated confidence, quotation and translation choice, and change-of-state noticing/repair. She is the cast's home for **"say exactly as much as the evidence supports, and no more"** — the calibration voice's cinematic sibling (Sophie calibrates confidence in prose; Ruparna calibrates it in a subtitle).

Following the canonical **Need → Input → Lesson → Repair → Transfer → Consequence → Return** beat pattern (`CONTENT-LINKAGE.md`). Reservoir bands below are authoring reservoirs, not completion gates.

### Ch 19 lead — reservoir `l2-l12`–`l2-l15` (band 19: *coordinate simultaneous actions, layered reasons, environmental evidence, and a failed day*)
- **Need the scene manufactures:** the subtitle failed; the learner must understand a clip they only partly follow, and must *report their partial understanding accurately* instead of faking 100%.
- **Language functions the learner NEEDS:**
  - **`～ながら`** (simultaneous actions) — narrate what happens on screen while it happens: 「歩きながら話している」.
  - **`～し`** (layered/stacked reasons) — justify a *reading*: 「音も長いし、字幕もないし、たぶん…」.
  - **Environmental evidence / appearance & hearsay — `～そう / ～ようだ / ～らしい / ～みたい`** — the core inference toolkit: distinguish "it looks like," "it seems," "apparently." This is the beating heart of her speciality.
  - **Hedged confidence:** `たぶん`, `～かもしれない`, `～と思う` — the grammar of "70%."
- **Activity types hosted:** **listening** (partial-comprehension clip, gist-then-gap on the rewatch), **grammar** (evidentials + hedges), **speaking** (report what you inferred and flag what you couldn't). A **transfer** task: the subtitle disappears and the learner must caption one line at the confidence level they can actually defend.
- **Why the real texture makes it necessary:** "watch twice with subtitles on" is literally the two-pass activity design — pass 1 gist, pass 2 gap. The learner can't fake full comprehension because the scene *is about* the gap. Inference grammar is the only way out.
- **Signature learner line (canon):** `字幕にすると、ちょっと言い切りすぎじゃない？` — a live prompt to downgrade an over-certain reading from 断定 to `～らしい / ～みたい`.

### Ch 24 contribution — reservoir `l2-l31`–`l2-l34` (band 24 exhibition finale: *conditional plan, health goal, conversational continuation, menu reading*)
- **Need:** the live event needs captions written *now*; say enough to be vivid, not more than was observed.
- **Functions:** **`～てしまう`** (completion / change-of-state — her `字も消えてしまいました` habit: name what disappeared), quotation `～と言っていた` for reporting the clip, and continuation `～ておく / ～ていく` to stage a caption before the event.
- **Activity types:** **writing** (compose a live caption line), **transfer** (caption a changed clip under time pressure), light **speaking** (read the caption aloud for timing — hands to Xingyu/Mika for the count).
- **Real texture → necessity:** `「この字幕、いいね」` becomes the rubric — the learner writes a caption a Ruparna would praise: accurate, warm, ambiguity intact.

### Ch 31 lead — reservoir band **31–33: N2 quotation, translation choice, evidentiality, hedging** (`CONTENT-LINKAGE.md`; four-skill proof: *annotate an audio/text source; justify a subtitle choice; distinguish observation/hearsay/inference; write calibrated claims*)
- **Need:** a "cleaner" subtitle erased uncertainty; the learner must *restore* the warranted ambiguity and justify the choice.
- **Functions:**
  - **Quotation & reported speech:** `～と言った / ～という / ～とのこと` vs. direct quote — the difference between quoting and paraphrasing.
  - **Modality / translation choice:** `～と言える / ～とされる / ～とは限らない / 言い切る` — the grammar of *how much a translation is allowed to claim.*
  - **Evidentiality trichotomy:** observation (`～のが見えた`) vs. hearsay (`～そうだ / ～らしい`) vs. inference (`～はずだ / ～に違いない / ～ようだ`).
- **Activity types:** **reading** (annotate the source transcript), **writing** (a calibrated subtitle + a one-line justification), **transfer** (re-subtitle to preserve rather than resolve). This is the exact `annotate → justify → distinguish → write calibrated claims` proof.
- **Real texture → necessity:** the cinephile who catches "the grammar hiding inside a line of dialogue" is the only lens that makes N2 modality feel *earned* rather than drilled — the learner is repairing an actual dishonest subtitle.

### Ch 44 return — reservoir band **43–45: N1 editing rationale, mastery-aware production** (four-skill proof includes *revise for public use; perform an extended spontaneous response*)
- **Need:** produce a vivid public version *without* restoring withdrawn detail — constraint-driven editing.
- **Functions:** nominalized editing/adaptation vocabulary, concessive `～ものの / ～とはいえ`, scope-limiting `～範囲で / ～かぎり`, and artistic-rationale explanation (justify a cut in Japanese).
- **Activity types:** **writing** (the constrained final caption), **speaking** (defend the editing rationale aloud), **transfer** (adapt under a hard constraint). Shared scene — hand visual rationale to Stasi, sound to Xingyu.

### Ch 46 future — reservoir band **46–48: integrated N1 public inquiry & mediation** (four-skill proof: *listen and repair live; explain sources; mediate a disagreement*)
- **Need:** a live misunderstanding on stage must be repaired transparently, in real time.
- **Functions:** live **listening + repair** (`すみません、今のは～という意味ですか`), source explanation (`これは聞いた話で、確認はできていません`), and the public three-way split: heard / inferred / cannot establish (`聞こえた / 推測した / まだ確かめられない`).
- **Activity types:** integrated **listening → speaking** under uncertainty; **transfer/mediation** with no source surface left. The `callback:seventy-percent` payoff line lives here.

**Consolidated learning functions Ruparna's story teaches:** evidentiality (`そうだ/ようだ/らしい/みたい`), hedged confidence (`たぶん / ～かもしれない / ～と思う`), `～ながら`, `～し`, `～てしまう` (change-of-state), reported speech & quotation (`～と言っていた / ～という`), N2 modality & translation choice (`～と言える / ～とされる / ～とは限らない / 言い切る`), evidential distinction (observation vs. hearsay vs. inference), N1 editing/adaptation rationale (`～ものの / ～範囲で`), and live listening-and-repair / mediation.

## 4. Elective bond route — **PROPOSAL (bond-candidate, not yet authorized)**

> Canon guard (`RELATIONSHIP-MATRIX.md`): every person is `continuity-only` by default; an elective appointment route requires a **separate reviewed manifest entry with state `bond-authored`.** This section is a *sketch for that manifest*, not a claim that the route is live. Three required steps (Recognition → Friction → Support) are folded across the six appointments below. Route theme: **subtitle club / "watch it twice."**

1. **Recognition — "The second watch."** Ordinary task: watch a short clip together and each write one caption line. Ruparna quietly writes two versions and keeps both. *Function:* `～ながら` + gist reporting. *Beat:* she recognizes the learner noticed the gap, not just the gist.
2. **A different route — "Nice subtitle."** She shows a subtitle she loves *because* it preserved an ambiguity; asks the learner to find one that flattened it. *Function:* evidentials `そう/よう/らしい`; `「この字幕、いいね」` as praise register. Different method: appreciation before critique.
3. **Reciprocity — "What changed in the room."** The learner brings *her* a changed-detail puzzle (two near-identical scene photos / two subtitle cards). She reports what disappeared. *Function:* `～てしまう` change-of-state; `ext-ruparna-subtitle-change` (N4: *compare two original subtitle cards and report what disappeared*). She receives, not only gives.
4. **Friction — "Too definitive."** A clip where the learner's subtitle over-claims. She pushes back: `言い切りすぎじゃない？` The learner must defend or downgrade. *Function:* modality `～とは限らない / 言い切る`; graduated confidence. Genuine disagreement, warmly held.
5. **Repair — "70% is a door."** After the friction, they re-cut the caption together to the confidence it can actually bear. *Function:* hedging + quotation split (heard vs. inferred). The `seventy-percent` ethic resolves the friction.
6. **Future — "Whose cut?"** They subtitle something that isn't theirs and decide together what to leave out of frame. *Function:* N2/N1 translation-choice + scope-limiting `～範囲で`. Open-ended: the bond's future is a shared practice, not a destination.
